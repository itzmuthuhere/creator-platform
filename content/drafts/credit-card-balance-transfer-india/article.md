# Credit card balance transfer in India: when it saves money, and the catch

A credit card balance transfer moves what you owe on one card to another card that charges less, sometimes nothing for a couple of months. When you're paying a card's full revolving rate, the saving can be large. But Indian balance transfers come with conditions that can quietly cancel the saving, especially one that affects everything else you buy on the new card.

This guide explains the two kinds of balance transfer Indian issuers offer, what they cost compared with staying put, the catch in the fine print, and how to decide.

## Why it's worth looking at

Card debt you don't clear is charged at the revolving rate. SBI Card's balance transfer terms put its finance charge at up to **3.75% a month (45% a year)** on unsecured cards, plus GST. A balance transfer swaps that for a much lower rate for a fixed period, so more of each payment goes to the debt itself.

It only helps if you **stop adding to the debt** and **clear the transferred amount within the offer period**. Money9's [explainer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=86Dbx4KITjM) frames it the same way: the point is paying the debt off faster.

## The two kinds of balance transfer

SBI Card's offers show the two types clearly:

| | Balance transfer (one-time) | Balance transfer on EMI |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | **0% for 60 days**, or 1.7% a month (20.4% a year) for 180 days | **10%–17.5% a year over 3 months; 12.5%–20% over 6 months** |
| Fee | Processing fee applies | 0.75%–1% of the amount |
| Repayment | Pay off within the offer period | Fixed EMIs on your statement |
| **New purchases on the card** | **Lose the interest-free period** | Keep the interest-free period |

*From SBI Card's [balance transfer](https://www.sbicard.com/en/personal/benefits/lower-interest-option/balance-transfer.page) and [BT on EMI](https://www.sbicard.com/en/personal/benefits/lower-interest-option/balance-transfer-on-emi.page) pages, October 2026. Rates depend on your credit score.*

Both are paid by NEFT straight to your other card, usually within three to four working days. The amount is limited to between ₹5,000 and **75% of your available limit**.

## The catch: your interest-free period

This is the condition most people miss. SBI Card's [balance transfer terms](https://www.sbicard.com/sbi-card-en/assets/docs/pdf/tnc/balance-transfer-tnc.pdf) say that while you have a one-time balance transfer on the card, **every new purchase is charged interest from the date of purchase** at the full finance rate. The usual interest-free period of up to 50 days doesn't apply.

So if you move ₹1 lakh at 0% and keep using that card for ₹30,000 of shopping a month, those purchases start costing interest straight away: roughly ₹1,100 a month at 45% a year plus GST, over an average 25 days. That eats much of the saving.

**The fix:** don't use the receiving card for purchases while a one-time transfer is outstanding, or choose **BT on EMI**, which SBI Card says keeps the interest-free period on purchases.

BankBazaar's [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AYoQLuktxQM) flags two related points: the low rate applies only for a limited period, and the transferred amount uses up part of your new card's limit.

## The numbers: ₹1 lakh at the revolving rate

Suppose you owe ₹1 lakh at 3.75% a month plus GST and can pay about ₹17,000 a month.

| Option | Cost of interest, fees and GST | Time to clear |
|---|---|---|
| Stay on the old card | about ₹18,400 | 7 months |
| BT on EMI, 6 months at 12.5% | about ₹5,500 | 6 months |
| BT on EMI, 6 months at 20% | about ₹8,200 | 6 months |
| 0% for 60 days (2% fee, the top of the typical range) | about ₹2,400 | 2 months, at ₹50,000 a month |

*Our calculation. BT on EMI includes a 1% fee and GST at 18% on interest and fee. The 0% option needs ₹50,000 a month to clear in time; whatever is left after 60 days moves to the rate in your terms.*

Even the most expensive BT on EMI cuts the cost by more than half. The 0% offer is cheapest **only if** you can clear it inside 60 days and don't use the card for anything else meanwhile.

## How to decide

1. **Can you clear it within 60 days?** Then a 0% one-time transfer is cheapest, but don't spend on that card until it's paid.
2. **Need three to six months?** Use BT on EMI, compare the quoted rate and fee, and keep using the card normally.
3. **Need longer than six months?** A balance transfer won't cover it. Compare a card EMI with a personal loan; the larger the amount, the more a personal loan tends to win.

4. **Check your score first.** SBI Card prices BT on EMI by bureau score; our guide to [credit scores](https://techpulzo.in/credit-score-in-india-how-it-works-and-how-to-fix-it) explains what lenders see. A low score means a higher rate, sometimes close to what you're paying now.
5. **Stop the old card's spending.** Moving the debt while still running up the old card just doubles it.

Card Buddhi's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7eJxomiP3v4) walks through the SBI Card booking screens for both types, if you want to see the flow before you start.

## Other costs to check

- **Processing fee.** [Axis Bank's guide to card charges](https://www.axis.bank.in/blogs/credit-card/credit-card-charges-and-fees) puts typical balance transfer fees at 1%–2%, and some issuers set a minimum. HDFC Bank's [card terms](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/content/dam/hdfcbankpws/in/en/personal-banking/discover-products/cards/credit-cards/personal-mitc/mitc-in-english.pdf) list balance transfer processing at 1% of the amount or ₹250, whichever is higher.
- **Foreclosure.** SBI Card charges 3% of the outstanding principal to close a BT on EMI early.
- **No cancellation.** SBI Card's terms allow cancelling a one-time transfer only on the day you book it.
- **Credit utilisation.** The transferred amount takes up the new card's limit, which raises your utilisation until it's repaid.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a credit card balance transfer?

It's shifting your outstanding dues from one bank's card to a card from another bank, at a lower interest rate or 0% for a limited period. The new card pays the old one directly, and you repay the new card.

### Is a credit card balance transfer worth it?

Yes, if you're paying the revolving rate (up to 3.75% a month), you can clear the transferred amount within the offer period, and you stop adding new debt. A six-month BT on EMI can cut the cost of a ₹1 lakh balance by more than half.

### Do I lose the interest-free period after a balance transfer?

With a one-time balance transfer, often yes. SBI Card's terms say new purchases are charged interest from the purchase date while the transfer is outstanding. Its BT on EMI keeps the interest-free period, so check which type you're booking.

### How much can I transfer?

It depends on the issuer and your available limit. SBI Card allows from ₹5,000 up to 75% of your available credit limit on the receiving card.

### Does a balance transfer affect my credit score?

Indirectly. It uses up part of the new card's limit, raising utilisation until you repay, and missing a payment on the new card hurts your score like any other missed payment. Paying it off on time helps.

## The short version

A balance transfer is a cheap bridge out of 45% card interest, not a solution in itself. Use a 0% transfer only if you can clear it in 60 days and leave that card unused; otherwise choose BT on EMI, which keeps your interest-free period. Compare total cost including fees and GST, and stop using the old card.
