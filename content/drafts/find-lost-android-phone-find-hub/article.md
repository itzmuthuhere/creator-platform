# Lost your Android phone? Find My Device (now Find Hub) and what to do next

Google's Find My Device, renamed **Find Hub** in May 2025, can show a lost Android phone on a map, ring it, lock it with a message, or erase it, from any browser. It works only if a few settings were on before the phone went missing, and it can't stop a thief from switching the phone off. In India there's a second tool for that: blocking the handset's IMEI through the government's Sanchar Saathi portal, which makes the phone useless on every network.

This guide covers both: what to switch on today, and exactly what to do, in order, if your phone is lost or stolen.

## Set it up now

According to [Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/6160491?hl=en), Find Hub can only reach a phone that is signed in to a Google account, has Find Hub turned on, has power and a mobile data or Wi-Fi connection, and is visible on Google Play. Check these now:

1. **Screen lock.** Set a PIN, pattern or password. Without one, anyone who picks up the phone can open it.
2. **Find Hub on.** Settings > Google > All services > **Find Hub** (the path varies by brand), and make sure the device is allowed to be located.
3. **Location on**, with location accuracy improved, so the map is as precise as possible.
4. **Offline finding.** In Find Hub's settings, the option to find offline devices lets other Android phones nearby report your phone's location through Bluetooth when it has no internet. Mufungo Geeks Tech's [settings video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WVW8psrzBeI) recommends the "everywhere" setting for the best chance of a location.
5. **Theft protection.** Newer Android versions have **Theft Detection Lock**, **Offline Device Lock** and **Remote Lock** (Settings > Google > Theft protection or Security). Remote Lock lets you lock the phone from android.com/lock using only your phone number.
6. **Know your Google password.** You'll need it on another device, and after an erase.
7. **Note your IMEI numbers.** Dial `*#06#` and save the result somewhere other than the phone. You'll need them for a police complaint and for blocking.

[9to5Google reported](https://9to5google.com/2025/05/13/googles-find-my-device-network-is-now-find-hub/) that Find Hub works on Android 6 and later.

## Lost it? The first hour, in order

1. **Locate it.** On another phone, tablet or computer, go to **android.com/find**, or open the Find Hub app on another Android device (use guest mode on a friend's phone). Sign in with your Google account.
2. **Ring it.** If it's nearby, **Play sound** rings the phone at full volume for five minutes, even on silent.
3. **Lock it.** If it's not nearby, use **Mark as lost**. It locks the phone and shows a message and a contact number on the lock screen. Smartphones Made Easy's [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U_Yq_8ionKI) advises using a family member's number and nothing more sensitive.
4. **Protect your money.** Call your bank to block UPI on that number, and change your Google password. Our guide to [how UPI fraud happens](https://techpulzo.in/how-upi-fraud-actually-happens-the-five-warning-signs) explains why speed matters.
5. **Get your SIM blocked and reissued.** Ask your operator to block the SIM, then get a duplicate SIM for the same number. You need it to receive OTPs, including for the CEIR request below.
6. **File a police complaint** for the lost or stolen phone and keep the complaint number and copy.
7. **Block the phone through CEIR** (below).
8. **Decide on erasing.** See the next section.

## What Find Hub shows, and when to erase

- **Location.** The map shows the current location if the phone is online, or the last known location if it isn't. Android Help says accuracy ranges from about 20 metres with GPS to several kilometres when only mobile towers are available, so treat it as a guide.
- **Erase (factory reset)** permanently deletes everything on the phone, possibly except an SD card. After that, **Find Hub can no longer locate it**, and the phone needs your Google account password to be set up again.

Erase when you're sure the phone won't come back and you're worried about the data on it. If there's a chance of recovery, lock it first and keep tracking.

## Secure your accounts

Your phone holds sign-ins to email, banking and social apps. Once it's locked:

- Change your Google password and review **signed-in devices** in your Google account; sign the lost phone out.
- Check other important accounts (email, social, shopping) for unfamiliar activity.
- If your phone was your second factor for logins, use backup codes or another method. Our guide to [two-factor authentication](https://techpulzo.in/how-two-factor-authentication-actually-stops-account-hacks) covers setting up backups.

## Block it with CEIR (India)

The Department of Telecommunications runs **Sanchar Saathi**, which includes a service to **block your lost or stolen mobile handset** through the Central Equipment Identity Register (CEIR). A blocked phone can't be used on any Indian network, even with a new SIM. As of October 2026, the [portal](https://www.sancharsaathi.gov.in/) reports 60.88 lakh phones blocked and 37.81 lakh traced.

The [CEIR blocking form](https://www.ceir.gov.in/Request/CeirUserBlockRequestDirect.jsp) lists what to keep ready:

- **Police report or FIR copy** for the lost or stolen phone.
- **A reissued (duplicate) SIM** for the lost number, with SMS working, because the OTP is sent to it.
- **Valid ID proof** (Aadhaar, PAN, voter ID, driving licence or other) to upload.
- **IMEI numbers**, if you have them, and the **purchase invoice** (optional).

You'll also enter the phone's brand and model, where and when it was lost, the state, district, police station and complaint number. After submitting, keep the **request ID** to check the status on Sanchar Saathi.

## If you find it

- In Find Hub, choose to mark the phone as found, then unlock it with your usual PIN or pattern.
- If you blocked it through CEIR, use Sanchar Saathi's **unblock found mobile handset** option so it works on networks again.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I find my Android phone if it's switched off?

Not its current location. Find Hub can show the last known location from when the phone was last online, and if offline finding was enabled, nearby Android devices may have reported its location. Once it's off, your best protection is locking it and blocking it through CEIR.

### How do I find my lost Android phone from another phone?

Open android.com/find in any browser, or the Find Hub app on another Android phone (use guest mode on a friend's device), and sign in with the Google account used on the lost phone. You'll see its location and can ring, lock or erase it.

### What do I need to block a lost phone on CEIR?

A police complaint or FIR copy, a reissued SIM for your lost number (to receive the OTP), valid ID proof, and ideally the IMEI numbers and purchase invoice. Submit the request on the Sanchar Saathi portal and keep the request ID.

### Should I erase my phone remotely?

Only if you don't expect to get it back and you're worried about your data. Erasing deletes everything, and Find Hub can't locate the phone afterwards. Lock it first and keep tracking while there's a chance of recovery.

### Does Find Hub work without a SIM card?

Yes, if the phone has a Wi-Fi connection, or if offline finding is on and other Android devices are nearby. It needs the phone to be signed in to your Google account with Find Hub enabled.

## Do these three things today

Turn on Find Hub with offline finding, set a screen lock, and save your IMEI numbers somewhere other than the phone. If the worst happens, those three steps decide whether you can find it, protect your data and block it.
