# Google Security Checkup: what each warning means and what to do

Google Security Checkup is a free review of your Google account's security that lists problems and suggestions in one place: weak or leaked passwords, devices you've forgotten, apps with access to your data, missing recovery details. The warnings are useful, but they don't all carry the same weight, and some need action today while others are tips you can take or leave.

This guide shows how to open it, how to read the colours, what each warning means, and what to do if you find something that isn't you.

## How to open it and read the colours

Go to **myaccount.google.com/security-checkup**, or on Android open **Settings > Google > Manage your Google Account > Security**. CoolTechZone's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nt2S_hU24VE) shows you can also get there from the account icon in Chrome, Gmail or YouTube.

[Google's help page](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/46526?hl=en) explains the colour coding:

| Colour | Meaning | When to act |
|---|---|---|
| Red | Urgent security issue | Now |
| Yellow | Important step to take | This week |
| Blue | Tip or suggestion | When convenient |
| Green shield | No issues found | Nothing needed |

## Warning by warning

| What Checkup says | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| **Compromised passwords** | A password you've saved appeared in a data breach outside Google | Change it on that site now, and anywhere else you used it |
| **Reused passwords** | The same password is used on several sites | Change the important ones (email, banking, shopping) first |
| **Weak passwords** | Easy to guess | Replace with a long, unique password from a password manager |
| **Recovery phone or email missing or outdated** | Google can't reach you to verify or alert you | Add a current phone and a second email |
| **Turn on 2-Step Verification** | Your password alone protects the account | Turn it on; a passkey or security key is strongest |
| **Device signed in that you don't use** | An old or unknown phone or computer still has access | Sign it out |
| **Device without screen lock** | Anyone holding it could open your account | Set a PIN or pattern on that device |
| **Third-party access** | An app or website can read some of your Google data | Remove anything you don't recognise or no longer use |
| **Recent security activity** | A list of sign-ins, password changes and other events | Check each one is you |
| **Turn on Enhanced Safe Browsing** | Stronger protection against phishing and malware in Chrome | Optional; see below |

A few of these deserve more detail.

### Passwords

The Password Checkup covers passwords saved in Google Password Manager. Tutorial Made Easy's [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MGyjjtC4LLs) explains the three categories: **compromised** (found in a breach), **reused** and **weak**. Treat compromised as red even if the Checkup shows yellow, because a leaked password can be tried on other sites automatically. Our explainer on [why reused passwords are dangerous](https://techpulzo.in/why-you-should-not-reuse-passwords-what-happens-when-one-site-gets-breached) covers why, and a [password manager](https://techpulzo.in/how-to-set-up-a-password-manager-in-15-minutes) makes unique passwords painless.

### Sign-in and recovery

Your recovery phone and email let Google send alerts and get you back in if you're locked out. Make sure the phone number is one you'll keep, not a work number. For 2-Step Verification, Google recommends **security keys** as the strongest option, and passkeys are equally phishing-resistant. Our guide to [two-factor authentication](https://techpulzo.in/how-two-factor-authentication-actually-stops-account-hacks) explains the options.

### Third-party access

This lists apps and sites you've allowed to use your Google account: "Sign in with Google" logins and apps that can read Gmail, Drive or Contacts. Google suggests removing anything you don't need, especially apps using older, less secure sign-in methods. Removing access doesn't delete your account with that service; you may just need to sign in again.

## If you see something that isn't you

If **Recent security activity** or **Your devices** shows a sign-in, device or change you don't recognise:

1. **Change your Google password** immediately, to one you haven't used anywhere else.
2. **Sign out the unknown device** from Your devices.
3. **Check your recovery phone and email** haven't been changed, and fix them if they have.
4. **Check Gmail's forwarding and filters** (Settings > See all settings > Forwarding, and Filters) for rules you didn't create; attackers use them to copy or hide email.
5. **Remove unfamiliar third-party access.**
6. **Turn on 2-Step Verification** if it wasn't on.
7. Change the passwords of any accounts whose reset emails go to this Gmail, starting with banking.

A sign-in from your own new phone, or from a city your mobile operator routes through, can look odd. If the device and time match something you did, it's probably you.

## Enhanced Safe Browsing: worth it?

Safe Browsing warns you about dangerous websites and downloads. **Enhanced** protection goes further: [BleepingComputer explains](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/google/google-rolls-out-new-enhanced-safe-browsing-security-feature/) that instead of checking against a list updated periodically, it checks sites with Google in real time, and sends small samples of pages and suspicious downloads to Google to spot new threats. Google says in a [February 2025 post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/chrome/google-chrome-safe-browsing-one-billion-users/) that people using Enhanced Protection are twice as safe from phishing and scams as those on Standard Protection.

The trade-off is privacy: more of your browsing is checked by Google. For most people, especially anyone who's been phished before or who shares a computer with less careful users, the protection is worth it. You can turn it on in the Checkup or in Chrome's privacy and security settings.

## How often to run it

- **Every three months**, or after you change phones.
- **Immediately** if Google emails you a security alert, or you get a code you didn't request.
- After you stop using any shared or office computer you signed in to.

Most runs take five minutes once you've fixed the first round of warnings.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is Google Security Checkup?

It's a free page in your Google account (myaccount.google.com/security-checkup) that reviews your security in one place: saved passwords, signed-in devices, recent security activity, recovery options, two-step verification and apps with access to your account. It flags issues by colour, from red for urgent to blue for tips.

### What does "compromised password" mean in Google?

One of your saved passwords has been found in a data breach on another website. Change it on that site immediately, and on any other site where you used the same password.

### Should I remove third-party access from my Google account?

Remove any app or website you don't recognise or no longer use. Removing access doesn't delete your account with that service; it just stops it reading your Google data, and you can sign in again later if needed.

### What should I do if I see a sign-in I don't recognise?

Change your Google password, sign out the unknown device, check that your recovery phone and email haven't been changed, look for unfamiliar Gmail forwarding rules or filters, and turn on 2-Step Verification.

### Is Enhanced Safe Browsing worth turning on?

For most people, yes. It checks websites and downloads with Google in real time and, according to Google, makes users twice as safe from phishing as Standard protection. The trade-off is that more browsing data is sent to Google for checking.

## The five-minute routine

Open the Checkup, fix anything red, change compromised passwords, sign out old devices and remove apps you don't use. Then come back in three months.
