# Rooftop solar subsidy under PM Surya Ghar: cost, size and payback in 2026

The rooftop solar subsidy is generous: up to ₹78,000 from the central government under PM Surya Ghar, and in Tamil Nadu up to ₹22,000 more from the state since September 2026. But the subsidy isn't what decides whether solar pays off for you. Your electricity use and your tariff slabs do. For a household with a large bill, a small system can pay for itself in about four years. For a household already in its state's free or cheap slabs, the same system may take well over a decade.

This guide covers the current subsidy, how much a system produces, and how to size one, with worked examples using Tamil Nadu's 2026 tariff.

## The subsidy in 2026

**Central subsidy (all states).** The [PM Surya Ghar portal](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) lists the amounts for residential rooftop systems:

| System size | Central subsidy |
|---|---|
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 |
| 3 kW or more | ₹78,000 (the cap) |

Housing societies and resident welfare associations get ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities, and special-category states get an extra 10%. The portal also says the scheme **closes on 31 March 2027**.

**Tamil Nadu top-up.** The state's scheme, launched on 21 September 2026, adds to the central amount. [SolarBytes reports](https://solarbytes.info/india-bytes/tnpdcl-state-solar-rooftop-installation-inr-50-crore-central-state-government-energy-resources-minister-nirmal-kumar-12570315) the combined figures:

| System size | Central | Tamil Nadu | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 kW | ₹30,000 | ₹5,000 | ₹35,000 |
| 2 kW | ₹60,000 | ₹10,000 | ₹70,000 |
| 3 kW | ₹78,000 | ₹22,000 | ₹1,00,000 |

The state has set aside ₹50 crore for this year and aims to cover one lakh homes by March 2027. Consumers who applied for rooftop solar from 5 August 2026 onwards are eligible. Other states have their own top-ups or none; check your electricity board's website.

The subsidy applies to systems built with **DCR panels** (domestic content requirement: Indian-made cells and modules) from vendors registered on the portal. Deepak Chaudhary of Nexus Solar explains in [his video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R6SRu8ga_RI) that you'll usually see three quotes: a DCR system that qualifies for the subsidy, a non-DCR system that's cheaper but gets no subsidy, and a hybrid system with batteries, often 30–40% dearer. For most homes on a reliable grid, the DCR on-grid system is the one the subsidy is designed for.

## How much a system produces

A rule of thumb: **1 kW of panels produces about 1,400 units a year** in most of India, or roughly 4 units a day averaged over the year. That's SolarSquare's assumption in its [3 kW payback video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DRu6RIrtgU0), and Tamil Yojana's [video on the state scheme](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Pf1S72U-7o8) gives 4–5 units a day; both are in the same range. Output varies with your roof's direction, shading, dust and the season. Panels also lose a little output each year; SolarSquare assumes about 2% in the first year and 0.6% a year after that.

So, per two-month billing cycle:

| System | Units a year | Units per 60-day cycle |
|---|---|---|
| 1 kW | about 1,400 | about 230 |
| 2 kW | about 2,800 | about 460 |
| 3 kW | about 4,200 | about 690 |

With an on-grid system and a net meter, your bill is calculated on **net** consumption: what you drew from the grid minus what your panels exported, as [Deccan Chronicle explains](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/southern-states/tamil-nadu/tns-subsidy-scheme-to-bring-down-rooftop-solar-panels-cost-for-domestic-consumers-1979505) for Tamil Nadu.

## The sizing question: bring your bill to zero, not your usage

The portal's own guide suggests 1–2 kW for homes using up to 150 units a month, 2–3 kW for 150–300, and more above that. That's a good start, but slab tariffs change the answer.

In Tamil Nadu since May 2026, a household using up to 500 units in a two-month cycle gets the first 200 units free. Units beyond that cost ₹4.70, then ₹6.30; cross 500 and the free allowance drops to 100 units and the rates rise. Solar is worth most when it removes the units you pay the highest rates for. Once your net use is under 200 units, extra panels save nothing, unless your surplus exports are credited, which this guide doesn't assume.

## Worked examples: two Tamil Nadu households

Assumptions: generation as above; installed cost about ₹80,000 for 1 kW and ₹1.9 lakh for 3 kW, mid-points of the ranges Tamil Yojana quotes, and ₹1.35 lakh for 2 kW (our estimate between those); surplus exports earn nothing.

**Household A uses 300 units per cycle.** Its bill is ₹470 (100 units at ₹4.70).

| System | Net units | New bill | Saved a year | Cost after both subsidies | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 kW | 70 | ₹0 | ₹2,820 | ₹45,000 | about 16 years |
| 2 kW | 0 | ₹0 | ₹2,820 | ₹65,000 | about 23 years |
| 3 kW | 0 | ₹0 | ₹2,820 | ₹90,000 | about 32 years |

**Household B uses 558 units per cycle**, with an AC and a water heater. Its bill is ₹2,527.20.

| System | Net units | New bill | Saved a year | Cost after both subsidies | Payback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 kW | 328 | ₹601.60 | ₹11,554 | ₹45,000 | about 3.9 years |
| 2 kW | 98 | ₹0 | ₹15,163 | ₹65,000 | about 4.3 years |
| 3 kW | 0 | ₹0 | ₹15,163 | ₹90,000 | about 5.9 years |

*Our calculation. Tariff: Tamil Nadu domestic consumer rates from May 2026.*

What this shows:

- **Household A gains little.** Most of its use is already free, so even the smallest system takes 16 years to repay, and a 3 kW system never really does. The ₹1 lakh subsidy doesn't change that.
- **Household B is the case solar is made for.** A 2 kW system takes its bill to zero and pays back in about four years. A 3 kW system costs ₹25,000 more for no extra saving, unless the surplus is credited or the family's use grows.
- **The biggest subsidy isn't always the best deal.** The full ₹1 lakh comes with 3 kW, but that's the right size only if you use enough electricity to absorb it.

If you live in a state without free units, every unit solar replaces saves money, so the payback for a small household is better than Household A's, and a larger system is easier to justify.

## What to check in a quote

- **DCR or non-DCR?** Only DCR systems qualify for the subsidy. Compare the DCR price minus subsidy with the non-DCR price.
- **Panel and inverter details:** brand, panel wattage, number of panels, and warranties (panels typically carry long performance warranties; check the inverter's separately).
- **What's included:** mounting structure, wiring, earthing, the net meter and its application, and any maintenance visits.
- **Your roof:** a shadow-free area facing south is best; ask the vendor how much area the system needs and whether nearby buildings or tanks shade it.
- **Fixed charges after solar:** your bill may not fall to exactly zero if your board levies fixed or meter charges.
- **Financing:** if you're borrowing, compare the loan's full cost, not only the EMI; our guide to [reading a loan's APR](https://techpulzo.in/how-to-read-a-loan-apr-instead-of-just-the-emi) explains how.

## How to apply

1. Register on [pmsuryaghar.gov.in](https://pmsuryaghar.gov.in/) with your mobile number, then choose your state, electricity board and consumer number.
2. Submit the application and wait for the board's feasibility approval.
3. Choose a registered vendor and have the system installed.
4. Apply for the net meter; the board inspects and commissions the system.
5. Submit bank details for the subsidy, which is paid to your account after commissioning.

In Tamil Nadu, the state top-up is handled through TNPDCL's rooftop solar portal alongside the national one.

## Should you wait for "PM Surya Ghar 2.0"?

Nexus Solar's video reports that MNRE discussed proposals for a next phase with the industry in July 2026, but nothing had been notified when it was published. Until a new order is issued, the current rules apply, and the current scheme is due to close on 31 March 2027. If your numbers work today, waiting for an unannounced change means paying the higher bill in the meantime.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much is the rooftop solar subsidy in 2026?

The central subsidy is ₹30,000 per kW for the first 2 kW and ₹18,000 for the third, up to ₹78,000. In Tamil Nadu, the state adds ₹5,000, ₹10,000 or ₹22,000 for 1, 2 or 3 kW, for a total of up to ₹1 lakh.

### How many units does a 3 kW solar system produce?

About 4,200 units a year, or roughly 690 units every two months, in most of India. Actual output depends on your roof direction, shading, dust and season, and falls slightly as panels age.

### Will my electricity bill become zero with solar?

It can if your system produces as much as your net use, but fixed or meter charges may remain. In Tamil Nadu, households using under 500 units every two months already get 200 units free, so they only need enough solar to cover the units above that.

### Is rooftop solar worth it for a small household?

Often not, where low usage is already free or cheap. In our Tamil Nadu example, a 300-unit household would take about 16 years to recover the cost of even a 1 kW system after subsidies. Households with large bills recover the cost far faster.

### Do I need DCR panels to get the subsidy?

Yes. The PM Surya Ghar subsidy applies to systems using domestically manufactured (DCR) panels installed by a registered vendor. Non-DCR systems can still be installed and net-metered, but without the subsidy.

## The decision in one line

Work out how many units you pay for above your free or cheapest slab, size the system to cover those, and check the payback after subsidy. If it's under six or seven years, solar is a good investment; if it's over ten, your money probably works harder elsewhere.
