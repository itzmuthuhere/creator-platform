# DICGC deposit insurance: is your money safe if your bank fails?

If an Indian bank fails, your deposits there are insured up to ₹5 lakh by the Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation (DICGC), an RBI subsidiary. You don't apply for it or pay for it; it's automatic. But the ₹5 lakh is counted in ways that surprise people: per bank rather than per account, including interest, after any loan you owe the bank is deducted, and differently for joint accounts depending on the order of the names.

This guide explains exactly what DICGC deposit insurance covers, how the limit is counted, what happens and how fast when a bank fails, and how to keep larger savings covered.

## What's covered

According to the [DICGC's own guide](https://www.dicgc.org.in/guide-to-deposit-insurance):

- **Banks covered:** all commercial banks, including foreign banks' Indian branches, local area banks and regional rural banks (small finance banks are commercial banks too), and all co-operative banks. Membership is compulsory; a bank can't opt out.
- **Deposits covered:** savings, fixed, current and recurring deposits.
- **Not covered:** deposits of governments, inter-bank deposits, and deposits received outside India. Deposits with **NBFCs**, mutual funds, shares, bonds, ETFs and crypto are not bank deposits and aren't insured. Primary co-operative **societies** (as opposed to co-operative banks) aren't covered either.
- **Who pays:** the bank pays the premium, not you.

## How the ₹5 lakh is counted

**Per depositor, per bank, principal plus interest.** The limit is ₹5 lakh for everything you hold in a bank "in the same right and same capacity", across all branches and account types. The DICGC's example: ₹4,95,000 of principal plus ₹4,000 of interest is fully insured at ₹4,99,000, but if the principal alone is ₹5 lakh, the interest on top isn't covered.

**Different banks are separate.** Deposits at two banks are insured separately, even if both fail on the same day.

**Different capacities are separate.** Accounts you hold as a partner of a firm, guardian of a minor, director of a company, or trustee are each insured separately from your personal accounts.

**Joint accounts depend on the order of names.** Joint accounts with the same holders in the same order are added together. If the order or the set of names differs (A-B versus B-A, for instance), each counts as a different capacity with its own ₹5 lakh.

**Loans are deducted first.** Banks can set off what you owe them against your deposits, and insurance applies to what's left.

## A worked example

The Rao family has money at two banks.

| Account | Balance | Insured |
|---|---|---|
| **Bank X**, Ravi's savings | ₹1,50,000 | |
| **Bank X**, Ravi's FD (with interest) | ₹6,00,000 | |
| Ravi's total at Bank X, same capacity | ₹7,50,000 | **₹5,00,000** (₹2,50,000 exposed) |
| **Bank X**, joint FD, Sita first, Ravi second | ₹3,00,000 | **₹3,00,000** (separate capacity) |
| **Bank Y**, Ravi's FD | ₹4,00,000 | **₹4,00,000** (separate bank) |

Ravi's exposure is the ₹2.5 lakh over the limit at Bank X. Moving it to another bank, or into an account held in a different capacity, would cover it.

Learn With Mahesh's [explainer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVGDfA3w_9I) puts the general rule simply: opening more accounts at the same bank doesn't add cover; spreading money across banks does.

## What happens when a bank fails, and how fast

Most bank failures in India begin with the RBI placing the bank under **All-Inclusive Directions (AID)**, which restricts withdrawals.

Under Section 18A of the DICGC Act (in force since September 2021), the DICGC's guide sets this timeline:

1. Within **45 days** of the AID, the bank sends DICGC a list of every depositor's balance.
2. You submit a signed **willingness form** to the bank, with identity documents. There's no cut-off date, as long as the bank is still under AID.
3. DICGC verifies the claims within **30 days** and pays within **15 days** after that, with the whole process capped at **90 days** from the AID.

If a bank goes into liquidation instead, the DICGC pays the liquidator within two months of receiving the claim list, and the liquidator pays depositors.

**A recent case.** When the RBI imposed AID on New India Co-operative Bank on 13 February 2025, withdrawals were frozen. From 27 February depositors could take out up to ₹25,000, which covered the entire balance of more than half of them, [India TV reported](https://www.indiatvnews.com/business/news/new-india-co-operative-bank-depositors-can-withdraw-up-to-rs-25000-rbi-latest-updates-2025-02-24-977757). Deposits up to ₹5 lakh were then eligible for DICGC payment.

**Money above ₹5 lakh** isn't necessarily lost; it depends on what's recovered in the bank's resolution or liquidation, which can take years. Desi FIRE's [podcast clip](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2Dho-pRB2LU) points to the PMC Bank case, where insured amounts were paid first and the bank was later taken over by Unity Small Finance Bank, with the rest of the deposits handled under that scheme.

## How to keep larger savings covered

1. **Keep no more than about ₹5 lakh per bank**, including the interest your FDs will earn. A ₹4.8 lakh FD at 7% will cross ₹5 lakh within a year.
2. **Spread FDs across banks.** [IndusInd Bank's guide](https://www.indusind.bank.in/iblogs/fixed-deposit/fd-insurance-limit-explained-how-and-why-it-matters-for-investors/), updated in August 2026, recommends the same, and confirms the limit is still ₹5 lakh.
3. **Use family members' own accounts or different joint combinations** where it makes genuine sense, because each different capacity has its own cover.
4. **Be careful with higher-rate FDs.** Small finance banks and co-operative banks are covered by DICGC like any other bank, but a higher rate usually means higher risk, so the ₹5 lakh ceiling matters more there.
5. **Know what isn't a bank deposit.** NBFC and corporate FDs aren't DICGC-insured at all.

Money9's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wQw0qYX63Eo) covers the same ground in Hindi, including what to do if you hold more than ₹5 lakh.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much of my bank deposit is insured in India?

Up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, covering principal and interest together across all your savings, current, fixed and recurring deposits held in the same capacity. The DICGC pays this if the bank fails.

### Are FDs in small finance banks insured by DICGC?

Yes. Small finance banks are commercial banks and are covered up to ₹5 lakh per depositor, like any other bank. Deposits with NBFCs are not covered.

### Is a joint account insured separately?

Sometimes. Accounts held by the same people in the same order count as one. If the names are in a different order or the group of holders is different, each is insured separately up to ₹5 lakh.

### How long does DICGC take to pay?

For banks placed under All-Inclusive Directions, the law caps the process at 90 days from the date of the directions. For banks in liquidation, DICGC pays the liquidator within two months of getting the claim list.

### Do I need to apply for DICGC insurance?

No. Every bank in India is covered automatically and the bank pays the premium. If your bank fails, you'll need to submit a willingness form to the bank so your claim can be passed to DICGC.

## The short version

Your deposits are insured up to ₹5 lakh per bank, interest included, with loans you owe the bank deducted first. More accounts at the same bank don't add cover; more banks, or different joint-holder combinations, do. If a bank is frozen, the insured amount should reach you within 90 days, so keep each bank's balance, including FD interest, under ₹5 lakh.
