# ITR-U updated return: how to fix a missed or wrong return years later

You forgot to report interest from a fixed deposit. Or a capital gain slipped through. Or you never filed a return at all for a year when you should have. Once the 31 December deadline for belated and revised returns has passed, the only way to put it right yourself is an **ITR-U updated return**. It costs extra tax and it can't get you a refund, but it's far cheaper than waiting for the tax department to find the gap.

This guide covers what ITR-U can and can't do, which years are still open as of October 2026, what it costs, the steps on the e-filing portal, and what to do instead if you're owed a refund.

## What ITR-U is for

An updated return lets you **add income or correct a return** for an earlier year by paying the tax due, interest, and an extra charge. The [income tax department's FAQ](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/%20income%20tax%20returns-faq) sets out the key rules:

- You can file it **whether or not you filed a return** for that year.
- It **can't reduce your tax, increase a refund, or increase a loss**.
- **Only one** updated return per year.
- The extra tax is paid along with it.

For years up to 2025-26 these rules sit in Section 139(8A) of the old Income-tax Act. From 2026-27 they're in Section 263(6) of the Income-tax Act, 2025, which the FAQ says is "substantively the same".

**So ITR-U suits:** income you left out (interest, rent, capital gains, freelance work), deductions you claimed but shouldn't have, or a year you never filed when tax was due.

**It doesn't suit:** a refund you never claimed, or extra deductions you forgot. CA Guru Ji's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Ocb_VJdpWA) answers exactly that question, about a retired parent's excess TDS: an updated return can't be used to get it back. See the refund section below for the route that can.

## Which years are open (as of October 2026)

The window is **48 months from the end of the assessment year**, which is the year after the income year. It was 24 months until April 2025. The extra charge rises with each 12 months:

| Income year (FY) | Assessment year | Extra charge if filed now | Last date |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2020-21 | 2021-22 | Closed | 31 Mar 2026 |
| 2021-22 | 2022-23 | 70% | 31 Mar 2027 |
| 2022-23 | 2023-24 | 60% (70% from April 2027) | 31 Mar 2028 |
| 2023-24 | 2024-25 | 50% (60% from April 2027) | 31 Mar 2029 |
| 2024-25 | 2025-26 | 25% (50% from April 2027) | 31 Mar 2030 |
| 2025-26 | 2026-27 | Use a belated or revised return until 31 Dec 2026 | — |

The charge is a percentage of the **tax plus interest** you owe for that year, not of your income. Notice that every band steps up on **1 April 2027**, so filing before 31 March 2027 locks in the current rate for every open year. For FY 2025-26, the FAQ confirms a belated return is still possible until 31 December 2026, which costs a late fee (₹1,000 or ₹5,000 depending on income) and interest on unpaid tax, but no extra charge.

## What it costs: an example

Say you find you left out income for FY 2024-25, and the tax plus interest on it comes to ₹20,000.

| When you file | Extra charge | Total you pay |
|---|---|---|
| By 31 Mar 2027 | 25% = ₹5,000 | ₹25,000 |
| Apr 2027 to Mar 2028 | 50% = ₹10,000 | ₹30,000 |
| Apr 2028 to Mar 2029 | 60% = ₹12,000 | ₹32,000 |
| Apr 2029 to Mar 2030 | 70% = ₹14,000 | ₹34,000 |

The longer you wait, the more you pay, and the higher the chance that a notice arrives first. Interest also keeps adding up each month until you pay.

## Before you file: find what's missing

1. Download your **Annual Information Statement (AIS)** for the year from the e-filing portal. It lists interest, dividends, securities sales and other transactions reported against your PAN.
2. Compare it with the return you filed (or with your bank statements if you never filed).
3. Work out the tax on the missing income at that year's rates, under the regime you used for that year. Subtract any TDS already deducted.

If the numbers involve capital gains, business income or several years at once, a chartered accountant is worth paying for. Errors in an updated return can't be fixed with another one.

## How to file ITR-U on the portal

CA Puneet Jain's [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9d_1CCeT9fU) uses the offline utility:

1. **Download the utility.** On incometax.gov.in, go to **Downloads**, choose the assessment year and your ITR form (ITR-1, ITR-2 and so on), and pick the Excel utility marked for the updated return.
2. **Fill it in.** Enter the full return as it should have been, plus the updated-return section: why you're updating, which 12-month band you're in, and the extra income.
3. **Pay the tax first.** Pay the tax, interest and extra charge through **e-Pay Tax** before submitting, then enter those challan details in the updated-return part of the utility. The video points out these go separately from the normal tax-paid entries.
4. **Generate the JSON** from the utility.
5. **Upload it.** Log in, go to **e-File > Income Tax Returns > File Income Tax Return**, select the assessment year and the updated-return option, and upload the JSON.
6. **Verify it** with Aadhaar OTP, net banking or a digital signature. An unverified return doesn't count.

## If a reassessment notice has already arrived

Until 2026, an updated return wasn't allowed once the department had started reassessment proceedings. The Finance Act 2026 changed that. You can now file one in response to a reassessment notice, within the time the notice allows, paying the normal extra charge **plus another 10%**. [SAG Infotech's summary](https://blog.saginfotech.com/budget-2026-updated-itr-filing-allowed-reassessment-notice-it-act-2025) notes that income disclosed this way is protected from penalty. It's still more expensive than acting first, and at this stage professional help is worth having.

ITR-U still isn't available if a search or survey has started against you.

## Owed a refund instead? Use condonation of delay

If you missed a return and tax was over-deducted (common for pensioners and people with FD interest), ITR-U won't help. The route is a **condonation of delay** request to the tax department, raised from the e-filing portal. Under [CBDT Circular 11/2024](https://www.mondaq.com/india/tax-authorities/1543964/cbdt-issues-fresh-guidelines-for-handling-applications-for-condonation-of-delay-under-section-1192b-for-refunds-and-loss-claims), these can be accepted for claims made within five years from the end of the assessment year, where the delay had a reasonable cause. Commissioners decide claims of up to ₹1 crore a year, and no interest is paid on the refund for the delay.

Watch for anyone offering to "get your refund back with ITR-U". The rules don't allow it, and the same caution applies to messages promising refunds by link; our guide to [how UPI fraud happens](https://techpulzo.in/how-upi-fraud-actually-happens-the-five-warning-signs) covers the pattern.

## Frequently asked questions

### Who can file an updated return?

Anyone who wants to report more income or correct a return for an open year, whether or not they filed a return for that year. It's not allowed if it would lower your tax, increase a refund or loss, if you've already filed an updated return for that year, or if a search or survey has started.

### How many years back can I file ITR-U?

Up to 48 months from the end of the relevant assessment year. As of October 2026 that covers FY 2021-22 to FY 2024-25. FY 2021-22 closes on 31 March 2027.

### How much extra tax do I pay with ITR-U?

25% of the tax plus interest if you file within 12 months of the end of the assessment year, 50% within 24 months, 60% within 36 months and 70% within 48 months. After a reassessment notice, another 10% is added.

### Can I claim a refund through ITR-U?

No. An updated return can't create or increase a refund. If you're owed money for a year you didn't file, apply for condonation of delay through the e-filing portal instead.

### Can I file ITR-U for FY 2025-26?

You don't need to yet. A belated or revised return for FY 2025-26 (AY 2026-27) can be filed until 31 December 2026, and it costs a late fee and interest rather than the extra ITR-U charge.

## The short version

Check your AIS against what you filed, work out the tax you missed, pay it with interest and the extra charge, and upload an ITR-U before the band steps up on 31 March 2027. If you're owed money rather than owing it, ITR-U is the wrong tool; ask for condonation of delay instead.
