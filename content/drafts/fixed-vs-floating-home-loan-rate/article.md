# Fixed vs floating home loan rates in India: which to choose in 2026

Choosing fixed vs floating for a home loan sounds like a bet on where interest rates go. In India it's mostly a question of price. Fixed-rate home loans cost well over a percentage point more than floating ones, and many "fixed" loans are fixed for only the first few years. With repo-linked floating rates around 7.5–8% for good borrowers, rates would have to rise a long way and stay there before fixing came out ahead.

This guide compares what banks charge for each in 2026, works through the numbers, explains what "fixed" really means in loan agreements, and covers the few situations where fixing makes sense.

## What each one is

- **Floating rate:** linked to an external benchmark, which for banks such as HDFC and SBI is the RBI's repo rate. When the repo moves, your rate moves at the next reset. HDFC Bank's [rate page](https://homeloans.hdfc.bank.in/checklist/home-loan-interest-rates), for example, prices its adjustable-rate home loans at repo + 2.50% to 7.95%, which works out to 7.75% to 13.20% with the repo at 5.25%.
- **Fixed rate:** the rate is set at the start and doesn't follow the repo, for the period the agreement says.
- **Hybrid (fixed, then floating):** fixed for an initial period, then floating. HDFC's "TruFixed" loan, on the same page, is fixed for a period such as the first two years and then becomes adjustable. Moneyview's [explainer video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3ch0G9JdqMA) notes that hybrid loans are commonly fixed for three to five years.

Both types work on the reducing balance, so interest is charged only on what you still owe. CA Hardik K. Thacker's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SwBSNfHYDWM) contrasts this with "flat" rates on some other loans.

## The numbers: ₹50 lakh over 20 years

Compare a floating loan at 7.75% with a fixed loan at 9.5%, roughly mid-range for Bank of Baroda's fixed band.

| Scenario for the floating rate | Total interest, floating | vs fixed at 9.5% (₹61.9 lakh) |
|---|---|---|
| Stays at 7.75% | ₹48.5 lakh | **₹13.3 lakh less** |
| Rises 1% after two years and stays there | ₹54.8 lakh | ₹7.1 lakh less |
| Jumps 2.5% for three years, then falls back | ₹52.7 lakh | ₹9.2 lakh less |
| Falls 1% after two years | ₹42.4 lakh | ₹19.4 lakh less |

*EMI recalculated on the remaining balance at each change; tenure kept at 20 years.*

The EMI at 9.5% fixed is ₹46,607, against ₹41,047 floating, so you'd pay about ₹5,560 a month more from day one for protection. For fixed to win, the floating rate would have to **average more than 9.5% over the whole loan**, about 1.75 points above today's rate for 20 years. A short spike, even a large one, doesn't do it.

## What "fixed" often means in the fine print

Before paying extra for a fixed rate, read the agreement for:

- **How long it's fixed.** Many products are fixed for two to five years and then float. Ask what rate it becomes afterwards.
- **A reset clause.** Check whether the agreement lets the lender revise the "fixed" rate at intervals or in set circumstances.
- **Prepayment charges.** The RBI's ban on foreclosure and prepayment charges applies to **floating**-rate loans to individuals. Fixed-rate loans can still carry a charge, as long as it's disclosed upfront, [News On AIR reported](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/rbi-bars-pre-payment-charges-on-transfer-of-floating-rate-loans-for-individual-borrowers/) when the 2025 rules came out.

That last point matters more than it looks. Moneyview's video shows that on a ₹25 lakh, 9%, 20-year loan, paying one extra EMI a year cuts the loan to about 16 years. On a floating loan you can do that freely; on a fixed loan it may cost a fee each time.

## When a fixed rate can make sense

- **The premium is small.** If a lender's fixed rate is within about a percentage point of its floating rate (some public-sector banks at the low end), the cost of certainty is modest.
- **Your budget can't absorb any rise.** A tight EMI-to-income ratio, a single income, or a fixed pension can justify paying for a predictable EMI.
- **A short fixed period when rates are likely to rise.** A hybrid loan fixed for two or three years can soften a rate cycle without locking you in for 20 years. With the repo at 5.25% after the 2025 cuts and [inflation picking up](https://www.gopocket.in/news/rbi-policy-october-2026-repo-rate-inflation-emi-outlook) in 2026, this is the case borrowers will hear most.

For most borrowers, the better protection against rising rates is a floating loan plus a buffer: borrow less than the maximum, and prepay when you can. Our guide to [how much home loan you can afford](https://techpulzo.in/how-much-home-loan-you-can-actually-afford) covers leaving that room.

## Already on a floating loan? Switching options

The RBI's rules on [resetting floating rates](https://www.rbi.org.in/commonman/Upload/English/FAQs/PDFs/FAQRFIR10012025.pdf) let lenders offer existing borrowers a switch to a fixed rate when rates reset. Since October 2025 that's at the lender's discretion, and lenders may charge a switching fee, which must be disclosed. If your bank offers a switch, compare the fixed rate with your current floating rate using the same logic as above: what average floating rate would make the fixed one cheaper?

If your real worry is that your floating rate is high, switching lenders or asking for a lower spread usually helps more than fixing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is fixed or floating better for a home loan in India?

For most borrowers, floating. Fixed-rate home loans cost about 1 to 6 percentage points more depending on the bank, and on a ₹50 lakh, 20-year loan the floating rate would have to average about 1.75 points higher for the whole term before fixed came out cheaper.

### Does a fixed home loan rate stay the same for the whole tenure?

Not always. Many products are fixed only for the first two to five years and then convert to floating, and some agreements allow the lender to reset the fixed rate. Check the period and any reset clause before signing.

### Can I prepay a fixed-rate home loan without charges?

Not necessarily. The RBI's ban on prepayment charges covers floating-rate loans to individuals. Fixed-rate loans can carry a prepayment charge if it's disclosed in the loan documents.

### Can I switch my floating home loan to a fixed rate?

If your lender offers it. RBI rules allow lenders to offer a switch at the time of a rate reset, and since October 2025 it's optional for them. A switching fee may apply.

### Should I fix my home loan rate before the RBI raises rates?

Only if the fixed rate is close to your floating rate or your budget can't take any increase. A temporary rise of even 2.5 percentage points for a few years usually costs less than paying a fixed premium for 20 years.

## The short version

In India a fixed rate usually costs one to six points more than floating, and is often fixed only for a few years. On a typical loan, the floating rate would need to average well above today's level for the whole term before fixing pays. Choose floating, keep a buffer, prepay freely, and consider fixed only when the premium is small or your budget truly can't flex.
