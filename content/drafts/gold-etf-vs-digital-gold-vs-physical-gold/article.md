# Gold ETF vs digital gold vs physical gold: which to buy in 2026

If you want gold as an investment rather than as jewellery, a gold ETF is the cheapest and best-protected way to hold it for most people in India. A gold mutual fund does the same job without a demat account. Digital gold on payment apps is convenient, but it costs about 3% in GST the moment you buy, carries a buy-sell spread, and isn't regulated by SEBI. Physical coins and bars make sense if you specifically want metal you can hold, and jewellery is a purchase, not an investment.

The details behind that, and the cases where the answer changes, are below.

## The four ways to own gold in 2026

Sovereign Gold Bonds used to be the obvious answer, but the government hasn't issued a new one since February 2024 (more on that below). That leaves four routes:

| | Gold ETF | Gold mutual fund (fund of funds) | Digital gold | Physical coin or bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What you own | Units of a fund that holds vaulted gold | Units of a fund that holds gold ETF units | A claim on gold held by the platform's vault partner | The metal |
| Regulator | SEBI | SEBI | None | None for the investment |
| Where it's held | Your demat account | With the fund house (no demat needed) | The platform's records | Your home or bank locker |
| GST when buying | None | None | 3% | 3% |
| Yearly cost | Expense ratio, median 0.51% | Its own expense ratio, plus the ETF's | No fee, but a buy-sell spread | Storage, locker rent |
| Smallest purchase | One ETF unit | A small SIP amount | Very small amounts | Usually a gram or more |
| Long-term tax after | 12 months | 24 months | 24 months | 24 months |

*ETF expense ratios: AMFI data for September 2026, 12 gold ETFs. Tax: see the tax section below.*

## Digital gold: what SEBI said, and what's changing

On 8 November 2025, SEBI issued a [public caution about digital gold](https://www.sebi.gov.in/media-and-notifications/press-releases/nov-2025/caution-to-public-regarding-dealing-in-digital-gold-_97676.html). Its points, in plain terms:

- Digital and "e-gold" products sold on apps are **neither securities nor regulated commodity derivatives**. They operate entirely outside SEBI's oversight.
- They **may expose you to counterparty and operational risk**: if the seller or its partners fail, you depend on their contract with you.
- **None of the investor protections** of the securities market apply.
- SEBI's regulated gold routes are gold ETFs, Electronic Gold Receipts traded on stock exchanges, and exchange-traded gold derivatives.

Zerodha's Daily Brief explained in [its episode on the warning](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=syW84KpuLCg) why this matters. A gold ETF's units sit in your demat account with a depository, and the fund has trustees, a custodian, auditors and a published daily NAV. With digital gold, what you own is defined by the platform's terms and conditions, and questions like whether your gold is set aside separately or pooled with everyone else's depend on that contract.

**What's changing.** In September 2026, The Economic Times reported that the Finance Ministry had [sought views from RBI and SEBI](https://inshorts.com/en/news/rbi--sebi-may-regulate-digital-gold--physical-gold-backing-proposed--report-1789810487052) on treating digital gold as a regulated investment under securities law. The proposal would require platforms to hold matching physical gold for every unit sold, report their holdings regularly and allow independent audits. It is at the [consultation stage with no timeline](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/sebiexchange/Government-Moves-to-Regulate-dollar3B-Digital-Gold-Market/6aaceb59ed7f8e81734fb787). Until rules are actually in force, SEBI's November 2025 caution describes the position.

## What each route costs you

Costs show up at three points: when you buy, while you hold, and when you sell.

**Gold ETF.** No GST. You pay brokerage (zero or close to it at many brokers) and the expense ratio, which across 12 gold ETFs in [AMFI's September 2026 TER data](https://www.amfiindia.com/ter-of-mf-schemes) ranged from 0.35% to 0.65% a year, with a median of 0.51%. When you buy or sell on the exchange, you also pay the small gap between the buying and selling price, which is usually narrow for the larger ETFs.

**Gold mutual fund.** These are funds of funds that invest in a gold ETF. The direct plans' own expense ratios had a median of 0.18% in the same data, but you also bear the expense ratio of the ETF they hold, so the total is higher than holding the ETF directly. In return, you don't need a demat account and you can run a monthly SIP easily.

**Digital gold.** You pay **3% GST** on every purchase, because legally you're buying goods, not an investment. Platforms also quote different buying and selling prices. Zerodha's estimate is a gap of about 2–3%, so ₹10,000 of digital gold is worth roughly ₹9,400–9,500 if you sell it straight back. There's no annual fee, but the entry cost is high.

**Coins and bars.** The same 3% GST applies. Banks and jewellers often charge a premium over the gold price when you buy, and when you sell, a jeweller usually pays less than the market rate. XY-Axis Education's walkthrough also points to making charges, which apply to jewellery and, at a lower level, to some coins.

**Jewellery** adds making charges, with 5% GST on those charges, on top of the 3% on the gold. That's why jewellery is a poor way to invest, whatever its other value.

### Example: ₹1 lakh held for five years

Suppose gold rises 8% a year for five years and you then sell. With no costs, ₹1 lakh would be worth about ₹1.47 lakh.

| Route | Value after 5 years, before tax | After 12.5% long-term tax |
|---|---|---|
| Gold ETF (0.5% a year) | about ₹1,43,300 | about ₹1,37,900 |
| Digital gold (3% GST, 3% spread) | about ₹1,38,400 | about ₹1,33,600 |

*Our calculation. The 3% spread is Zerodha's estimate; actual spreads vary by platform. Tax excludes cess.*

The ETF ends about ₹4,300 ahead on a ₹1 lakh investment, mostly because of the GST paid on day one. The longer you hold, the more the ETF's yearly fee eats into that lead, but at 0.5% a year it would take more than a decade to use up the 6% that digital gold loses upfront.

## How each is taxed

| | Short-term (taxed at your slab rate) | Long-term (12.5%, no indexation) |
|---|---|---|
| Gold ETF | Held 12 months or less | Held more than 12 months |
| Gold mutual fund (FoF) | Held 24 months or less | Held more than 24 months |
| Digital gold, coins, bars, jewellery | Held 24 months or less | Held more than 24 months |

*Sources: [Aditya Birla Capital](https://www.adityabirlacapital.com/abc-of-money/tax-on-gold-etf) and [CAalley](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/gold-taxation-explained-physical-vs-etf-vs-sgb-vs-inherited-gold), 2026.*

The 12-month rule for gold ETFs is fairly new: it applies from 1 April 2025. Before that, gold ETFs were taxed at slab rates however long you held them, so older articles may say otherwise. Gold mutual funds still need 24 months, which is one reason to prefer the ETF if you already have a demat account.

## What about Sovereign Gold Bonds?

SGBs paid 2.5% interest a year on top of the gold price, and gains at maturity were tax-free, which made them the best way to hold gold for eight years. But the last tranche was issued in February 2024, and none has been announced since.

Existing SGBs still trade on the stock exchanges, so you can buy them there. Two cautions:

- Trading is thin for many series, so prices can sit above or below the gold value.
- Per [CAalley's summary of Budget 2026](https://www.caalley.com/news-updates/indian-news/gold-taxation-explained-physical-vs-etf-vs-sgb-vs-inherited-gold), the tax-free treatment at maturity now applies only to bonds bought at the original issue and held to maturity. If you buy SGBs on the exchange, gains are taxable.

If you already hold SGBs from the original issue, there's rarely a reason to sell early.

## Which should you choose?

- **You have a demat account and want gold as part of your portfolio:** a low-cost gold ETF. Compare expense ratios and check that the ETF trades in reasonable volumes.
- **You don't have a demat account, or want an automatic monthly SIP:** a gold mutual fund in the direct plan, accepting a slightly higher total cost and the 24-month tax rule.
- **You're buying small amounts on a payment app:** know that it's unregulated, that you start about 5–6% behind, and that you're relying on the platform. For more than small sums, an ETF or gold fund does the same job more cheaply. Apps earn from these spreads, much as we described in [how cashback apps make money](https://techpulzo.in/how-cashback-apps-actually-make-money-off-you).
- **You want metal you can hold, as insurance against a crisis:** coins or bars from a reputable seller, with a hallmark, kept safely. Alok Jain makes this case in [a Groww interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-QVmJ-KpE0Q), arguing that physical gold removes the dependence on any company. The trade-off is the 3% GST, storage, and a lower price when you sell.

**How much gold?** That's a separate question, and answers vary widely. Jain's own rule of thumb, in the same interview, is your age divided by two as a percentage of your net worth; many investors hold far less. Whatever you choose, adding to it gradually through a SIP avoids the risk of buying at a peak; our guide to [SIP vs lump-sum investing](https://techpulzo.in/sip-vs-lump-sum-how-to-actually-decide-where-to-put-your-money) covers that choice.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is digital gold safe?

It's not regulated by SEBI or RBI. SEBI's November 2025 caution says digital gold carries counterparty and operational risk and that none of the securities market's investor protections apply. Your rights depend on the platform's contract, not on securities law. A September 2026 proposal to regulate it is still at the consultation stage.

### Is a gold ETF better than a gold mutual fund?

For someone with a demat account, usually yes: the total cost is lower, and gains become long-term after 12 months instead of 24. A gold mutual fund is the better fit if you don't have a demat account or want a simple monthly SIP.

### Do I pay GST on gold ETFs?

No. GST of 3% applies to buying physical gold and digital gold, but not to gold ETFs, gold mutual funds or Sovereign Gold Bonds.

### Can I still buy Sovereign Gold Bonds?

Not from the government: no new tranche has been issued since February 2024. You can buy existing bonds on the stock exchange, but trading is thin, and after Budget 2026 only original subscribers who hold to maturity get tax-free gains.

### How long must I hold a gold ETF for long-term capital gains?

More than 12 months, for units bought from 1 April 2025. Gains are then taxed at 12.5% without indexation. If you sell within 12 months, the gain is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.

## The short version

Buy gold the way you'd buy any other investment: cheaply, in a regulated wrapper, and in a form you can sell easily. For most people that's a gold ETF or a direct-plan gold fund. Keep digital gold for small, occasional amounts if you like the convenience, and buy physical gold when you actually want the metal.
