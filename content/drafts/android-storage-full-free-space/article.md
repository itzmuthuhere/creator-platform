# Android storage full? How to free space without deleting your photos

When your Android storage is full, the phone slows down, apps won't update and the camera may refuse to save. The usual advice is to delete photos, but you rarely need to. Most phones have gigabytes tied up in apps you don't use, app caches, WhatsApp media and forgotten downloads, and your photos can stay safe in the cloud while their copies leave the phone.

This guide works through the fixes in order of payoff, using Android's own tools, and lists the popular tips that can cost you data.

## First, see what's using the space

Open **Settings > Storage**. You'll see a breakdown by category: apps, images, videos, audio, documents, system and other files. On most phones there's also a **Free up space** button that lists large files, unused apps and backed-up photos, as [Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/7431795?hl=en) describes.

Your phone maker's file app (Files by Google, or My Files on Samsung) usually has a "storage analysis" or "clean" view that finds large files, duplicates and junk.

Note the two or three biggest categories. That's where to start.

## 1. Photos and videos: keep them, but not on the phone

If photos and videos are the biggest category, the fix is to keep them in the cloud and remove only the copies on your phone.

1. **Confirm your backup.** In Google Photos, check that backup is on and complete for the folders you care about. If it shows items waiting, let it finish on Wi-Fi.
2. **Use "Free up space".** In Google Photos, tap your profile picture and choose **Free up space**. According to [Google Photos Help](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128843?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid), it deletes the device copies of photos and videos that are already backed up, which you can still see in the Google Photos app and at photos.google.com. Items less than 30 days old may be kept on the phone.
3. **Optionally turn on Smart Storage** (Settings > Storage), which removes backed-up photos automatically when the phone is nearly full.

Two cautions: removed items won't show in your phone's built-in gallery app or when you're offline, only in Google Photos; and cloud backups use your Google storage, so check you have room. If your Google storage is the problem too, compare options in our guide to [Google Drive, OneDrive and iCloud](https://techpulzo.in/google-drive-vs-onedrive-vs-icloud-which-cloud-storage-to-use).

## 2. Archive apps you rarely use

Archiving removes most of an app but keeps your data and its icon, so nothing is lost. Google's [Android Developers Blog](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2023/04/reduce-uninstalls-for-your-app-with-auto-archive.html) says it frees up to about 60% of an app's space. The icon shows a small cloud, and tapping it downloads the app again with your data intact.

- To do it automatically: Play Store > your profile picture > **Settings > General > Automatically archive apps**, the toggle [Android Police](https://www.androidpolice.com/google-play-store-auto-app-archiving-rollout-everyone/) describes.
- To find candidates: Settings > Apps, sort by size, and look for big apps you haven't opened in months (travel, shopping, games).

As an illustration, archiving ten rarely used apps of about 300 MB each frees roughly 1.8 GB, with no logins or data lost.

## 3. Clear caches, but not data

Every app keeps a **cache** of temporary files to load faster. Clearing it is safe; the app rebuilds what it needs. **Clearing storage or data** is different: it resets the app, signs you out and can delete things saved only on the phone.

1. Settings > Apps, and sort by size.
2. Open the biggest apps (browsers, social media, streaming apps).
3. Tap **Storage**, then **Clear cache** only.

Device Guardian's [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1PokGP-1yyY) makes the same point: clear the cache of your ten largest apps, but never tap Clear data unless you mean to reset the app.

## 4. WhatsApp and other media apps

Messaging apps quietly fill phones with forwarded videos and images.

- **WhatsApp:** Settings > **Storage and data > Manage storage**. Review the "Larger than 5 MB" list and the chats using the most space, and delete media you don't need.
- **Downloaded content:** streaming and music apps keep offline downloads. Remove downloads from YouTube, Spotify, YouTube Music, Netflix and similar apps once you've watched or no longer need them, as Android Help suggests.

## 5. Downloads, large files and the bin

- Open your Files app and check **Downloads** for old PDFs, APKs and videos.
- Use the **large files** view to find old screen recordings and unedited videos.
- **Empty the bin.** Many file and gallery apps keep deleted files for a period (often 30 days) before removing them, so deleting without emptying the bin frees nothing yet.
- For files you want to keep but not on the phone, copy them to a computer with a USB cable, then delete them from the phone.

## What not to do

- **Don't tap "Clear data" on apps you use.** It signs you out and resets them. RaRe iTech's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6MjUJCLE3o0) does this to Chrome; clearing the cache would have been enough.
- **Deleting the Play Store's "Not installed" list frees nothing on your phone.** That list is your account's history of apps, not files on the device, even though some videos suggest otherwise.
- **Don't delete unknown system or hidden folders** because a video says they're junk. Some belong to apps and their removal can break things.
- **Skip "cleaner" and "booster" apps.** Android's own Free up space tool and your Files app do the same job without ads or extra permissions.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I free up space on Android without deleting photos?

Back your photos up to Google Photos, then use its Free up space option to remove only the copies on your phone. Then archive apps you rarely use, clear app caches, delete large WhatsApp media and old downloads, and empty your Files app's bin.

### Is it safe to clear cache on Android?

Yes. The cache holds temporary files that apps rebuild when needed. Clearing it doesn't sign you out or delete your data. Clearing storage or data is different: it resets the app completely.

### What does archiving an app do?

It removes most of the app's files but keeps your data and the icon on your home screen, with a small cloud on it. Tapping the icon downloads the app again and you carry on where you left off. Google says it frees up to about 60% of the app's space.

### Why is my storage still full after deleting files?

Your Files or Gallery app probably moved them to a bin, where they still take space for a period, often 30 days. Empty the bin to free the space immediately.

### Will Google Photos "Free up space" delete my photos?

Only the copies on your phone, and only for items already backed up. They stay in Google Photos and at photos.google.com. Check that backup is complete before you use it, and remember they won't show in your phone's built-in gallery app.

## A ten-minute routine

Check Settings > Storage, confirm your Photos backup and free up its device copies, archive two or three big unused apps, clear the cache of your largest apps, trim WhatsApp media and empty the bin. Repeat every few months and the warning rarely comes back.
