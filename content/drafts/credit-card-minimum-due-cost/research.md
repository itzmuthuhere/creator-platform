# Research notes: What paying only the credit card minimum due costs you

Internal working file. Not published.

## 1. Source videos (in my own words)

- **Primary:** सावधान! Credit Card का Minimum Due आपको कंगाल बना देगा! (Zee Business, interview with Harshvardhan Roongta), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gunMbslFflQ. ~10 min, ~2 months old, Hindi auto-captions.
- **Second:** Minimum Due: The Credit Card Trap… (Finance Samjho), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Sm4Bz8KK-94. ~5 min, ~1 month old, Hindi auto-captions.
- **Third:** Credit Card - Minimum Amount Due Changed on SBI Cards (Money & Banking, Tamil), https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=biyG_Ius2tQ. ~11 min, ~1 year old, Tamil auto-captions.
- Considered and dropped: VD Tamil (ePoi4xkGwZM): mostly the creator's background in collections; little checkable material.

**Zee Business:** paying the minimum keeps the card active and avoids late fees, but the rest rolls over at roughly 35–45% a year. Ways out: redeem investments, a cheaper personal loan, or a balance transfer, each as a one-time rescue rather than a habit.

**Finance Samjho:** a dramatised explainer. Claims the minimum is set by an RBI rule (higher of interest+fees or 5%), that ₹1 lakh at ~42% paid at the minimum takes ~20 years, cites a 2009 UK study (Neil Stewart) on minimum payments as anchors, Indian card-market statistics, and suggests converting to EMI or paying double the minimum.

**Money & Banking:** explains SBI Card's change (from July 2025) to a higher minimum-due formula: 100% of GST, EMIs, fees and finance charges plus a share of the balance, and a new order in which payments are applied.

Useful material across the three:
- The minimum avoids late fees and keeps the account current; the rest is charged interest.
- Payoff takes decades on the minimum; paying double or a fixed amount changes that drastically.
- Issuers are raising the minimum (SBI Card 2025).
- Ways out: EMI conversion, personal loan, balance transfer.

**Verdict:** PROCEED.

**Chosen angle:** Most explainers stop at "the minimum is a trap, it takes years". The bigger surprise in the MITCs is that paying *almost* all of the bill is also expensive: interest runs from each purchase date on the whole bill, and new purchases lose their interest-free period. The article prices that, compares the two current minimum formulas (HDFC 5%, SBI Card 2%), and lays out what to do this month.

## 6. Value the video doesn't give

- Interest on the full bill from purchase date when you pay most but not all: ₹5,000 left from a ₹50,000 bill costs ~₹3,000 for one month.
- GST on interest: 45% becomes 53.1%.
- Side-by-side of current HDFC and SBI Card minimum formulas with simulations.
- RBI rules (no negative amortisation, 3-day past-due rule, warning legend, interest-free period suspension).
- Official SmartEMI rates as the realistic exit route, with the APR.
- Correction of the "RBI 5% rule" claim.
