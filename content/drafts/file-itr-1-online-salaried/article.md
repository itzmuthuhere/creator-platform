# How to file ITR-1 online: a salaried person's guide (including late filing)

If you haven't filed your return for 2025-26 yet, you still can, until 31 December 2026, but it now costs a late fee and you're locked into the new tax regime. If you're reading this ahead of next July, you'll have both regimes and no fee. Either way, the steps to file ITR-1 online are the same, and for most salaried people the whole thing takes under an hour once the documents are ready.

This guide covers who can use ITR-1, what changes if you're filing late, the four documents to collect first, the steps on the e-filing portal, and how to fix the mismatches that cause notices.

## Can you use ITR-1?

ITR-1 (also called Sahaj) is the simplest return form. According to the [Income Tax Department's ITR-1 FAQ](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/ITR1-FAQ), for 2025-26 incomes you can use it if you're a resident individual with total income up to ₹50 lakh from:

- salary or pension;
- up to two house properties;
- other sources such as savings and FD interest or family pension;
- long-term capital gains on listed shares or equity funds up to ₹1.25 lakh;
- agricultural income up to ₹5,000.

You **can't** use ITR-1 if you're a company director, held unlisted shares, have foreign assets or income, have business income, have any short-term capital gains, or want to carry forward losses. Those cases need ITR-2 or another form.

## If you missed 31 July

The due date for ITR-1 for 2025-26 was 31 July 2026, and it was [not extended](https://cleartax.in/s/due-date-tax-filing). You can still file a **belated return** until **31 December 2026**, with three consequences:

1. **A late fee.** ₹1,000 if your total income is up to ₹5 lakh, ₹5,000 if it's higher. If your income is below the basic exemption limit, so that you weren't required to file at all, the portal doesn't charge it, as a Tax Filing School video demonstrates.
2. **Interest on any unpaid tax**, at 1% a month from the due date.
3. **No old regime.** The department's [regime FAQ](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/new-tax-vs-old-tax-regime-faqs) says the choice must be made by the due date under section 139(1). As tax expert Suresh Surana [explains](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/forced-new-tax-regime-missed-itr-deadline.html), a belated return can't opt for the old regime. If you'd planned to claim HRA or home-loan interest, that saving is gone for this year.

Even so, file. A belated return gets your TDS refund processed, keeps a clean record for loans and visas, and avoids a notice for not filing. After 31 December, the remaining route is an updated return (ITR-U), which has its own conditions and extra costs.

## Before you start: four documents

Download and keep these open while you file:

1. **Form 16** from your employer. Part B has the salary breakdown you'll check against.
2. **Form 26AS**, which shows TDS deducted against your PAN. Download it from the e-filing portal under e-File → Income Tax Returns → View Form 26AS.
3. **AIS (Annual Information Statement)**, which shows interest, dividends, high-value transactions and more. In her walkthrough, CA Shweta Verma recommends filing after mid-June so the AIS is fully updated.
4. **Bank account details** for every account you held during the year, plus the one you want refunds paid into.

If you switched jobs during the year, you'll have two Form 16s, and both salaries must be added.

## Step by step on the e-filing portal

The sequence below follows FinTaxPro's live demo of ITR-1, checked against the other walkthroughs.

1. **Log in** to the e-filing portal with your PAN and password. (Lost your PAN card? You only need the number, but here's [how to get a duplicate PAN card](https://techpulzo.in/how-to-get-a-duplicate-pan-card-online).)
2. Go to **e-File → Income Tax Returns → File Income Tax Return**. Choose assessment year **2026-27** (for income earned April 2025–March 2026), **online** mode, and **Start New Filing**.
3. Select **Individual**, then **ITR-1**. Choose the reason for filing, usually "taxable income is more than the basic exemption limit".
4. **Personal information.** Check your name, address and contact details; select your **nature of employment** (private-sector employees choose "Others"). The filing section shows **139(1)** if you're on time, or **139(4)** for a belated return. Confirm the **regime**: the new regime is the default, and a belated return can't change it.
5. **Bank accounts.** List all accounts and select one for the refund. The account must be pre-validated on the portal.
6. **Gross total income: salary.** Salary is often pre-filled from your employer's TDS data. Match every figure with Form 16 Part B, including any exempt allowances. The ₹75,000 standard deduction (new regime) is applied automatically.
7. **Other income.** Savings and FD interest are pre-filled from AIS when available; add anything missing. Report exempt items, such as PF withdrawals or tax-free interest, under the exempt-income section, not as taxable income.
8. **House property**, if you have one or two.
9. **Deductions.** Under the new regime very few apply; your employer's NPS contribution is the main one. Under the old regime (on-time filers only), enter the deductions you can document.
10. **Tax paid.** TDS from Form 16 and other TDS/TCS appear here. Match them with Form 26AS; add any advance or self-assessment tax.
11. **Tax liability.** Review the computation. If tax, interest or the late fee is due, pay it online before submitting.
12. **Preview, validate and submit**, then **e-verify** immediately with an Aadhaar OTP.

FinTaxPro's demo also warns against claiming allowances that aren't on your Form 16. Fake HRA or allowance claims are a common reason returns get picked for scrutiny.

## Mismatches and how to fix them

| What you see | Why it happens | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| TDS in Form 16 more than in 26AS | Employer filed TDS late or incorrectly | Ask the employer to correct the TDS return; claim only what 26AS shows until it's fixed |
| Interest in AIS higher than you expected | AIS includes all FDs and accounts | Report the AIS figure, or give AIS feedback if an entry is wrong |
| Salary pre-filled differently from Form 16 | Two employers, or pre-fill incomplete | Edit to match your Form 16s; add both employers' salaries |
| Error on bank account | Account not pre-validated | Validate it under the profile's bank account section, then file |
| Portal won't offer old regime | You're filing after the due date | Expected; belated returns use the new regime |

Reporting everything AIS shows matters: as Shweta Verma notes, an AIS entry missing from your return can lead to a notice.

## After filing: e-verify, refund, revise

- **E-verify within 30 days.** The [ITR-1 FAQ](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/ITR1-FAQ) gives 30 days. Aadhaar OTP is the quickest way.
- **Refunds** go to the bank account you nominated once the return is processed. You can track the status on the portal.
- **Made a mistake?** Use the revised-return option on the portal; for 2025-26 the window closes on 31 December 2026, the same date as belated returns.
- **Next year:** returns for income earned from April 2026 are filed under the new Income-tax Act, 2025, so form names and section numbers on the portal may change. The process should stay similar.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I still file ITR for 2025-26 after 31 July 2026?

Yes. You can file a belated return until 31 December 2026. You'll pay a late fee of ₹1,000 if your total income is up to ₹5 lakh, or ₹5,000 above that, plus interest on any unpaid tax. You'll be taxed under the new regime, and any capital losses can't be carried forward.

### Is filing ITR mandatory if my employer already deducted TDS?

If your total income is above the basic exemption limit, yes. TDS doesn't replace the return. Filing is also how you get back any excess TDS, how lenders and embassies verify income, and it avoids a notice for not filing.

### Can I file ITR-1 if I sold mutual funds?

Only if all your gains were long-term gains on equity funds or listed shares, up to ₹1.25 lakh in total. Any short-term capital gain, or long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh, means you need ITR-2.

### What happens if I don't e-verify my return?

Verification completes the filing, and the department's FAQ gives 30 days to do it. Don't leave it: an unverified return isn't processed, so any refund waits too. E-verifying with an Aadhaar OTP right after submitting takes a minute.

### Can I switch to the old regime in a belated return?

No. The option to choose the old regime has to be exercised in a return filed by the due date. A belated return filed after 31 July is taxed under the new regime.

## File now, then set a reminder for June

If you're late this year, file before 31 December and accept the new regime and the fee; the refund and a clean record are worth more. Then set a reminder for mid-June next year, when Form 16 and AIS are ready, to file early with both regimes on the table.
