# How PPF interest is calculated, and why the 5th matters

Put ₹1.5 lakh into your PPF account on 3 April and it earns ₹10,650 of interest that year. Put the same amount in on 6 April and it earns ₹9,762. Three days cost ₹887, because of one line in the scheme's rules about the 5th of the month.

That line is the core of PPF interest calculation, and it's worth understanding properly. But as the numbers below show, the 5th is the smaller of two timing decisions. The month of the year you deposit in matters far more.

## How PPF interest is calculated, month by month

The [Public Provident Fund Scheme, 2019](https://taxguru.in/finance/public-provident-fund-scheme-2019-detailed-analysis.html) says interest is earned for each calendar month "on the lowest balance at the credit of an account between the close of the fifth day and the end of the month". In practice:

- Money in the account by the end of the 5th earns interest for that whole month.
- Money deposited on the 6th or later doesn't count for that month. It starts earning from the next month.
- A withdrawal during the month lowers the balance for that month, because the lowest balance is what counts.

Interest is worked out monthly at one-twelfth of the yearly rate, but it's added to your account only once a year, on 31 March, as [SBI's PPF FAQ](https://sbi.bank.in/web/faq-s/faq-public-provident-fund) explains. So interest compounds yearly, not monthly.

The rate itself is set by the government each quarter. For October–December 2026 it's [7.1% a year](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/ppf-rate-unchanged-at-7-1-what-investors-need-to-know-this-quarter-126100200411_1.html), unchanged from the previous quarter.

A small example: you have ₹2,40,000 in the account on 1 June and deposit ₹30,000 on 12 June. The lowest balance between the 5th and 30 June is ₹2,40,000, so June's interest is ₹2,40,000 × 7.1% ÷ 12 = ₹1,420. The ₹30,000 starts counting in July. (CA Rahul Agarwal walks through a similar case in his explainer.)

## What the 5th is worth

One month's interest on ₹1.5 lakh at 7.1% is ₹887. That's the cost of depositing on the 6th instead of the 5th, once. If you contribute monthly, it adds up across the year and the 15-year term.

Here are five ways of putting ₹1.5 lakh a year into PPF for 15 years, all at 7.1%:

| How you deposit ₹1.5 lakh a year | Maturity after 15 years | Short of the best by |
|---|---|---|
| Lump sum on 1–5 April | ₹40.68 lakh | — |
| Lump sum on 6 April or later in April | ₹40.46 lakh | ₹22,000 |
| ₹12,500 a month, on or before the 5th | ₹39.45 lakh | ₹1.24 lakh |
| ₹12,500 a month, after the 5th (say, salary on the 7th) | ₹39.22 lakh | ₹1.46 lakh |
| Lump sum in March, at the end of the year | ₹37.99 lakh | ₹2.70 lakh |

*You deposit ₹22.5 lakh in every case. Assumes 7.1% throughout; the actual rate changes quarterly. Our calculation.*

Missing the 5th costs about ₹22,000 over 15 years, whether you invest once a year or monthly. That's worth avoiding, but it isn't large.

## The bigger lever: which month of the year

The table's real message is in the first and last rows. Depositing in March instead of April costs ₹2.7 lakh over 15 years, more than ten times the cost of missing the 5th.

The reason is simple. Money deposited in April earns interest for 12 months in that financial year; money deposited in March earns interest for at most one. Since interest is credited only on 31 March, the March deposit also misses a year of compounding on the interest it would have earned.

Many people deposit in February or March because that's when they sort out tax-saving investments for the year. If you're on the old tax regime and claim PPF under the ₹1.5 lakh deduction, the deduction is the same whether you deposit in April or March. So move the deposit to April if your cash flow allows; you lose nothing on tax and gain on interest.

The same logic explains the monthly rows. ₹12,500 a month means only the first instalment earns for the full year; the last earns for one month. Monthly investing still ends about ₹1.2 lakh behind an April lump sum, even when every instalment is in by the 5th. If you have the money in April, a lump sum wins. If you're saving it from salary, monthly by the 5th is the next best option, and our [SIP vs lump sum comparison](https://techpulzo.in/sip-vs-lump-sum-how-to-actually-decide-where-to-put-your-money) covers that trade-off for other investments.

## Making it automatic

- **If your salary arrives after the 5th**, schedule PPF for the 1st–5th of the *following* month. Money deposited on 7 July starts earning only from August anyway, so depositing it on 1 August instead loses nothing, and every instalment counts for its full month.
- **Use a standing instruction** from your savings account for the 2nd or 3rd of the month, not the 5th. Transfers can take a day to post, and only money in the account by the end of the 5th counts.
- **For a yearly lump sum**, set a reminder for 1 April. If you deposit at a branch, check its holiday calendar for the first days of April so the money is credited by the 5th.
- **There's no limit on the number of deposits.** Some older explainers, including one of the videos used in researching this article, say you can deposit only 12 times a year. That was the old scheme's rule. Under the 2019 scheme, SBI's FAQ is clear: "There is no restriction on number of instalments," as long as each deposit is a multiple of ₹50 and the year's total stays between ₹500 and ₹1.5 lakh.

## Other rules that change your return

- **The rate can change every quarter.** The tables assume 7.1% for 15 years; the actual maturity will move with the rate.
- **Withdrawals reduce interest the same way deposits add to it.** A partial withdrawal, allowed after five complete financial years, lowers that month's minimum balance.
- **Closing early costs 1%.** Premature closure, allowed in specific cases after five years, pays interest at 1% less than the rate credited throughout, under the [scheme rules](https://taxguru.in/finance/public-provident-fund-scheme-2019-detailed-analysis.html).
- **Extending keeps the clock running.** After 15 years you can extend in five-year blocks, with or without fresh deposits, by submitting Form 4 within a year of maturity. The same 5th-of-the-month rule applies.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens if I deposit in PPF on the 5th of the month?

It counts. The rule looks at the lowest balance from the close of the 5th to the end of the month, so a deposit credited to your PPF account on or before the 5th earns interest for that whole month. Make sure the money reaches the account by then: an online transfer started late on the 5th may post on the 6th.

### Is it better to invest in PPF monthly or in a lump sum?

If you have the full amount at the start of the financial year, a lump sum between 1 and 5 April gives the highest maturity: about ₹1.2 lakh more over 15 years than ₹12,500 a month, at 7.1%. If you're investing from monthly salary, monthly deposits on or before the 5th are the next best choice. The worst pattern is waiting until March.

### How is PPF interest calculated for a year?

Each month, the bank takes the lowest balance between the 5th and the month end and multiplies it by the annual rate divided by 12. The twelve monthly amounts are added up and credited to the account on 31 March. Next year, interest is calculated on the balance including that credited interest, which is how it compounds.

### Is there a limit on how many times I can deposit in PPF in a year?

No. The 2019 scheme removed the old limit of 12 deposits a year. You can deposit any number of times, in multiples of ₹50, as long as you deposit at least ₹500 and no more than ₹1.5 lakh in the financial year.

### Does depositing late affect my tax deduction?

No. Under the old tax regime, PPF deposits count towards the deduction in the financial year they're made, up to ₹1.5 lakh, whatever the month. Timing affects only the interest you earn.

## April, then the 5th

If you remember one thing: get your PPF money in early in the financial year, and early in the month. Of the two, April is worth roughly ten times as much as the 5th.
