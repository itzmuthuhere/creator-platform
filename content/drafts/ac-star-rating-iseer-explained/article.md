# AC star rating and ISEER: what the numbers mean for your electricity bill

The stars on an AC's energy label are a summary. The useful numbers sit next to them: the **ISEER**, which measures efficiency, and the **annual units**, which estimate consumption. Read those, scale them to how you actually use the AC, and you can work out whether a 5-star model is worth its extra price for you. It often isn't for light users, and usually is for anyone running an AC most nights in a hot city.

This guide explains the label, why the stars changed in January 2026, and how to do that calculation, with the payback worked out at real Indian electricity rates.

## What ISEER means

ISEER stands for **Indian Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio**. It's the amount of cooling an AC delivers over a year divided by the electricity it uses to do it. Higher is better: an AC with an ISEER of 5.0 gives about 25% more cooling per unit of electricity than one rated 4.0.

The "seasonal" part matters. The older measure, EER, tested an AC at a single outdoor temperature, as Girish Shinde's [video on EER and ISEER](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XCxHyUTD_Hc) explains. That suited fixed-speed ACs but undersold inverter ACs, which run efficiently at part load. India introduced ISEER in 2015. As [Down To Earth explains](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/energy/what-is-the-star-rating-label-of-an-air-conditioner-telling-you), it tests performance across outdoor temperatures from 24°C to 43°C, weighted by how many hours a year each temperature occurs, based on weather data from 54 Indian cities.

## What's on the label

A BEE label on an AC shows:

| Field | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Stars (1 to 5) | Which efficiency band the ISEER falls in, for that label period |
| ISEER | The actual efficiency figure; compare this between models |
| Annual energy consumption (units per year) | Estimated kWh a year, assuming 1,600 hours of use |
| Cooling capacity | Usually in watts; the real size of the AC |
| Label period | The years the star bands on that label apply to |

The **1,600-hour assumption** is the one to keep in mind. It's a national average. Down To Earth points out that about 1,100 of those hours are at outdoor temperatures of 30°C or below, when many people don't run an AC at all, and that a city like Chennai has far more hot hours than Bengaluru. India uses one national label, so the units figure is a starting point, not your bill.

## The stars changed in January 2026

BEE periodically raises the efficiency needed for each star. The previous AC table was [extended to 31 December 2025](https://www.bl-india.com/notifications/bee-registration/instructions-for-room-air-conditioners), and stricter bands applied from 1 January 2026 under the latest amendments listed on [BEE's star label portal](https://www.beestarlabel.com/Content/Files/AC_Notification.pdf). The practical effect, as Electro by Keshav's [2026 guide](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YILZsm_A7V0) explains, is that a model labelled 5-star under the old bands may only qualify for 4 stars under the new ones.

Two consequences for buyers in 2026:

- **Shops still carry older stock.** A model with an old-period label isn't worse than the label says; its stars were just awarded on the older, easier scale. Check the label period.
- **Stars across periods aren't comparable, but ISEER is.** If you're choosing between an old-label 5-star and a new-label 4-star, compare their ISEER values and annual units directly.

Published guides don't agree on the exact new ISEER cut-offs for each star, so this guide doesn't print a table. You don't need one: the ISEER number on the label is what matters. Within the same star band, the gap can be large. ClearBuy's [3-star vs 5-star comparison](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnXo9f-5nu8) makes this point: two 5-star ACs can differ meaningfully in ISEER, and the higher one will use less power.

## Turn the label into your running cost

Scale the label's annual units to your own hours:

**Your yearly units ≈ label units × your hours of use a year ÷ 1,600**

Down To Earth puts the typical 1.5-ton split AC at about 1,008 units a year for a 3-star model and 740 for a 5-star, on the label basis. For a household that runs the AC 8 hours a night for five months (about 1,200 hours):

- 3-star: 1,008 × 1,200 ÷ 1,600 ≈ **756 units a year**
- 5-star: 740 × 1,200 ÷ 1,600 ≈ **555 units a year**

That's about 200 units a year saved. It's an approximation: if you live somewhere hotter than average, the AC works harder during your hours and uses more than this, and the gap between models widens too.

**Which rate to use.** Most Indian tariffs charge more per unit as usage rises, so the units an AC adds are charged at your highest slab, not your average rate. In Tamil Nadu, for example, a household's last units cost ₹4.70, ₹6.30 or ₹8.40 depending on the slab it reaches in a two-month cycle. Use the rate of the top slab on your bill.

## 3-star or 5-star: payback by usage

Suppose the 5-star model costs ₹7,000 more; ClearBuy's video puts the typical gap at ₹6,000–8,000. The years to recover it:

| How you use the AC | Hours a year | Units saved a year | Payback at ₹4.70 | at ₹6.30 | at ₹8.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light: 4 h a day, 4 months | 480 | about 80 | 18.5 years | 13.8 years | 10.4 years |
| Moderate: 8 h a day, 5 months | 1,200 | about 200 | 7.4 years | 5.5 years | 4.1 years |
| Heavy: 10 h a day, 8 months | 2,400 | about 400 | 3.7 years | 2.8 years | 2.1 years |

*Our calculation, using Down To Earth's average label figures and a ₹7,000 price difference.*

So:

- **Light use, a moderate climate, or a guest room:** a 3-star is usually fine. The extra cost may never come back within the AC's life.
- **Nightly use in a hot city, or a high top slab:** a 5-star (or the highest ISEER you can afford) pays back in a few years and keeps saving after that.
- **In between:** compare the actual price difference and annual units of the two models you're considering, and redo the sum.

Paying more upfront to spend less every month is the same trade-off as comparing a loan's total cost rather than its EMI, which we covered in [reading a loan's APR](https://techpulzo.in/how-to-read-a-loan-apr-instead-of-just-the-emi).

## Tonnage: check the watts

A "ton" of cooling is a unit from the days of ice: 1 ton of refrigeration is 3.517 kilowatts of cooling. Brands round to 1, 1.5 or 2 tons, but the label and spec sheet give the actual cooling capacity in watts.

ClearBuy found that some 3-star "1.5-ton" models are smaller than the name suggests. One example it gives has a cooling capacity of 5,000 W, which is 5,000 ÷ 3,517 ≈ **1.42 tons**, while the brand's 5-star model is 5,280 W, a full 1.5 tons. On a 43°C afternoon, the smaller unit will struggle more in the same room. When comparing models, compare capacity in watts as well as ISEER.

## A buying checklist

1. Check the **label period** to see which star scale applies.
2. Compare **ISEER** values across shortlisted models, not just stars.
3. Compare **annual units** and scale them to your hours.
4. Check **cooling capacity in watts** against the tonnage claimed.
5. Work out the **payback** at your top slab rate.
6. Verify the model on BEE's star label portal (beestarlabel.com) if the label looks unusual.

You can keep the comparison in a simple sheet; our guide to [building a spreadsheet budget tracker](https://techpulzo.in/how-to-turn-a-spreadsheet-into-a-simple-budget-tracker) shows the basics.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good ISEER rating for an inverter AC?

Higher is better, and the bands for each star were raised in January 2026. Rather than aiming for a number, compare the ISEER of the models you're considering: an AC with ISEER 5.5 uses roughly 10% less electricity than one with 5.0 for the same cooling.

### How many units does a 1.5-ton AC use per hour?

It depends on the model, the outside temperature and your thermostat setting. On the label basis, a typical 3-star 1.5-ton split AC uses about 1,008 units over 1,600 hours, or about 0.63 units an hour; a 5-star about 0.46. In a hot city during peak summer, actual use is higher.

### Is a 5-star AC worth it over a 3-star?

If you run the AC 8 hours or more a day for several months, usually yes: the extra cost typically pays back in four to seven years. For a few hours a day in a short summer, the savings are small and the payback can exceed a decade.

### Why did my 5-star AC become 4-star?

It didn't change; the scale did. BEE raised the efficiency required for each star from 1 January 2026, so new labels award fewer stars for the same ISEER. Your AC uses the same electricity as before.

### What is the difference between EER and ISEER?

EER measures efficiency at one fixed outdoor temperature. ISEER measures it across a range of Indian temperatures (24°C to 43°C), weighted by how often each occurs, which gives a fairer picture for inverter ACs that run at part load most of the time.

## The number to remember

When you're in the shop, ask for the label and look past the stars: ISEER for efficiency, annual units for running cost, watts for real size. Then multiply the unit difference by your top slab rate and your hours. That calculation, not the star count, tells you which AC is cheaper for your home.
