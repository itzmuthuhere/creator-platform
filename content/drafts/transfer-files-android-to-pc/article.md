# How to transfer files from Android to a PC: Quick Share, cable or cloud

There are four easy ways to transfer files from an Android phone to a Windows PC, and each suits a different job. **Quick Share** sends a few photos or a document wirelessly in seconds. A **USB cable** is still the fastest, most reliable way to move a whole camera folder. **Phone Link** lets some phones drag and drop files straight onto the PC. And **cloud storage** works when the phone and PC aren't in the same place.

This guide shows how to use each, what each needs, and what to try when it doesn't work.

## Which method for which job

| Job | Best method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A few photos, a PDF, a video clip | Quick Share | Wireless, no account needed beyond Google's own setup, works both ways |
| Hundreds of photos or large videos | USB cable | Fastest and most reliable for big batches |
| Dragging files while using the phone on the PC | Phone Link (supported phones) | Drag and drop from the phone's screen into Windows |
| Files you'll need on several devices | Cloud (Google Drive, OneDrive) | Available anywhere, also a backup |

## Quick Share (wireless)

Quick Share is Google's built-in sharing for Android, and it works with Windows through a free app.

**What you need**, according to Google's [Quick Share for Windows page](https://www.android.com/better-together/quick-share-app/):

- A 64-bit Windows 10 or later PC (Windows 11 or later on ARM-based PCs).
- Wi-Fi and Bluetooth turned on on both devices, and the devices within about 5 metres.
- Android 6 or later; Quick Share is already on the phone.
- On Samsung PCs, use Samsung's own Quick Share app from the Microsoft Store instead.

**Set it up:**

1. On the PC, download **Quick Share** from android.com and install it.
2. On the phone, swipe down twice and find the **Quick Share** tile (tap Edit to add it if it's missing). Tech is Easy's [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=f_oU2v6m4aM) shows where it is.
3. Choose who can share with you. Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9286773?hl=en) lists options such as your contacts, or everyone for 10 minutes. If the phone and PC use the same Google account, selecting your own devices is simplest.

**Send phone to PC:** in Gallery or Files, select the items, tap **Share > Quick Share**, and choose your PC. Accept on the PC; the app shows where the file was saved (usually your Downloads folder).

**Send PC to phone:** in the Quick Share app, choose Select files (or drag files onto its window), then select your phone.

Google says transfers are end-to-end encrypted and the receiver has to confirm each one. Our explainer on [what end-to-end encryption means](https://techpulzo.in/what-end-to-end-encrypted-actually-means-for-an-app-you-use-daily) covers why that matters.

## USB cable

For large transfers, a cable is hard to beat.

1. Unlock the phone and connect it to the PC with a USB cable.
2. On the phone, tap the notification that says **Charging this device via USB** and choose **File transfer**, as [Android Help](https://support.google.com/android/answer/9064445?hl=en) describes.
3. Open **File Explorer** on the PC; the phone appears under This PC. Photos and videos are usually in **Internal storage > DCIM > Camera**.
4. Copy (rather than move) the files to the PC, check they open, then delete them from the phone if you want the space.
5. Eject the phone in Windows before unplugging.

If the phone only charges and never shows up, try another cable: Google notes that not all USB cables can transfer files.

## Phone Link

Microsoft's **Phone Link** app on Windows, paired with **Link to Windows** on Android, mirrors your phone on the PC. On some phones it also lets you move files.

- **Drag and drop:** [Microsoft says](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/apps/phonelink/seamlessly-transfer-content-between-your-devices) this works on select Samsung, HONOR, OPPO, ASUS, vivo and Xiaomi phones, with the phone and PC on the same Wi-Fi. You can move up to 100 files at a time, up to 512 MB each, but not folders.
- **Share menu:** on many Android 11+ phones, you can use **Send to PC** in the share menu; files arrive in **Downloads\Phone Link**, as [gHacks explains](https://www.ghacks.net/2024/08/20/how-to-send-files-between-windows-and-android-using-link-to-windows/). You'll need a Microsoft account to set it up.

Phone Link is most useful if you already use it for messages and notifications; for occasional transfers, Quick Share is quicker to set up.

## Cloud

Upload from the phone to Google Drive, OneDrive or Google Photos, and open the file on the PC in a browser or the sync app. It's slower for big files and uses your mobile data or cloud storage, but it works when the devices aren't together and leaves you with a backup. Our comparison of [Google Drive, OneDrive and iCloud](https://techpulzo.in/google-drive-vs-onedrive-vs-icloud-which-cloud-storage-to-use) helps choose.

## Fixes when it doesn't work

**Quick Share can't see the PC (or the phone):**

- Make sure Bluetooth and Wi-Fi are on on both, and the devices are close.
- On the PC, check Quick Share's own settings for who can share with you.
- Glitch Guru's [troubleshooting video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ewThGHLJEl8) suggests opening Windows Bluetooth settings and making sure the option that lets Bluetooth devices find the PC is turned on, then pairing the phone once in Bluetooth settings.
- Close and reopen Quick Share on the PC, or restart both devices.

**USB shows only charging:**

- Change the USB mode to **File transfer** from the notification each time you connect.
- Try a different cable and port; cheap charging cables often lack data wires.
- Unlock the phone; locked phones don't expose files.

**Phone Link drag and drop not available:** check your phone is one of the supported brands and on the same Wi-Fi as the PC, and use the share-menu method instead.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I transfer files from Android to PC without a cable?

Use Quick Share: install the Quick Share app on your Windows PC, turn on Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on both devices, then share the files from your phone and accept them on the PC. Phone Link and cloud storage are the other wireless options.

### Why does my PC only charge my phone and not show files?

The phone is in charging mode. Unlock it, tap the "Charging this device via USB" notification and choose File transfer. If that option doesn't appear, try another cable, since some cables only carry power.

### Does Quick Share work on all Windows PCs?

It needs a 64-bit version of Windows 10 or later, or Windows 11 or later on ARM-based PCs, with Wi-Fi and Bluetooth. Samsung PCs should use Samsung's own Quick Share app from the Microsoft Store.

### Where do Quick Share files go on my PC?

Usually to your Downloads folder; the Quick Share app shows the location after each transfer and lets you open the file from there.

### What is the fastest way to move many photos from Android to PC?

A USB cable in File transfer mode. Open the phone's DCIM\Camera folder in File Explorer and copy the files across. It's faster and more reliable than wireless methods for large batches.

## Pick by size

A handful of files: Quick Share. A whole camera roll: cable. Files you need everywhere: cloud. Set up Quick Share once on your PC and most everyday transfers take a few seconds.
