Rates checked on 29 September 2026 from each bank's own website, for deposits below ₹3 crore. Banks revise these often, sometimes several times a year, so confirm the rate on the bank's booking screen before you invest.

If you're putting money into a one-year FD, it barely matters which big bank you choose. Seven of the nine banks I checked pay between 6.25% and 6.35% for a year. Stretch the same money to three years and the gap widens to more than a full percentage point, which on ₹5 lakh is about ₹20,800 of extra interest. Where the difference hides is in the tenure, and in some oddly specific day counts.

I went through the official rate pages of SBI, HDFC Bank, ICICI Bank, Axis Bank, Kotak Mahindra Bank, Punjab National Bank, Bank of Baroda, IDFC FIRST Bank and AU Small Finance Bank. Each figure below links back to the page it came from.

The Day-Count Traps

The most useful thing I found wasn't in the headline rates. Several banks set their best rate to start one day after a round tenure, or reserve it for a specific odd number of days.

  • AU Small Finance Bank, 12 months vs 12 months and 1 day. A deposit of exactly 12 months falls in the "6 months 1 day to 12 months" bucket and earns 6.35%. Add a single day and it moves to the next bucket at 7.10%. On ₹5 lakh, that one day is worth roughly ₹3,750 of interest over the year.
  • HDFC Bank and ICICI Bank at 3 years. Both pay 6.45% for exactly 3 years and 6.50% for 3 years and 1 day onwards.
  • Special-tenure schemes at public sector banks. Bank of Baroda's 555-day "bob Golden Goal" deposit pays 6.75%, well above its 6.25% for one to three years. PNB pays 6.60% for exactly 444 days against 6.30% either side of it. SBI's 444-day "Amrit Vrishti" scheme pays 6.45%.

If a tenure a few days or weeks longer doesn't matter to you, look at the bank's full rate table rather than picking "1 year" or "3 years" from a dropdown. When booking online, set the tenure in days and check the rate shown on the confirmation screen before you pay.

Senior Citizen Rates

Eight of the nine banks add 0.50% for resident senior citizens (60 and above) on a 3-year deposit. IDFC FIRST adds only 0.25%, which narrows its lead: at 3 years its senior rate is 7.35% against AU's 7.90%. A few other details matter if you're booking for a parent:

  • SBI's 5-year senior rate of 7.05% includes an extra 0.50% under its "We-care" scheme.
  • Axis pays seniors 7.25% for 5 to 10 years, an extra 0.75% over its general rate for that tenure.
  • PNB and Bank of Baroda pay "super senior citizens" (80 and above) a further premium on top.
  • AU's senior rates are listed with an effective date of 10 June 2026, earlier than its general rates (1 September), and require an existing banking relationship with AU. Confirm the current senior rate when booking.

Is the Higher Rate at a Small Finance Bank Safe?

AU Small Finance Bank paid the highest 3-year rate in this comparison, and small finance banks usually do; they pay more to attract deposits. They're licensed and regulated by the RBI like other scheduled banks, and deposits in them are covered by DICGC deposit insurance.

That cover is up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank, and it includes both principal and interest. So the practical rule is simple: keep what you hold in any one bank, across all its accounts and FDs in your name, within ₹5 lakh including the interest it will earn. If you're investing more, splitting across two or three banks keeps every rupee inside the insured limit, and it costs nothing.

Before You Book

  • Premature withdrawal usually costs 0.5% to 1%. HDFC takes 1% off the rate if you break a deposit early; ICICI takes 0.5% under one year and 1% after. You're also paid the rate for the period the money actually stayed, not the rate you booked. Bank of Baroda is a useful exception: it charges no penalty on deposits up to ₹5 lakh that have stayed at least 12 months. Some high-rate products are "non-callable", meaning you can't withdraw early at all; check which kind you're booking.
  • Interest is taxable at your slab rate, and banks deduct TDS once your FD interest for the year crosses the threshold. A 7.40% FD in the 30% bracket earns about 5.2% after tax.
  • Rates here are for deposits below ₹3 crore. Larger deposits have separate "bulk" rates.

Compare the Tenure, Then the Bank

For a one-year deposit, choose the bank that's most convenient; the rate differences are too small to matter much. For two to three years, the spread is wide enough to shop around: IDFC FIRST and AU led this comparison, with Kotak ahead among the large private banks at 2 years. For any tenure, check whether a specific day count beats the round number, because on this list that single check was worth more than switching banks.
