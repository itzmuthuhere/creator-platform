# Credit card EMI vs personal loan: which is cheaper for a big bill?

You're looking at a credit card bill you can't clear in full. Two quick fixes are on offer: convert it to EMIs on the card, or take a personal loan and pay the card off. Both beat carrying the balance at the card's revolving rate. Which is cheaper depends on the rate you're offered, the fees, the GST, and the size of the amount.

This guide compares the two with real 2026 pricing from one bank's own disclosures, shows where each one wins, and lists the costs that don't appear in the headline rate.

## First: either is far better than revolving

If you pay only part of a card bill, the rest is charged interest at the card's revolving rate, which HDFC Bank's [card terms](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/content/dam/hdfcbankpws/in/en/personal-banking/discover-products/cards/credit-cards/personal-mitc/mitc-in-english.pdf) set at 3.75% a month (45% a year) on most of its cards, plus GST, and you lose the interest-free period on new purchases. Say you owe ₹1.5 lakh and pay ₹13,766 a month for a year: at 3.75% a month plus GST, you'd still owe about ₹40,000 after 12 payments, having paid about ₹55,000 in interest and GST. An EMI or a personal loan at the same monthly payment clears the whole amount.

So the real question is which instalment route costs less.

## What one bank actually charges

HDFC Bank publishes the rates it actually gave customers, which makes it a useful benchmark:

| | Card EMI (SmartEMI) | Personal loan |
|---|---|---|
| Rate range | 10.80%–24.96% IRR | 9.97%–24.06% IRR (Apr–Jun 2026) |
| **Average rate charged** | **18.20%** | **10.89%** |
| Average APR (including fees) | 21.57% | 11.18% |
| Processing fee | Up to about ₹1,000 + GST | Up to ₹6,500 + GST |
| Tenure | 6–48 months | Longer |
| Prepaying | 3% of the outstanding principal to pre-close | 2–4% on part payments |

*Sources: HDFC Bank's [SmartEMI page](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/smartemi), card terms (September 2026) and [personal loan rates and charges](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/personal-loan/interest-rates-and-charges). HDFC's pages quote SmartEMI fees between ₹249 and ₹999 depending on the product, so the comparison below uses ₹999.*

The ranges overlap, but the averages tell the story: card EMIs at this bank cost about seven percentage points more on average. Rates depend heavily on your profile, so your own offer is what matters.

**Your credit score sets the card EMI rate.** SBI Card's [Flexipay terms](https://www.sbicard.com/sbi-card-en/assets/docs/pdf/tnc/flexipay-emi-low-roi-tnc-15june26to14Jul26.pdf) from June 2026 tie the rate to your bureau score:

| Bureau score | SBI Card Flexipay rate |
|---|---|
| Above 780 | 9.75%–15% |
| 701–780 | 16%–19% |
| 700 or below | 21%–24% |

The processing fee is 1% of the amount or ₹2,000, whichever is less (waived for some tenures during offers). With a strong score, a card EMI can be priced like a personal loan; with a weak one, it rarely is.

## Costs that hide outside the rate

**GST on card EMIs.** HDFC's SmartEMI page says your monthly repayment is the EMI **plus GST**, so 18% is added on top of the interest. The personal loan's APR above already includes its fees.

**Your card limit is blocked.** The full EMI amount is blocked against your credit limit until it's repaid, HDFC notes. On a ₹2 lakh limit, a ₹1.5 lakh EMI leaves you ₹50,000 to spend, and shows as 75% utilisation on your credit report. A personal loan used to clear the card frees the limit.

**Reward points.** HDFC's card terms say reward points earned on a purchase are reversed if you convert it to SmartEMI.

**Less paperwork, fewer protections.** RBI rules require lenders to give you a **Key Facts Statement** with the APR before most retail loans. The rules exclude credit card receivables, [EMI Calculator's summary notes](https://emicalculator.net/key-facts-statement-from-october-1-2024-all-loan-charges-must-be-disclosed-transparently/), so for a card EMI you'll need to check the rate and fees yourself.

## The numbers: ₹1.5 lakh over 12 months

| Option | EMI | Interest | Fee + GST | **Total cost** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Card EMI at 18.2% (HDFC's average) | ₹13,766 | ₹15,195 | ₹3,914 | **₹19,109** |
| Card EMI at a 12% offer | ₹13,327 | ₹9,928 | ₹2,966 | **₹12,894** |
| Personal loan at 10.89% (HDFC's average), ₹3,000 fee | ₹13,250 | ₹8,995 | ₹3,540 | **₹12,535** |
| Personal loan at 16%, ₹3,000 fee | ₹13,610 | ₹13,316 | ₹3,540 | **₹16,856** |

*GST at 18% on the card EMI's interest and fee, and on the personal loan's fee.*

At typical rates the personal loan wins. A good card EMI offer comes close, and a poorly priced personal loan loses to it.

## When the card EMI wins: small amounts and short terms

Fees matter more than the rate when the amount is small. For **₹40,000 over six months**:

| Option | Total cost |
|---|---|
| Card EMI at 12% (₹999 fee) | ₹2,845 |
| Card EMI at 18.2% | ₹3,716 |
| Personal loan at 10.89% (₹3,000 fee) | ₹4,820 |

A personal loan's fixed processing fee eats the rate advantage. For smaller bills, a reasonably priced card EMI, especially one with a low or waived fee, is usually cheaper and much quicker.

BankBazaar's [comparison video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WM5SmG0nNu8) makes a related practical point: card loans are pre-approved and usually come through within a day, while personal loans take a few days of paperwork.

## How to decide

1. **Get both actual offers.** Check the card EMI rate and fee in your card app for that specific amount, and a personal loan quote from your bank, which must come with a Key Facts Statement showing the APR.
2. **Compare total cost**, not the EMI: interest + fees + GST.
3. **For amounts above about ₹1 lakh over a year or more,** the personal loan usually wins unless your card offer is close to the loan rate.
4. **For small amounts or a few months,** the card EMI usually wins.
5. **Check prepayment terms** if you might clear it early. HDFC charges 3% of the outstanding principal to pre-close a SmartEMI, and 2–4% on personal loan part-payments.
6. **Fix the cause.** If this keeps happening, the problem is the spending, not the financing. Our guide to [credit card vs debit card for everyday spending](https://techpulzo.in/credit-card-vs-debit-card-for-everyday-spending-what-actually-saves-you-money) may help.

Money9's [explainer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DKPnLGNqi8s) adds that repaying either on time builds your credit history. Missing payments on either hurts your score the same way; see our [credit score guide](https://techpulzo.in/credit-score-in-india-how-it-works-and-how-to-fix-it).

## Frequently asked questions

### Is credit card EMI cheaper than a personal loan?

Usually not for larger amounts. HDFC Bank's published averages are 18.2% for card EMIs against 10.89% for personal loans, and card EMIs also attract GST on the interest. For small amounts over a few months, a card EMI can be cheaper because personal loans carry higher processing fees.

### Is GST charged on credit card EMI?

Yes. Banks add GST at 18% to the interest on card EMIs; HDFC's SmartEMI page says each repayment is the EMI plus GST. GST is also charged on processing fees for both card EMIs and personal loans.

### Does a credit card EMI affect my credit limit?

Yes. The full EMI amount is blocked against your credit limit and released as you repay, which reduces your available limit and raises your credit utilisation.

### Can I use a personal loan to pay off my credit card bill?

Yes, and it's often cheaper than a card EMI for larger balances. Make sure the loan's total cost, including the processing fee, is lower, and don't run the card balance up again afterwards.

### What is better than both?

Paying the bill in full. If that's impossible this month, either option is far cheaper than paying the minimum due and carrying the rest at the card's revolving rate.

## The short version

Both options beat revolving. For a large bill over a year or more, a personal loan is usually cheaper, as long as you compare the APR. For a small amount over a few months, a card EMI often wins because there's no big processing fee. Either way, compare total cost including GST, and remember a card EMI ties up your limit.
