# HRA exemption calculation for 2026-27, worked out with real numbers

House Rent Allowance (HRA) is the part of your salary meant for rent, and under the old tax regime some or all of it can be tax-free. The HRA exemption calculation itself is short: three numbers, take the smallest. What trips people up is which "salary" to use, what happens when you rent for only part of the year, and the new paperwork that came in with the Income-tax Rules 2026.

This guide works through the formula with examples, shows when the 2026 change to the metro-city list matters, and covers the documents your employer will now ask for. HRA isn't available in the new regime; if you haven't chosen yet, decide that first.

## The formula

The exempt part of your HRA is the **lowest** of:

1. **The HRA you received.**
2. **50% of salary** if you live in one of the eight listed cities, **40%** anywhere else.
3. **Rent paid minus 10% of salary.**

Whatever HRA is left over is taxed with the rest of your salary.

**"Salary" here is not your gross pay.** It means basic pay plus dearness allowance (DA) that counts for retirement benefits, plus any commission fixed as a percentage of turnover, as [ClearTax's HRA guide](https://cleartax.in/s/hra-house-rent-allowance) and [JM Financial's 2026 summary](https://www.jmfinancialservices.in/blogs-and-articles/hra-exemption-2026-new-rules-from-april-1) both define it. For most private-sector employees that is simply basic pay.

## The 2026 change: eight cities at 50%

From 1 April 2026, the Income-tax Rules 2026 added **Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad** to Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai in the 50% group, according to [KPMG's flash alert](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-081.html) on the new rules.

That only changes your exemption when the second number is the smallest. In practice the rent line is often smaller, as the first example shows.

## Example 1: when the city change doesn't matter

Anita works in Bengaluru. Her basic pay is ₹50,000 a month, her HRA ₹20,000, and she pays ₹22,000 rent. For the year:

| Test | Amount |
|---|---|
| HRA received (₹20,000 × 12) | ₹2,40,000 |
| 50% of salary (40% before April 2026) | ₹3,00,000 (was ₹2,40,000) |
| Rent − 10% of salary (₹2,64,000 − ₹60,000) | **₹2,04,000** |

Her exemption is **₹2,04,000** under both the old 40% and the new 50% rule; ₹36,000 of her HRA stays taxable. The rent line limits her, so the new city status changes nothing.

**A quick way to see where you stand.** The rent line stops being the limit once your rent is at least **60% of basic** in a 50% city (50% of basic elsewhere), and only then does the city percentage start to matter. Anita's rent is 44% of her basic. Your full HRA becomes tax-free only when it's also no more than both of the other numbers.

## Example 2: renting for part of the year

Karthik lived with his parents from April to September and rented a flat at ₹25,000 a month from October. Basic ₹50,000, HRA ₹20,000 a month, in Chennai.

- **Worked out over the whole year:** rent ₹1,50,000 minus 10% of the full year's salary (₹60,000) = ₹90,000.
- **Worked out over the six months he actually rented:** HRA ₹1,20,000; 50% of salary ₹1,50,000; rent ₹1,50,000 minus 10% of six months' salary (₹30,000) = ₹1,20,000. Exemption **₹1,20,000**.

The calculation belongs to the period you live in rented accommodation. Doing it for the whole year subtracts 10% of salary for months you paid no rent, and here understates the exemption by ₹30,000. HR Payroll & Excel Academy's [calculator video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zIzjzAYKb9Y) makes the same point: work it out month by month whenever rent, salary or city changes during the year.

## What your employer will ask for in 2026

The Income-tax Rules 2026 replaced Form 12BB, the declaration you give your employer for HRA and other deductions, with **Form 124**. KPMG notes that HRA claims now require you to **disclose your relationship with the landlord**, if any, with supporting documents attached.

Keep these ready:

- **Rent receipts** or a rental agreement for the months you claim.
- **Proof of payment**, ideally bank transfers or UPI rather than cash.
- **The landlord's PAN** if the rent is more than ₹1 lakh a year (about ₹8,333 a month), as JM Financial lists.
- **The relationship**, if the landlord is a relative.

Taxation With Archit's [video on the new rules](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h9n5UI6m_84) ties these changes to the tax department's recent notices over inflated or invented HRA claims, and the disclosure fields make that kind of claim easier to check.

## Special cases

- **Paying rent to parents.** Allowed if it's genuine: a rental agreement, payments through the bank, and the parent showing the rent as income in their own return, as ClearTax describes. You now have to declare the relationship.
- **Paying rent to your spouse.** ClearTax lists this as not allowed.
- **You own a house too.** You can still claim HRA if you live in rented accommodation, for example because your own house is in another city. ClearTax notes that HRA and the home-loan interest deduction can then both be claimed.
- **Living in your own house.** No HRA exemption; the allowance is fully taxable.
- **No HRA in your salary** (or self-employed). A separate rent deduction applies under the old regime: the smallest of ₹5,000 a month, 25% of total income, or rent minus 10% of total income. [Tax Garden's guide](https://taxgarden.in/blog/section-80gg-rent-deduction-without-hra-india-ay-2026-27) adds that Form 10BA must be filed first. Someone with ₹7 lakh of income paying ₹15,000 a month would get the full ₹60,000.

## Where HRA fits in the bigger decision

HRA is often the largest single item that keeps people in the old regime. At most salaries the old regime needs several lakh rupees of exemptions and deductions in total to beat the new one, so a modest HRA exemption on its own rarely tips it. Run the comparison with your real HRA figure, not the HRA on your payslip, since only the exempt part counts. ## Frequently asked questions

### How is HRA exemption calculated?

It's the lowest of three amounts: the HRA you received, 50% of salary (40% outside the eight listed cities), and rent paid minus 10% of salary. Salary means basic pay plus retirement-linked DA and fixed commission. The rest of the HRA is taxable.

### Which cities get the 50% HRA limit from April 2026?

Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai, plus Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad, which were added by the Income-tax Rules 2026. Every other city uses 40%.

### Can I claim HRA in the new tax regime?

No. HRA exemption is available only under the old regime. In the new regime the full allowance is taxable.

### Do I need my landlord's PAN for HRA?

Yes, if the rent you pay is more than ₹1 lakh in the year. If you rent from a relative, you also need to declare the relationship in Form 124 under the 2026 rules.

### What if I paid rent for only part of the year?

Calculate the exemption only for the months you rented, using the salary and HRA for those months. Calculating over the full year subtracts 10% of salary for months without rent and can understate the exemption.

## The short version

Use basic plus DA as "salary", take the smallest of the three amounts, and work month by month if anything changed during the year. Pay rent through the bank, keep receipts, get the landlord's PAN above ₹1 lakh a year, and declare any family relationship in Form 124.
