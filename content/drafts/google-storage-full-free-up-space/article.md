# Google storage full? What's using your 15 GB and how to free it

When your Google storage is full, the warning matters more than it looks: Gmail stops receiving email, and messages sent to you bounce back to the sender. Your free 15 GB is shared by Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos, plus a few things most people don't realise count, such as WhatsApp backups and Android phone backups. The good news is that a few targeted clean-ups usually free several gigabytes in minutes.

This guide shows how to see what's using the space, what to delete first, how to stop it filling again, and when paying for more is the sensible choice.

## What happens when it's full

According to [Google One Help](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/6374270?hl=en), when you hit the limit:

- **Gmail:** you can't send or receive email; incoming messages go back to the sender.
- **Drive:** you can't upload or create files.
- **Photos:** new photos and videos stop backing up.

If you stay over the limit for **two years**, Google may delete your content across Gmail, Drive and Photos. Long before that, though, missed emails (bank alerts, OTP emails, job replies) are the real cost.

## Step 1: find what's using it

Open the **Google One storage manager** at one.google.com/storage/management (also reachable from the storage link in Drive, Gmail or Photos). It shows your total use split by Gmail, Drive and Photos, and a "clean up" section with suggestions such as large files, large email attachments and items in the trash. Bijay Tech Skills' [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_lUlMBUp-f4) shows these screens on a phone.

Check the split before deleting anything. If Photos is 11 of your 15 GB, clearing Gmail won't solve the problem.

## What counts towards your 15 GB

From Google's page on [how storage works](https://support.google.com/googleone/answer/9312312?hl=en):

| Counts | Doesn't count |
|---|---|
| Photos and videos in original quality | Photos in Storage saver (high quality) backed up before 1 June 2021 |
| Photos and videos in Storage saver or Express quality backed up after 1 June 2021 | Files other people shared with you ("Shared with me") |
| Gmail messages and attachments, including Spam and Trash | Docs, Sheets and Slides files last edited before 1 June 2021 |
| Everything in Google Drive, including its trash | |
| Google Docs, Sheets, Slides and similar files edited after 1 June 2021 | |
| Google Meet recordings | |
| WhatsApp backups on Android | |
| Android device backups (messages, call logs, settings, app data) | |

The last two surprise people. WhatsApp backups on Android [started counting in 2024](https://9to5google.com/2023/11/14/whatsapp-backup-google-account-storage/), and a chat backup with years of videos can run to several gigabytes. Old phone backups also linger after you've changed phones.

## Step 2: clean up in this order

Start with the changes that free the most space for the least effort.

1. **Empty the bins.** Gmail's Trash and Spam, Drive's Trash, and Photos' Bin all count until emptied. Google calls this the quickest win.
2. **Delete old phone backups.** In the storage manager, look for device backups from phones you no longer use and delete them.
3. **Shrink the WhatsApp backup.** In WhatsApp on Android, go to Settings > Chats > Chat backup and turn off **Include videos**; then delete large media you don't need from chats.
4. **Find big emails.** In Gmail's search box, type `has:attachment larger:10M` to list emails with attachments over 10 MB. Download what you need, then delete. You can also try `older_than:3y` to find old mail in bulk, and clear promotional folders.
5. **Sort Drive by size.** In Drive, open **Storage** to see files ranked by size. Remove duplicates (files named "Copy of…" or ending "(1)") and old videos.
6. **Clear large photos and videos.** In Photos, review large videos first; one minute of HD video can take as much space as dozens of photos. Download anything you want to keep to a computer or drive before deleting.

## Step 3: stop it filling again

A rough illustration of why storage fills so fast: if your phone backs up about 150 photos and 20 minutes of HD video a month to Google Photos, that's around 3 GB a month, and videos make up roughly 85% of it. At that rate, 12 GB of free space lasts about four months.

*Our estimate, assuming about 3 MB per photo and 130 MB per minute of 1080p video.*

So:

- **Set Photos to back up in Storage saver** rather than original quality if you don't need full resolution.
- **Turn off backup for folders you don't need,** such as WhatsApp images or screenshots, in Photos' backup settings.
- **Keep WhatsApp videos out of the cloud backup.**
- **Unsubscribe** from newsletters that send heavy attachments.

If you've cleaned up and you're still near the limit, buying storage is often cheaper than the time spent deleting. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why is my Google storage full when I hardly use Drive?

Because the 15 GB is shared. Gmail (including Spam and Trash), Google Photos, WhatsApp backups on Android and Android phone backups all count. Open the storage manager at one.google.com/storage/management to see which service is using the space.

### What happens if my Google storage stays full?

You can't receive or send Gmail, upload to Drive or back up to Photos. If you stay over the limit for two years, Google may delete your content across Gmail, Drive and Photos.

### Does emptying Gmail's trash free up storage?

Yes. Emails in Trash and Spam count towards your storage until they're permanently deleted. Emptying both folders, along with Drive's trash and the Photos bin, is the quickest way to recover space.

### Do WhatsApp backups use my Google storage?

Yes, on Android. WhatsApp backups started counting towards your Google storage during 2024. Turning off "Include videos" in WhatsApp's chat backup settings keeps the backup much smaller.

### How do I find large emails in Gmail?

Search `has:attachment larger:10M` in Gmail to list emails with attachments bigger than 10 MB. Change the number to find smaller or larger ones, download what you need and delete the rest.

## The five-minute version

Open the storage manager, empty every bin, delete old phone backups, run `has:attachment larger:10M` in Gmail, and turn off WhatsApp video backups. If you're still over, compare the cost of a small plan with the time you'd spend deleting.
