# Form 26AS vs AIS vs Form 16: what each shows and how to check them

Three documents describe your income for the tax year, and they're made by three different parties. **Form 16** comes from your employer and covers only your salary. **Form 26AS** comes from the tax department's TDS system and shows tax deducted or collected against your PAN. **AIS** (Annual Information Statement) is the department's wide view: interest, dividends, share and mutual fund sales, large transactions, and the TDS as well.

When the numbers in your return don't line up with these documents, the department's systems notice. Knowing what each document is for, and checking them against each other before you file, is the simplest way to avoid a mismatch notice.

## The three documents at a glance

| | Form 16 | Form 26AS | AIS (with TIS) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who produces it | Your employer | Tax department (TRACES), from deductors' TDS returns | Tax department, from banks, brokers, registrars, employers and others |
| What it covers | Salary, exemptions and deductions your employer allowed, TDS on salary | TDS and TCS against your PAN | TDS/TCS, interest, dividends, securities and fund transactions, property deals, taxes paid, refunds, other information |
| When you get it | By 15 June after the year | Updated as deductors file TDS returns | Updated through the year |
| What you do with it | Fill the salary part of your return | Confirm the TDS you claim was actually deposited | Check every income item is in your return; give feedback on wrong entries |

## Form 16: your employer's salary certificate

Form 16 has two parts. As a [guide for employers](https://futurexsolutions.com/form-16-fy-2025-26-employer-guide/) explains, **Part A** is generated on the government's TRACES portal and lists your employer's TAN, your PAN and the TDS deducted and deposited each quarter; it can't be edited by the employer. **Part B** is prepared by your employer and shows the salary breakdown, the exemptions and deductions it accepted, and the tax it calculated.

Form 16 tells you nothing about income outside your job: not your bank interest, dividends or capital gains. Relying on it alone is the most common way people under-report.

If you changed jobs, you'll have two Form 16s. Add both salaries, and claim the standard deduction only once, a point CA Raj Mehta stresses in Fisdom's reconciliation video.

## Form 26AS: your tax credit statement

Since assessment year 2023-24, [Form 26AS shows only TDS and TCS](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/ais-faq), the tax deducted or collected against your PAN, as reported by whoever deducted it. Everything else moved to AIS.

Use 26AS for one job: confirming that tax deducted from your salary, interest or other payments actually reached the government against your PAN. Only TDS that appears here can be claimed as a credit without trouble.

## AIS and TIS: the department's full picture

The [department's AIS FAQ](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/ais-faq) describes AIS as a "comprehensive view of information for a taxpayer". Its Part B covers TDS/TCS, statements of financial transactions reported by banks and others (SFT), taxes you paid, demands and refunds, and other information.

**TIS** (Taxpayer Information Summary) sits inside AIS and totals it by category. For each category it shows a *processed* value (after the system removes duplicates) and a value *accepted by you or confirmed by the source*, after any feedback. That accepted value is what pre-fills your return.

Two things about AIS catch people out:

- **It shows sale values, not profits.** Sell ₹3 lakh of mutual funds and AIS shows ₹3 lakh. You're taxed only on the gain, which you work out from your purchase cost. Fisdom's video flags this as a common panic.
- **It includes accounts you've forgotten.** An old savings account or a small FD from years ago still shows up, interest and all.

You'll find AIS by logging in to the e-filing portal and choosing **Annual Information Statement (AIS)** on the dashboard. It can be downloaded as a PDF, JSON or CSV file.

## How they connect

All three documents are keyed to your PAN, so a wrong PAN with a bank or employer is a common reason income goes missing or lands on someone else's record. (If you've lost the card itself, here's [how to get a duplicate PAN card](https://techpulzo.in/how-to-get-a-duplicate-pan-card-online).)

The department's systems compare your return with AIS (through the TIS figures that pre-fill it) and your TDS claims with 26AS. Form 16 is your employer's version of the salary part. So:

- **Salary** in your return should match Form 16 (all of them, if more than one).
- **TDS you claim** should match 26AS.
- **Every other income** should match AIS, unless AIS is wrong, in which case you give feedback.

## A worked reconciliation

Priya changed jobs in November. Here's what her documents show, and what she does about each difference:

| Check | What she finds | What she does |
|---|---|---|
| Form 16s vs her return | Two Form 16s: ₹7.2 lakh and ₹4.8 lakh salary | Enters both salaries; claims the standard deduction once |
| Form 16 Part A vs 26AS | Old employer's Form 16 shows ₹38,000 TDS; 26AS shows ₹26,000 | Asks the old employer to correct its TDS return; until it does, claims only ₹26,000 |
| AIS vs her records: savings interest | AIS shows ₹6,200 from a forgotten account | Adds it to "income from other sources" |
| AIS vs her records: FD interest | AIS shows ₹38,500; matches her bank certificate | Reports it |
| AIS: mutual fund sale | ₹3,00,000 sale value | Works out the gain from her purchase cost and reports that |
| AIS: dividend | Same ₹2,400 dividend listed twice | Gives AIS feedback ("information is duplicate"); reports ₹2,400 once |

*Illustrative figures.*

The TDS gap is the one that costs money: until the old employer's correction reaches 26AS, the extra ₹12,000 can't be claimed. If it's corrected later, a revised return can claim it.

## When AIS is wrong: feedback, and why it isn't enough

You can give feedback on any AIS entry. Fisdom's video lists the options as: information is correct; income is not taxable; information is not fully correct; information relates to another PAN or year; information is duplicate; the transfer isn't a sale; and information is denied. The AIS then shows both the reported value and your modified value. CA Anita Agrawal's advice is simple: don't add a wrong AIS entry to your return just because it's there; give feedback instead.

The opposite mistake is just as common, and CA Raj Mehta calls it the biggest one: **feedback corrects the department's data, not your return.** If AIS correctly shows income you left out, marking it "correct" in AIS does nothing for the return you already filed. You still need to add the income, by revising the return or, if you haven't filed yet, by including it.

In short:

- **AIS is wrong** → give feedback, and report the correct figure in your return.
- **Your return is wrong** → fix the return (revise it, or include the income before filing).

## What changes next year

From April 2026, salary TDS certificates move to the new Income-tax Rules. [Form 130 replaces Form 16](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/form-130-tds-certificate-salary-replacing-form-16.html) for salary earned in tax year 2026-27, with three parts (basic details, a quarterly TDS summary, and a detailed computation) and the same 15 June deadline. So next June, expect a Form 130 from your employer instead of a Form 16. The way to check it doesn't change: match its TDS with your tax credit statement, and everything else with AIS.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between Form 26AS and AIS?

Form 26AS is a tax credit statement: since assessment year 2023-24 it shows only TDS and TCS against your PAN. AIS is much broader. It includes that TDS plus interest, dividends, securities and mutual fund transactions, large financial transactions, taxes paid and refunds. Use 26AS to check your TDS credits, and AIS to check that every income is in your return.

### What is TIS in AIS?

TIS (Taxpayer Information Summary) is a category-wise summary of your AIS. It shows the value the system processed and the value after your feedback or the source's confirmation. That second figure is what pre-fills your income tax return, which is why giving feedback on wrong AIS entries before filing is useful.

### Should I report income exactly as AIS shows it?

Report the correct income. Where AIS is right, include it. Where AIS is wrong (a duplicate, another person's transaction, a sale shown instead of the gain), give feedback in AIS and report the correct figure in your return. Don't copy AIS blindly, and don't ignore it either.

### Why is TDS in my Form 16 more than in 26AS?

Usually because your employer deducted the TDS but filed its TDS return late or with an error, so it hasn't reached your PAN in 26AS. Ask the employer to correct its TDS return. Claim only what 26AS shows; once the correction appears, you can revise your return to claim the rest.

### Will I still get Form 16 next year?

For salary earned up to March 2026, yes, by 15 June 2026. For salary earned from April 2026, employers issue Form 130 instead, under the new Income-tax Rules. It serves the same purpose with a slightly different layout.

## Three documents, three questions

Form 16 answers "what did my employer pay me and deduct?". Form 26AS answers "did that tax reach the government?". AIS answers "what does the department know about my money?". Check all three before you file, add whatever you missed, and give feedback on what's wrong. If you hold old fixed deposits you've forgotten, AIS will show their interest, and our explainer on [FDs and recurring deposits](https://techpulzo.in/the-real-difference-between-a-fixed-deposit-and-a-recurring-deposit) covers how that interest builds up.
