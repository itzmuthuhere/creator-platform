# How to check and pay traffic challans online, and what changed in 2026

To check a challan online, use the government's eChallan portal: you can look up pending traffic challans on any vehicle or driving licence there, and pay them with no extra charge. Two things trip people up in 2026. Many states have moved to a newer "NextGen" portal, so a challan you've been told about may show "no challan found" on the old one. And fake challan messages with payment links have become common. The rules also changed this year: you now have 45 days to pay or contest a challan, and five violations in a year can cost you your licence.

## Where to check

| Portal | Use it for |
|---|---|
| [echallan.parivahan.gov.in](https://echallan.parivahan.gov.in/) | The main MoRTH eChallan site; links to the right service for your state |
| [NextGen eChallan](https://echallan.parivahan.nic.in/challan/challan-services) (echallan.parivahan.nic.in) | The newer portal now used by many states, including Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal |
| Delhi Traffic Police website | Challans and notices issued in Delhi |
| The mParivahan app | Checking challans and documents on your phone |

Both Parivahan addresses end in **gov.in** or **nic.in**. That's how to tell them apart from lookalikes. Some search results and videos point to sites at a bare numeric address (a string of numbers instead of a name); Aman Tech Fix's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3PEllw2o1QY) specifically warns against opening one of these, and you shouldn't enter details or pay on any site that isn't an official domain.

## Checking and paying, step by step

On the NextGen portal:

1. Open **echallan.parivahan.nic.in** and choose **Pay Challan** or **Check Pending Challan Status**.
2. Search by **challan number**, **vehicle number** or **driving licence number**. For a vehicle or DL search, you'll verify with an OTP sent to the mobile number registered with that vehicle or licence.
3. Review each challan: the offence, date, place and amount. Many camera-based challans include a photo.
4. Pay online by UPI, card or net banking.
5. **Download the payment receipt** and keep it. The portal has a separate option to download it again later.

If a payment fails but money left your account, use **Check Pending Transaction** before paying again. The main portal links to the transaction-status pages for Parivahan's payment gateways.

**Avoid paying through private apps.** Car and insurance apps often show your challans, which is convenient, but Aman Tech Fix points out that paying through them can add a convenience fee. The government portal charges only the challan amount.

## "No challan found"? Why, and what to do

If you got an SMS about a challan but the portal says nothing is pending:

- **You're on the wrong portal.** If your state has moved to NextGen, search on echallan.parivahan.nic.in instead.
- **It was issued in Delhi.** Check the Delhi Traffic Police site.
- **The challan was sent to court.** Older unpaid challans may have gone to a virtual court, where they're paid separately.
- **The SMS was fake.** See the warning signs below.

## The 2026 rules: 45 days to pay or contest

The Central Motor Vehicles (Third Amendment) Rules, 2026, which took effect on 20 January 2026 according to [Pune Pulse](https://www.mypunepulse.com/five-traffic-offences-a-year-to-attract-strict-action-government-tightens-e-challan-rules/), set a clear timeline:

| Stage | Time limit |
|---|---|
| Challan delivered to you | Within 3 days if electronic, 15 days if physical |
| Pay it, or contest it on the portal with evidence | Within 45 days of issue |
| If you don't contest | It's treated as accepted; pay within the following 30 days |
| If your contest is rejected | Pay within 30 days, or go to court after depositing 50% of the fine |
| Five or more violations in a calendar year | You can be treated as a serious offender, with action against your licence |

If a challan stays unpaid, the vehicle or licence can be flagged as **"not to be transacted"** in the transport database, which blocks services such as renewals, ownership transfer and address changes, as [MotorBeam explains](https://www.motorbeam.com/delhi-to-introduce-fully-digital-time-bound-traffic-challan-system/). States are rolling the system out at different speeds: Chandigarh has started accepting online contests, while reports in September 2026 said Delhi had put its rollout on hold after concerns raised by advocates. Check what your state's portal offers.

## Contesting a challan

If you think a challan is wrong (wrong vehicle, misread number plate, an offence you didn't commit):

1. Don't pay it; paying usually counts as accepting it.
2. Within 45 days, use the portal's contest or grievance option, and upload evidence: photos, a location record, a document showing the vehicle was elsewhere, or proof the number plate was cloned.
3. Track the decision on the portal. According to [The Tribune's report](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/chandigarh/now-contest-camera-based-traffic-challans-in-chandigarh/) on Chandigarh's system, if the authority rejects your contest you can pay or take it to court by depositing half the fine.

## Fake challan messages

Scammers send SMS or WhatsApp messages saying you owe a traffic fine, with a link to "pay now". Advocate Prayansh's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-4wYvDGAcMQ) explains how these lead straight to the scammer's account. The eChallan portal itself warns that it never asks for your password, OTP or payment details by call, email, message or link.

How to tell:

- **The link isn't a gov.in or nic.in address**, or it's a shortened link.
- **It pressures you:** "pay today or your licence will be cancelled."
- **It asks for card details or an OTP** on a page you reached from the message.
- **The challan number doesn't show up** when you search it on the official portal yourself.

Never pay from the link. Open the official portal yourself, search the challan number or your vehicle number, and pay only if it's there. Report fake messages at cybercrime.gov.in or by calling 1930. ## Frequently asked questions

### How can I check if my vehicle has a challan?

Go to the official eChallan portal (echallan.parivahan.gov.in, or the NextGen portal at echallan.parivahan.nic.in if your state uses it) and search by vehicle number, driving licence number or challan number. You'll verify with an OTP sent to the registered mobile number. Delhi challans are on the Delhi Traffic Police website.

### Why does the portal say "no challan found" when I got an SMS?

Usually because your state now uses the NextGen eChallan portal, so search there instead. It can also mean the challan went to court, was issued in Delhi, or that the SMS was fake. Never pay through a link in the message.

### How many days do I have to pay a traffic challan?

Under the 2026 rules, you have 45 days from issue to pay or contest it on the portal. If you don't contest, it's treated as accepted and must be paid within the next 30 days. Unpaid challans can block licence and registration services.

### Can I contest a traffic challan online?

Yes, where your state has enabled it. Within 45 days, raise a contest or grievance on the portal with supporting evidence. If it's rejected, you can pay within 30 days or approach the court after depositing 50% of the fine.

### Is it safe to pay challans through apps like Paytm or car apps?

Some apps show challans correctly, but they may add a convenience fee, and lookalike apps exist. The safest route is the official eChallan portal or the mParivahan app, where you pay only the challan amount.

## The habit that helps

Check your vehicle number on the official portal every few months, even without an SMS. That way you'll catch a camera challan within its 45-day window, and you'll know immediately that any message demanding payment for something that isn't listed is fake.
