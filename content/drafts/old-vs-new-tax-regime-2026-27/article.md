# Old vs new tax regime for 2026-27: how to choose for your salary

The old vs new tax regime decision comes down to one number: the total of your deductions and exemptions. Add up everything you can claim under the old regime (HRA, home loan interest, the ₹1.5 lakh investment deduction, health insurance and the rest), then compare it with the break-even figure for your salary in the table below. If you're above it, the old regime saves you money. If you're below, the new one does.

For 2026-27 that comparison has shifted slightly. The slabs didn't change, but the new Income-tax Rules raised several exemptions that only the old regime allows. For some renters in Bengaluru, Pune, Hyderabad and Ahmedabad, that's enough to change the answer.

## This year's slabs

Budget 2026 left both regimes unchanged for 2026-27, as [ClearTax notes](https://cleartax.in/s/income-tax-slabs). The rates for salaried individuals under 60, from the [Income Tax Department](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1):

| New regime (default) | Rate | Old regime | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Up to ₹4 lakh | Nil | Up to ₹2.5 lakh | Nil |
| ₹4–8 lakh | 5% | ₹2.5–5 lakh | 5% |
| ₹8–12 lakh | 10% | ₹5–10 lakh | 20% |
| ₹12–16 lakh | 15% | Above ₹10 lakh | 30% |
| ₹16–20 lakh | 20% | | |
| ₹20–24 lakh | 25% | | |
| Above ₹24 lakh | 30% | | |

The differences that matter most:

- **Standard deduction:** ₹75,000 in the new regime, ₹50,000 in the old.
- **Rebate:** in the new regime, no tax if taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh, so a salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays nothing. In the old regime, the rebate covers taxable income up to ₹5 lakh.
- **Deductions:** the new regime allows almost none: no HRA, no home-loan interest on a house you live in, no ₹1.5 lakh investment deduction. Your employer's NPS contribution is one of the few that remain.

A 4% cess applies to the tax in both.

## How much in deductions the old regime needs

This table shows, for each salary, how much in deductions and exemptions (beyond the standard deduction) you'd need under the old regime just to pay the same tax as under the new one:

| Salary | Tax under new regime | Deductions needed for old regime to match |
|---|---|---|
| ₹10 lakh | ₹0 | ₹4.5 lakh |
| ₹12.75 lakh | ₹0 | ₹7.25 lakh |
| ₹15 lakh | ₹97,500 | ₹5.44 lakh |
| ₹18 lakh | ₹1,50,800 | ₹6.42 lakh |
| ₹20 lakh | ₹1,92,400 | ₹7.08 lakh |
| ₹25 lakh | ₹3,19,800 | ₹8.0 lakh |
| ₹30–50 lakh | ₹4.76–11.0 lakh | ₹8.0 lakh |

*Salaried, under 60, tax year 2026-27, including 4% cess; surcharge above ₹50 lakh not included. Our calculation; the ₹20 lakh and ₹25 lakh figures match those in ET Money's analysis.*

Three patterns stand out:

- **Up to about ₹12.75 lakh, the new regime is almost impossible to beat**, because it charges nothing. The old regime would need deductions of ₹4.5–7 lakh just to reach zero.
- **Between ₹15 and ₹20 lakh, you need ₹5.5–7 lakh** in deductions. That's reachable mainly if you pay rent with a large HRA, or have a home loan, or both.
- **Above ₹25 lakh, the bar stops rising at about ₹8 lakh.** Both regimes charge 30% at the top, so beyond that point extra income doesn't change the comparison. For high earners with HRA and a home loan, the old regime becomes realistic again.

## What changed in 2026 for the old regime

The Income-tax Rules, 2026, in force from 1 April 2026 alongside the new Income-tax Act, raised several exemptions. As summarised by [KPMG](https://kpmg.com/xx/en/our-insights/gms-flash-alert/2026/flash-alert-2026-081.html), all of these help only if you're on the old regime:

| Exemption | Before | From 2026-27 |
|---|---|---|
| HRA at 50% of basic (the "metro" rate) | Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai | Also Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune, Ahmedabad |
| Employer-provided meals | ₹50 per meal | ₹200 per meal |
| Children's education allowance | ₹100 per child per month | ₹3,000 |
| Children's hostel allowance | ₹300 per child per month | ₹9,000 |
| Gifts and vouchers from employer | ₹5,000 a year | ₹15,000 |
| Interest-free loan for medical treatment | ₹20,000 | ₹2 lakh |

The allowances only count if your employer actually pays them as part of your salary. As ET Money points out, many employers now pay a flat "special allowance" instead, so check with HR whether your salary can be restructured.

## Two Bengaluru renters

HRA exemption is the smallest of three amounts: the HRA you receive, 50% (or 40%) of basic pay, and rent paid minus 10% of basic. The new 50% rule helps only when the second amount is the smallest.

Take a salary of ₹18 lakh with ₹9 lakh basic pay, in Bengaluru. Both people also claim ₹1.5 lakh of investments (EPF, PPF and similar), ₹25,000 of health insurance and ₹50,000 of their own NPS contributions.

**Renter A pays ₹30,000 a month** and receives ₹3.6 lakh HRA. Rent minus 10% of basic is ₹2.7 lakh, the smallest of the three, so the exemption is ₹2.7 lakh under either rule. Total deductions come to ₹4.95 lakh, short of the ₹6.42 lakh break-even: old regime tax ₹1,96,560 against ₹1,50,800 in the new regime. The 2026 change makes no difference for A.

**Renter B pays ₹45,000 a month** and receives ₹4.5 lakh HRA. Under the old 40% rule the exemption was ₹3.6 lakh (40% of basic); under the new 50% rule it's ₹4.5 lakh.

| Renter B | HRA exempt | Old regime tax | New regime tax |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before (40% rule) | ₹3.6 lakh | ₹1,68,480 | ₹1,50,800 |
| 2026-27 (50% rule) | ₹4.5 lakh | ₹1,40,400 | ₹1,50,800 |

*Our calculation, including cess.*

For B, the 2026 change flips the answer: the old regime goes from costing ₹17,700 more to saving ₹10,400. Rent is a large part of many city budgets; our guide to [how much of your salary should go to rent](https://techpulzo.in/how-much-of-your-salary-should-actually-go-to-rent) covers that side of it.

## What counts towards your total

Add everything you can genuinely claim under the old regime. The main items for salaried people:

- **HRA exemption**, if you pay rent and receive HRA.
- **Investments and certain payments up to ₹1.5 lakh**: EPF contributions, PPF, ELSS, life insurance premiums, home-loan principal, children's tuition fees. Your EPF contribution alone often uses a large part of this.
- **Health insurance premiums** for yourself, family and parents.
- **Home-loan interest** on a house you live in, up to ₹2 lakh. Our [home loan affordability guide](https://techpulzo.in/how-much-home-loan-you-can-actually-afford) explains the trade-offs of the loan itself.
- **Your own NPS contributions**, including the extra ₹50,000 allowed over the ₹1.5 lakh limit.
- **The 2026 allowances above**, if your employer pays them.

Only count money you'd spend anyway. Buying an insurance policy you don't need, just to cross the break-even line, costs more than the tax it saves.

## How to choose and tell your employer

1. **Total your deductions** for this year using the list above.
2. **Compare with the table** for your salary. If you're within ₹50,000 of the break-even, the new regime's simplicity (no proofs, less scrutiny) may be worth more than a small difference.
3. **Tell your employer** at the start of the year which regime to use for TDS. If you say nothing, the new regime is the default.
4. **Your return is what counts.** The [Income Tax Department](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/individual/return-applicable-1) treats the new regime as the default, with the option to opt out. Salaried people without business income can choose afresh each year, as ET Money notes, so a choice made with your employer in April isn't a trap.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which tax regime is better for a ₹15 lakh salary?

At ₹15 lakh the new regime charges ₹97,500 including cess. The old regime only matches that if you have about ₹5.44 lakh in deductions and exemptions beyond the standard deduction, typically a large HRA plus the ₹1.5 lakh investment deduction and health insurance, or a home loan. Without rent or a home loan, the new regime usually wins.

### Is the new regime always better below ₹12.75 lakh?

Almost always. A salary up to ₹12.75 lakh pays no tax in the new regime, thanks to the ₹75,000 standard deduction and the rebate up to ₹12 lakh of taxable income. To pay zero under the old regime you'd need deductions of ₹4.5 lakh at ₹10 lakh and over ₹7 lakh at ₹12.75 lakh.

### Does the 50% HRA rule apply in the new regime?

No. HRA exemption, including the higher 50% rate now available in Bengaluru, Hyderabad, Pune and Ahmedabad, is available only under the old regime. The same goes for the higher meal, education and hostel allowance limits from April 2026.

### Can I switch regimes every year?

If you have no business income, yes: you can choose the regime each year, and the final choice is made when you file your return. Different rules apply if you have business income, so check those separately. Tell your employer your choice at the start of the year so the right amount of TDS is deducted.

### What if my deductions are close to the break-even?

Then the difference in tax is small, and other factors matter more: the new regime needs no investment proofs and draws less scrutiny of HRA claims. Run the numbers with your actual figures, and if the old regime saves only a few thousand rupees, many people reasonably choose the simpler option.

## The test, in one line

Add up what you'd genuinely claim under the old regime and compare it with the break-even for your salary. For most people earning under ₹15 lakh, or without rent or a home loan, the new regime wins. For high earners who rent in a big city or carry a home loan, especially in the four cities newly given the 50% HRA rate, the old regime deserves a fresh look this year.
