# How to calculate your electricity bill from appliance usage

Your electricity bill comes down to one number, the units you used, run through your state's tariff slabs. A unit is one kilowatt-hour (kWh): a 1,000-watt appliance running for one hour. So to calculate your electricity bill, you need each appliance's wattage and how long it runs. From those you can estimate your units, work out the bill before it arrives, and see which appliance to cut back on.

This guide sets out the method, which works in any state, and then works through a full household in Tamil Nadu, where the free-units scheme changed in May 2026 and created a sharp jump at 500 units that catches many families out.

## Step 1: units = watts × hours ÷ 1,000

For any appliance:

**Units (kWh) = power in watts × hours used ÷ 1,000**

- A 10-watt LED bulb on for 6 hours uses 10 × 6 ÷ 1,000 = 0.06 units a day.
- A 2,000-watt water heater on for 30 minutes uses 2,000 × 0.5 ÷ 1,000 = 1 unit a day.

Two conversions trip people up, as ElectroDecoded's [step-by-step video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ct9BNGDUb8Y) points out: divide watts by 1,000 to get kilowatts, and convert minutes to hours (30 minutes is 0.5 hours) before you multiply.

Where to find the wattage: on the appliance's rating sticker, in its manual, or on the box. For appliances with a BEE star label, such as refrigerators, use the label's yearly figure instead. The [Bureau of Energy Efficiency](https://www.beestarlabel.com/Content/Files/Session_2.pdf) rates refrigerators on annual energy consumption, printed in units per year, and lower is better. You can check any labelled model on beestarlabel.com.

Two appliances need care:

- **Refrigerators and air conditioners** don't draw full power all the time; the compressor switches on and off. For a fridge, divide the label's yearly units by 365. For an inverter AC, the power varies with the room temperature, so use an average or, better, measure it (see Step 4).
- **Standby loads** (Wi-Fi router, set-top box, chargers, TV on standby) are small but run 24 hours, so they add up.

## Step 2: list your appliances

Make a table for one billing period. Tamil Nadu bills every two months, so the example below uses 60 days; if your state bills monthly, use 30.

| Appliance | Watts (example) | Use | Units in 60 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 ceiling fans | 75 W each | 10 h a day | 135 |
| 8 LED lights | 10 W each | 6 h a day | 29 |
| Refrigerator | Label: 250 units a year | Always on | 41 |
| TV | 100 W | 4 h a day | 24 |
| 1.5-ton inverter AC | about 900 W average while running | 4 h a day | 216 |
| Water heater | 2,000 W | 30 min a day | 60 |
| Washing machine | 500 W | 1 h, 20 days | 10 |
| Router, chargers, standby | about 30 W in total | 24 h a day | 43 |
| **Total** | | | **558** |

Two things stand out in almost every household's table: the **air conditioner** and the **water heater** together account for nearly half the total, and the fans, which run for long hours, come next. Lights barely matter once they're LED.

## Step 3: apply your tariff's slabs

Domestic electricity in India is charged in **slabs**: the first block of units at one rate, the next block at a higher rate, and so on. You need your state electricity board's current domestic tariff.

**Tamil Nadu, from 10 May 2026.** The state government raised the free allowance for households that use up to 500 units in a two-month cycle from 100 units to 200, as [DT Next reported](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/free-power-supply-up-to-200-units-to-benefit-146-cr-domestic-consumers) on the day of the order. Households above 500 units stay on the old structure with 100 free units. The [government order and the regulator's order](https://thediscombill.com/guides/tamil-nadu-electricity-tariff-july-2026/) that put it into effect are G.O. (Ms) No. 50 of 10 May 2026 and TNERC Order No. 5 of 2026. There's no fixed monthly charge for domestic connections.

The rates households pay, after the government's subsidy, work out as follows:

| Units in two months | If you use 500 units or less | If you use more than 500 units |
|---|---|---|
| 0–100 | Free | Free |
| 101–200 | Free | ₹4.70 per unit |
| 201–400 | ₹4.70 per unit | ₹4.70 per unit |
| 401–500 | ₹6.30 per unit | ₹6.30 per unit |
| 501–600 | — | ₹8.40 per unit |
| Above 600 | — | Higher slabs; see your bill breakdown |

*Consumer-payable rates as used in bill calculations shown by [ElectricityBillCalc](https://electricitybillcalc.com/guides/tneb-old-vs-new-tariff-2026/) and in the Karthikeyan Mani video below. Check the slab breakdown on your own bill (Step 4).*

So a 350-unit bill is 150 units at ₹4.70 = ₹705, and a 450-unit bill is 200 at ₹4.70 plus 50 at ₹6.30 = ₹1,255.

## The 500-unit cliff in Tamil Nadu

Because the 200 free units apply only up to 500 units, one extra unit changes the whole calculation. Karthikeyan Mani's [video on high TNEB bills](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gZ1ACGmNhx8) shows it:

| Bimonthly units | How it's charged | Bill |
|---|---|---|
| 500 | 200 free; 200 × ₹4.70; 100 × ₹6.30 | ₹1,570.00 |
| 501 | 100 free; 300 × ₹4.70; 100 × ₹6.30; 1 × ₹8.40 | ₹2,048.40 |

*Our calculation.*

One unit more costs ₹478.40 more. According to DT Next's figures, about 87 lakh households use between 201 and 500 units a cycle, so many of them sit close to this line, especially in summer when ACs run longer. If your usage is near 500, it pays to know exactly where you are.

## Worked example: from 558 units to 484

The household in Step 2 used 558 units. Its bill:

100 units free, 300 × ₹4.70 = ₹1,410, 100 × ₹6.30 = ₹630, and 58 × ₹8.40 = ₹487.20. **Total: ₹2,527.20.**

Now suppose the family makes two changes: the AC runs 3 hours a night instead of 4 (saving 54 units) and the water heater runs 20 minutes instead of 30 (saving 20 units). Usage falls to 484 units, back under 500:

200 units free, 200 × ₹4.70 = ₹940, 84 × ₹6.30 = ₹529.20. **Total: ₹1,469.20.**

Cutting 74 units, about 13% of usage, cuts the bill by ₹1,058, or 42%, because the household gets back under the 500-unit line. That's the most useful thing this calculation tells you: which appliance's hours to trim, and by how much, to stay under your state's thresholds.

## Step 4: check against your actual bill

Your estimate will be off a little, because appliances rarely draw exactly their rated power. Three ways to close the gap:

- **Read your meter.** Note the reading, switch on one appliance for an hour with everything else off, and read it again. This works well for an AC or water heater.
- **Use a plug-in power meter** for appliances on a normal socket. It shows actual watts and units over time.
- **Look at your slab breakdown.** In Tamil Nadu, you can register on TNPDCL's online portal with your consumer number and click a bill to see how it was charged slab by slab, as the Karthikeyan Mani video demonstrates. If the breakdown doesn't match the tariff, raise it with your section office.

If your estimate is far below the bill, look for something you've missed: a geyser left on, an old fridge working hard, a motor pump, or a meter-reading error.

## Other states

The method is the same everywhere; only the slabs, the billing period and the extras differ. Some states bill monthly, some every two months; many add a fixed charge based on your connected load, electricity duty, or a fuel adjustment charge. Find your electricity board's current domestic tariff on its website or on the back of your bill, and replace the Tamil Nadu table with yours. Once you know your bill in advance, it's easier to plan for it; a [zero-based budget](https://techpulzo.in/zero-based-budgeting-how-to-build-a-budget-that-actually-works) gives every rupee, including the power bill, a place.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I calculate electricity units from watts?

Multiply the appliance's power in watts by the hours it runs, then divide by 1,000. A 1,500-watt appliance running for 2 hours uses 1,500 × 2 ÷ 1,000 = 3 units (kWh). Add up all your appliances over the billing period to get your total units.

### How much electricity does a 1.5-ton AC use?

It depends on the model, its star rating and the outside temperature. As a rough guide, an inverter AC averaging about 900 watts while running uses around 3.6 units in a 4-hour night, or about 108 units a month. Check the AC's BEE label for its rated yearly consumption, or measure it with your meter.

### Who gets 200 free units of electricity in Tamil Nadu?

Domestic consumers who use 500 units or less in a two-month billing cycle, from 10 May 2026. If you use more than 500 units, you stay on the earlier structure with 100 free units and higher charges on the next 100 units.

### Why did my TNEB bill jump when I used only a few more units?

If you crossed 500 units in the two-month cycle, you lost the extra 100 free units and moved to the higher slab structure. At 500 units the bill is ₹1,570; at 501 units it's ₹2,048.40, a jump of ₹478.40.

### Does switching appliances off at the plug save electricity?

Yes, a little. Devices on standby draw a few watts each, all day. A router, set-top box and chargers drawing 30 watts between them use about 43 units over two months. Switching off what you don't need overnight trims that, though the AC and water heater matter far more.

## Start with the two biggest loads

You don't need to measure every bulb. Work out your AC and water heater units first, since they usually dominate, then check where your total sits against your state's slab boundaries. If you're just over a threshold, a small change in those two appliances often saves more than anything else you could do.
