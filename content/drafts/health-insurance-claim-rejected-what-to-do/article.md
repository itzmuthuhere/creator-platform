# Health insurance claim rejected or cut? What to do next, step by step

A health insurance claim rejected after a hospital stay feels final. It usually isn't. Indian rules require insurers to explain every rejection, give policyholders a free complaint route that ends with an independent ombudsman, and in many cases bar insurers from disputing a claim at all after five years of cover. Some rejections are reversed simply because someone challenged them in writing, as the cases below show.

This guide explains how to read the rejection, the rules that protect you, and the exact escalation path, with the deadlines at each step.

## First, work out what actually happened

**Was cashless denied, or was the claim rejected?** A denied cashless request isn't the end of the claim. You can pay the hospital and file for **reimbursement**, and the insurer then decides on the full documents. Ditto Insurance's [case walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5zt-c330iQs) describes exactly this: a ₹8 lakh bypass where cashless was refused after an investigation, and the claim was then pursued on reimbursement.

**Was it rejected, or partly paid?** A "partial settlement" usually comes from policy limits: room-rent caps (which can trigger proportionate deductions on the whole bill), co-payment, disease-wise sub-limits, or items the policy lists as non-payable. These follow the policy wording, so check the deduction sheet line by line against it.

## The rules that protect you

IRDAI's [Master Circular on Health Insurance Business](https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/365525/%e0%a4%b8%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%b5%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%b8%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%a5%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%af+%e0%a4%ac%e0%a5%80%e0%a4%ae%e0%a4%be+%e0%a4%b5%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%af%e0%a4%b5%e0%a4%b8%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%af+%e0%a4%aa%e0%a4%b0+%e0%a4%ae%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%b8%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%9f%e0%a4%b0+%e0%a4%aa%e0%a4%b0%e0%a4%bf%e0%a4%aa%e0%a4%a4%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%b0+_+Master+Circular++on+Health++Insurance+Business++29052024.pdf/5e707a91-b5de-1ec1-cf18-b66273a6839d?t=1716962621002&version=1.0) (May 2024) sets several rules worth quoting back to an insurer:

- **No rejection without a committee.** A claim can't be repudiated without approval from the insurer's Product Management Committee or its three-member **Claims Review Committee**.
- **Reasons, with the clause.** If a claim is rejected or partly disallowed, the insurer must give full details, referring to the **specific terms and conditions** of the policy.
- **The five-year moratorium.** After **60 months of continuous coverage**, no health policy or claim can be contested on grounds of non-disclosure or misrepresentation, except **established fraud**. Time on a ported or migrated policy counts.
- **Documents from the hospital.** Insurers and TPAs must collect the required documents from the hospital; the policyholder isn't required to submit them.
- **Cashless timelines.** Cashless requests must be decided within **one hour**, and discharge authorisation granted within **three hours**. If discharge is delayed beyond that, any extra hospital charge is borne by the insurer.

## Common reasons for rejection, and how to respond

| Reason given | What to check | How to respond |
|---|---|---|
| Non-disclosure of a pre-existing disease | Was the condition diagnosed before you bought the policy? Have you had 60 months of continuous cover? | After 60 months, cite the moratorium. Before that, ask what evidence shows prior diagnosis; a later doctor's note isn't proof you knew. |
| Waiting period not over | The policy's waiting periods and your start date, including ported credit | If you ported, credit for waiting periods already served should carry over; check your portability terms. |
| "Not medically necessary" or "could be treated as OPD" | Doctor's notes and reasons for admission | Get a letter from the treating doctor explaining why admission was needed. |
| Exclusion clause | The exact clause quoted | Read the clause's exact words. Insurers sometimes stretch a clause beyond what it says. |
| Missing documents | Whether it was cashless or reimbursement | Supply them, and note that insurers must collect documents from the hospital for cashless claims. |

Deep Wealth Talks' [rundown of rejection reasons](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oZjFDZRrgaQ) puts undisclosed conditions first, with the example of diabetes left out of a proposal form. That's why the moratorium matters so much.

## The escalation path

### Step 1: Write to the insurer's grievance officer

Send a written complaint to the insurer's **Grievance Redressal Officer** (the address is on your policy and the insurer's website). Attach the rejection letter, the policy wording with the relevant clause marked, the discharge summary, and your argument. Keep the acknowledgement.

IRDAI's [Bima Bharosa FAQ](https://bimabharosa.irdai.gov.in/Home/FAQ) says the insurer should resolve the complaint **within 15 days**. The insurer's reply must also give the contact details of the ombudsman you can escalate to.

### Step 2: Register it on Bima Bharosa

If the reply is unsatisfactory or late, register the complaint on **Bima Bharosa**, IRDAI's grievance portal. You get a token number to track it, and if the insurer misses its deadline or you reject its answer, the complaint is escalated and reopened.

### Step 3: Go to the Insurance Ombudsman

The ombudsman is independent, free and fairly quick. According to the [Council for Insurance Ombudsmen's FAQ](https://www.cioins.co.in/Faqs):

- You must first have complained to the insurer, and either received a rejection, got no reply within **one month**, or be unhappy with the reply.
- File within **one year** of the insurer's rejection (or of the one-month deadline passing).
- Claims up to **₹50 lakh**.
- **No fee**, and no lawyer; the rules don't provide for one.
- File online at cioins.co.in, by email, or in writing to the office covering your address or the insurer's branch.
- The ombudsman passes an **award within three months** of receiving everything needed. Hearings can be held by video.
- The award binds the insurer. If you disagree, you can still go to court.
- You **can't** use the ombudsman if the same matter is already before a court or consumer commission.

Under the IRDAI circular, the insurer must comply with an award within **30 days**, or pay you **₹5,000 for every day** of delay, on top of penal interest.

Aamir Iqubal's [account of his own case](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p9-3ut9ckGw) is a useful reality check. His file was first closed because the ombudsman office said it hadn't received his documents. He reopened it by emailing proof of speed-post delivery, and was then heard online, where he argued the exact wording of the clause the insurer had relied on. Keep proof of every submission.

### Step 4: Consumer commission or court

For claims above ₹50 lakh, or if you're unhappy with the ombudsman's award, the consumer commission or the courts remain open. These are slower and more formal, so the ombudsman is usually worth trying first for amounts within its limit.

## A realistic timeline

| Step | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Insurer's grievance officer | Up to about 15 days |
| Bima Bharosa | Varies; the complaint is escalated if the insurer misses its deadline |
| Ombudsman award | Up to 3 months after your file is complete |
| Insurer pays after the award | Within 30 days |

## What to keep from day one

- Every letter and email from the insurer, especially the rejection with the clause quoted.
- The full policy wording and the proposal form you signed.
- Discharge summary, bills, investigation reports and prescriptions.
- A letter from the treating doctor on why treatment or admission was needed.
- Proof of every submission (courier receipts, email copies, portal screenshots).

## Frequently asked questions

### What can I do if my health insurance claim is rejected?

Write to the insurer's grievance officer with the rejection letter, the policy clause and your reasons. If they don't resolve it within about 15 days, register it on IRDAI's Bima Bharosa portal, then take it to the Insurance Ombudsman, which is free for claims up to ₹50 lakh.

### Can an insurer reject a claim for non-disclosure after five years?

No, except for established fraud. IRDAI's rules say that after 60 months of continuous coverage, a health policy or claim can't be contested for non-disclosure or misrepresentation. Time on ported or migrated policies counts toward the 60 months.

### How long do I have to complain to the Insurance Ombudsman?

One year from the date the insurer rejected your complaint, or from the end of the one-month period if the insurer didn't reply. You must complain to the insurer first.

### Does the Insurance Ombudsman charge a fee?

No. There's no fee to file, you don't need a lawyer, and hearings can be held online. The ombudsman handles claims up to ₹50 lakh and must pass an award within three months of receiving all the requirements.

### Is a cashless denial the same as a claim rejection?

No. If cashless is refused, you can pay the hospital and claim reimbursement, and the insurer decides again on the full documents. Insurers must decide cashless requests within one hour under IRDAI's rules.

## The short version

Read the rejection for the exact clause, check it against your policy and the IRDAI rules (committee approval, written reasons, the five-year moratorium), and complain in writing. Escalate from the insurer to Bima Bharosa to the free Insurance Ombudsman, keeping proof of every step. A written, clause-by-clause challenge is what gets rejections reversed.
