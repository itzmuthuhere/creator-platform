# Health insurance portability: what carries over when you switch insurers

Health insurance portability lets you move your policy to a new insurer without starting your waiting periods again. That matters because the waiting periods for pre-existing diseases and specific treatments can run to years, and the five-year moratorium protects your claims from non-disclosure disputes. Lose them, and you're back to square one.

But the credits carry over only up to the cover you already had, the new insurer can still say no, and timing matters. This guide covers what transfers under IRDAI's rules, what doesn't, a worked example, and how to port without a gap.

## What carries over

IRDAI's [Master Circular on Health Insurance Business](https://irdai.gov.in/documents/37343/365525/%e0%a4%b8%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%b5%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%b8%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%a5%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%af+%e0%a4%ac%e0%a5%80%e0%a4%ae%e0%a4%be+%e0%a4%b5%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%af%e0%a4%b5%e0%a4%b8%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%af+%e0%a4%aa%e0%a4%b0+%e0%a4%ae%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%b8%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%9f%e0%a4%b0+%e0%a4%aa%e0%a4%b0%e0%a4%bf%e0%a4%aa%e0%a4%a4%e0%a5%8d%e0%a4%b0+_+Master+Circular++on+Health++Insurance+Business++29052024.pdf/5e707a91-b5de-1ec1-cf18-b66273a6839d?t=1716962621002&version=1.0) (May 2024) says that when you port an indemnity health policy, you can transfer credits **"to the extent of the Sum Insured"** for:

- the **no claim bonus**,
- **specific waiting periods** (for listed illnesses and procedures),
- the **waiting period for pre-existing diseases**, and
- the **moratorium period**.

The five-year moratorium, after which a claim can't be contested for non-disclosure except for established fraud, explicitly counts time on ported and migrated policies.

The same circular puts deadlines on the insurers: the existing insurer must share your underwriting details and claim history within **72 hours** through the Insurance Information Bureau's portal, and the new insurer must decide on your proposal within **5 days** of receiving them.

## What doesn't carry over, or might not

- **Credit on extra cover.** Credits apply only up to your existing sum insured. If you increase cover while porting, [Bajaj General's guide](https://www.bajajgeneralinsurance.com/blog/health-insurance-articles/irda-health-insurance-portability-guidelines.html) notes that fresh waiting periods apply **only to the enhanced amount**.
- **Acceptance.** Porting is a new proposal. The new insurer can accept or reject it, or ask for medical tests, based on your age, health and claim history.
- **Your premium.** The new insurer prices you on its own terms; it won't necessarily match what you paid before.
- **Plan features.** Benefits such as OPD cover, restore benefits or room-rent terms depend on the new plan, not the old one.

## A worked example

Ravi has had a ₹5 lakh family floater with Insurer A for four years. He declared his diabetes when he bought it, and the three-year waiting period for pre-existing diseases is already over. He has built up a ₹2.5 lakh cumulative bonus. He ports to Insurer B and raises cover to ₹10 lakh.

| | Coverage |
|---|---|
| Credit carried over | ₹7.5 lakh (₹5 lakh sum insured + ₹2.5 lakh bonus, if B honours the bonus as cover) |
| Diabetes-related claims | Covered up to the carried-over amount from day one; the extra ₹2.5 lakh has a fresh waiting period |
| Moratorium | Four years counted; one more year of continuous cover to reach 60 months |
| Premium | Set by Insurer B for a ₹10 lakh policy at his age and health |

The details, especially how the bonus is treated, depend on Insurer B's terms, so ask for them in writing before you pay.

## When to apply

Insurers and advisers generally ask you to apply to the new insurer **45 to 60 days before your renewal date**; [Bajaj General's guide](https://www.bajajgeneralinsurance.com/blog/health-insurance-articles/irda-health-insurance-portability-guidelines.html) says at least 45 days. Ditto Insurance's [porting checklist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IzfTm-163pE) gives the same window, and [Ditto's guide](https://joinditto.in/articles/health-insurance/health-insurance-portability-rules-by-irdai/) (updated May 2026) adds that some insurers accept later applications but that this risks a gap in cover. Starting early leaves time for tests and paperwork.

**Don't let the old policy lapse.** If the new insurer hasn't approved the port by your renewal date, renew the existing policy. A lapse breaks continuity, and continuity is the whole point.

## How to port, step by step

1. **Compare plans** at the new insurer: sum insured, room rent, co-pay, sub-limits, network hospitals in your city, and the claim experience you can find.
2. **Apply 45–60 days before renewal** with the new insurer's proposal and portability forms.
3. **Share documents:** previous policy schedules (all years, to prove continuity), renewal notice, ID and address proof, medical reports, and your claim history.
4. **Disclose everything:** conditions, ongoing treatment and past claims. Non-disclosure on a new proposal can be used against a future claim until the moratorium is complete.
5. **Complete any medical tests** the new insurer asks for.
6. **Check the offer:** confirm in writing which waiting periods and how much credit carry over, and the premium.
7. **Pay and get the new policy before the old one expires.** Then let the old one run out. Use the 30-day free-look period to review the new policy's terms.

## Porting or migrating?

If you're happy with your insurer but want a different plan, **migration** to another plan with the same insurer carries over the same kinds of credits under the IRDAI circular, usually with less paperwork. Ditto's [porting vs migration video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U_z31RrP4Z4) suggests porting when the insurer itself is the problem (service, claims, network) and migration when only the plan needs to change.

Niva Bupa's [explainer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UHRvtYBUUaM) (in Hindi) walks through the same steps from an insurer's side.

## Frequently asked questions

### What happens to waiting periods when I port my health insurance?

Waiting periods you've already served, including for pre-existing diseases and specific illnesses, carry over to the new insurer up to your previous sum insured. If you increase your cover, the extra amount has fresh waiting periods.

### Does the five-year moratorium carry over when porting?

Yes. IRDAI's rules say time on ported and migrated policies counts towards the 60 months of continuous coverage after which a claim can't be contested for non-disclosure, except for established fraud.

### When should I apply to port my health insurance?

Apply to the new insurer 45 to 60 days before your current policy's renewal date; insurers generally want at least 45 days. If the port isn't approved by renewal, renew your existing policy so there's no break in cover.

### Can the new insurer reject my portability request?

Yes. The new insurer underwrites you afresh and can accept, reject, ask for medical tests or price the policy differently, based on your age, health and claim history. It must decide within five days of receiving your details from the old insurer.

### Will I lose my no claim bonus if I port?

IRDAI's rules allow the no claim bonus to be carried over to the extent of the sum insured. Confirm with the new insurer exactly how your bonus will be credited before you switch.

## The short version

Porting keeps the waiting periods, moratorium time and bonus you've earned, but only up to the cover you had. Apply 45–60 days before renewal, disclose everything, get the carried-over credits confirmed in writing, and renew the old policy if the new one isn't in place in time.
