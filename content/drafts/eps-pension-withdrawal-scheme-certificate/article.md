# EPS pension withdrawal, scheme certificate or monthly pension: what to choose in 2026

Part of your employer's PF contribution never reaches your PF balance. It goes into the Employees' Pension Scheme (EPS), a separate pool that pays a monthly pension in old age. When you leave a job, the EPS money raises a question the PF balance doesn't: withdraw it as a lump sum, keep the service with a scheme certificate, or (with enough years) claim a monthly pension.

The rules changed twice in 2026. A new pension scheme took effect on 29 June 2026 and added a three-year wait before EPS pension withdrawal. In September the wage ceiling rose from ₹15,000 to ₹25,000. This guide explains the three options as they stand now, the forms behind each, and a simple way to decide.

## How EPS works

- **Where the money comes from.** Of the employer's 12% contribution, 8.33% of your wages up to the ceiling goes to EPS. With the ceiling at ₹15,000 that was ₹1,250 a month; [Business Today reports](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/epfo-ceiling-hike-from-rs15000-to-rs25000-check-your-pension-gain-with-this-formula-555943-2026-09-17) that from 17 September 2026 the ceiling is ₹25,000, which makes the maximum about ₹2,083.
- **It's not your money in an account.** EPS doesn't hold a balance with interest in your name. What you build is **pensionable service** (years) and **pensionable salary** (an average of your last 60 months' wages, within the ceiling).
- **The 10-year line.** With 10 or more years of service you're entitled to a monthly pension. Below 10 years, the choice is a lump-sum withdrawal or a scheme certificate.

The pension formula, as summarised in [SCC Online's review of EPS 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/07/05/employees-pension-scheme-2026-key-changes/), is:

**Monthly pension = pensionable salary × pensionable service ÷ 70**

Someone with 10 years at a ₹15,000 pensionable salary gets 15,000 × 10 ÷ 70 = about **₹2,143 a month**. The minimum pension remains ₹1,000. Members with 20 or more years get two extra years of service added.

## Option 1: Withdraw the EPS amount (Form 10C)

If you have less than 10 years of service and leave covered employment, you can take a lump sum called the **withdrawal benefit**. It isn't a refund of contributions. It's your pensionable salary multiplied by a factor that depends on your completed months of service, set out in a table in the scheme. [Upstox's explainer](https://upstox.com/news/personal-finance/latest-updates/new-eps-scheme-2026-how-withdrawal-benefit-will-be-calculated-on-exit-before-pension-eligibility/article-196995/) gives one example: 36 months at ₹15,000 uses a factor of 2.82, so the withdrawal is **₹42,300**.

**The new 36-month wait.** Under EPS 2026 the withdrawal benefit is payable only after 36 months from the date your last contribution was due, or on reaching superannuation age if that comes first. Before this, you could claim two months after leaving. The change was first announced by EPFO's board in [October 2025](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2178522) and is now part of the notified scheme.

So if you leave a job in October 2026 and don't join another EPS-covered employer, the earliest you can withdraw the EPS amount is around October 2029.

## Option 2: Keep the service with a scheme certificate

A **scheme certificate** records your EPS service and salary so they can be added to service with a future employer. It's applied for with the same Form 10C, but choosing "scheme certificate" instead of "withdrawal benefit".

HR Wale Bhaiya's [explainer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=keQ6Ll3AmgU) uses a clear example: 2 years at company A and 8 years at company B. With a certificate (or a transfer) for the first job, the two add up to 10 years, enough for a monthly pension. Withdraw the first two years and you're back to 8.

**Do you even need one?** If you move straight to a new job under the same UAN and transfer your old PF, your EPS service moves with it, so a certificate adds nothing. It matters mainly when you're leaving covered work for a while (higher studies, a break, a job abroad, starting a business) and also settling your PF.

## Option 3: Monthly pension (Form 10D)

With at least 10 years of service:

- **From 58:** full pension, claimed with Form 10D.
- **From 50 (early pension):** a reduced pension, cut by 4% for each year before 58.
- **Deferred to 60:** the pension rises by 4% for each year you defer past 58.

All three rules are in SCC Online's summary of EPS 2026. Once you have 10 years, the lump-sum withdrawal is no longer available; the pension is the benefit.

## Which should you choose?

| Your situation | Sensible choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Changing jobs, new employer has PF | Neither form: just transfer your PF | Service carries over automatically |
| Leaving covered work for a few years but likely to return | Scheme certificate | Keeps the years counting towards 10 |
| Leaving for good with only a few years of service, and can wait 3 years | Withdrawal (Form 10C) after 36 months | You're unlikely to reach 10 years |
| 10+ years of service | Monthly pension (Form 10D) at 50–60 | Withdrawal no longer available |

**The trade-off in numbers.** Take Ravi, who leaves after three years at a ₹15,000 pensionable salary. Withdrawing gets him about ₹42,300 in 2029. If he instead returns to a PF-covered job later and reaches 10 years, he gets at least about ₹2,143 a month from 58, for life, and if he earns more than ₹15,000, his wages from 17 September 2026 count up to the higher ₹25,000 ceiling. Even before inflation, ₹2,143 a month matches the lump sum in under 20 months.

## How to apply on the EPFO portal

eLearningGang's [walkthrough of Form 10C](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UFTYbUeCiy4) on the new portal shows these steps:

1. **Check your KYC.** Under **Manage > KYC**, confirm the bank account; the money goes only there.
2. **Check Member Service History.** Your date of exit must be updated, and nothing should be "Due for transfer". Move old accounts to your primary Member ID first, or the claim throws an error.
3. Go to **Online Services > Claim**, enter your bank account number, tap **Verify** and accept the terms.
4. Choose **Proceed for online claim**, and under "I want to apply for" choose Form 10C (withdrawal benefit or scheme certificate, as needed).
5. Check the address and the service details shown (pre- and post-November 1995 service, dates of joining and exit).
6. Answer whether you want to **upload a scheme certificate** you already hold. Tech Career's [video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ufOpvFythNQ) shows the new option to enter an existing certificate's number so it can be surrendered and paid out.
7. Tick the consent, tap **Get Aadhaar OTP**, enter it and **Validate and submit**. Download the PDF acknowledgement.

## Common problems

- **"Not eligible" or a waiting-period message:** you're inside the 36-month window. How the new wait applies to people who left before 29 June 2026 isn't clearly set out in the reports we found, and claims in that group have shown mixed results on the portal, so check your claim's response rather than assume.
- **Date of exit missing:** mark it under Manage > Mark Exit once two months have passed since the last contribution.
- **Old account still "Due for transfer":** finish the transfer first.
- **Bank account fails verification:** fix KYC under Manage > KYC and wait for approval.

Agents and "PF consultants" charging to file these forms can't change the rules or the waiting period. The forms are free on the portal, and you should never share your OTP; our guide to [how UPI fraud happens](https://techpulzo.in/how-upi-fraud-actually-happens-the-five-warning-signs) covers the same tricks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I withdraw my EPS pension amount immediately after leaving a job?

Not any more. Under the Employees' Pension Scheme 2026, the withdrawal benefit is payable only 36 months after your last contribution was due, or at superannuation age if earlier. Previously it was two months.

### What is the difference between Form 10C and Form 10D?

Form 10C is for members with less than 10 years of service: it claims either the lump-sum withdrawal benefit or a scheme certificate. Form 10D claims the monthly pension once you have 10 or more years of service and have reached 50 (reduced) or 58 (full).

### Do I need a scheme certificate when I change jobs?

Usually not. If your new employer is covered by EPF and you transfer your old account under the same UAN, your EPS service moves with it. A certificate helps when you leave covered employment for a period and settle your PF.

### How much EPS pension will I get?

Pensionable salary × years of service ÷ 70. At a ₹15,000 salary and 10 years that's about ₹2,143 a month; at 20 years about ₹4,714, including the two-year bonus. Service after 17 September 2026 counts wages up to ₹25,000.

### Is the EPS withdrawal amount the same as what my employer contributed?

No. It's calculated from your pensionable salary and months of service using a table in the scheme, not from the contributions plus interest. In Upstox's 36-month example, the payout of ₹42,300 is less than the ₹45,000 that went in at ₹1,250 a month.

## The short version

If you're changing jobs, transfer your PF and leave EPS alone. If you're stepping away from covered work but may return, take a scheme certificate. Withdraw only if you're leaving for good with a short service record, and note the new three-year wait. With 10 years, the monthly pension is what you're building.
