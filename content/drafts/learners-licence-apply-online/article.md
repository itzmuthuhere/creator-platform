# How to apply for a learner's licence online, and take the test from home

In most states you can now get a learner's licence without visiting the RTO: apply on the Parivahan Sarathi portal, verify yourself with Aadhaar, take the multiple-choice test on a computer at home, and download the licence when you pass. It costs a few hundred rupees, depending on your state, and it's the first step to a permanent driving licence, which you can apply for 30 days later.

This guide covers who can apply, the fees, the forms, each step online, how the home test works, and what to do after you pass.

## Who can apply, and for which vehicles

According to the [Delhi Transport Department](https://transport.delhi.gov.in/transport/learner-licence-0), whose rules follow the central law:

| Age | What you can apply for |
|---|---|
| 16 | Gearless two-wheelers up to 50cc, with a parent's or guardian's consent |
| 18 | Motorcycles with gears, cars and other non-transport vehicles |

You choose the **classes of vehicle** on the application, for example "motorcycle with gear" and "light motor vehicle" (car). Each class adds to the fee, so pick only what you'll actually learn to drive.

## What it costs

The central government sets a floor in Rule 32 of the Central Motor Vehicles Rules: **₹150 per class** for the learner's licence and **₹50 for the test**, as [SMC Insurance's fee guide](https://www.smcinsurance.com/rto/articles/driving-licence-fee-india) summarises. States add their own service or processing charges, so what you pay varies:

| | Fee |
|---|---|
| Central fee, one class | ₹150 + ₹50 test |
| Delhi, one class | ₹500 |
| Delhi, two classes | ₹950 |
| Delhi, three classes | ₹1,400 |
| Retest (Delhi) | ₹50 |
| Tamil Nadu, one class | about ₹230–250, per Nel_AI's [Tamil walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZPLB-Tv_UGE) |

The portal shows the exact amount for your state and classes before you pay. Pay only on the official portal: sites that look like Parivahan but charge "processing fees" are a common scam, as are payment links sent on WhatsApp. Our guide to [how UPI fraud happens](https://techpulzo.in/how-upi-fraud-actually-happens-the-five-warning-signs) describes the patterns.

## Documents and forms

For the online, Aadhaar-based route you need:

- **Aadhaar**, with your current mobile number linked, for the OTP and e-KYC. The Ministry of Road Transport and Highways made the learner's licence one of 18 contactless services that use [Aadhaar authentication](https://www.autocarindia.com/car-news/drivers-licence-renewal-via-aadhar-verification-now-available-online-420138) in 2021. If you don't have Aadhaar yet, the enrolment slip is accepted, but you may need to visit the RTO.
- **Form 1**, a self-declaration of physical fitness, filled in online.
- **Form 1A**, a medical certificate signed by a doctor, **only if you're 40 or older** or applying for a transport vehicle.
- **A photo and signature**, uploaded if the portal doesn't take them from Aadhaar.
- **Parental consent** if you're 16 and applying for a gearless scooter.

With Aadhaar e-KYC, your name, date of birth and address come from Aadhaar, so check they're correct first.

## Applying online, step by step

1. Go to **parivahan.gov.in**, open **Driving Licence related services** (Sarathi), and select your state.
2. Choose **Apply for Learner Licence**.
3. Pick the option to apply with **Aadhaar authentication**, enter your Aadhaar number and the OTP, and consent to e-KYC.
4. Fill in the application: classes of vehicle, RTO, contact details and the Form 1 declaration.
5. Upload Form 1A and any other documents, if they apply to you.
6. **Pay the fee** on the portal and save the receipt and **application number**.
7. Note the password or test credentials that are sent to you by SMS; you'll need them for the test.

## Taking the test online

The learner's test is a set of multiple-choice questions on traffic signs, road rules and safe driving. In Delhi you need **60% to pass**. Before the questions start, you're usually asked to verify your identity with face authentication, and the test is monitored through your computer's camera.

Sarkari DNA's [walkthrough](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hSiDHH2OAzI) shows a recent change: the test now runs inside a locked-down browser app that you install on a **Windows computer**, rather than on a phone. If you don't have a laptop, a friend's will do, or a common service centre can help. The process can differ slightly by state.

To take it:

1. On the Sarathi portal, choose **Online LL test** and enter your application number.
2. Install and open the test application if asked, then log in with your application number and the password from the SMS.
3. Sit in good light, alone, facing the camera. Looking away, having other people in the frame or switching windows can get your test flagged.
4. Answer the questions within the time allowed.
5. If you pass, **download the learner's licence** from the portal straight away.

If you fail, you can book a retest by paying the retest fee.

## After you pass: the 30-day to 6-month window

- The learner's licence is **valid for six months**.
- You must hold it for **at least 30 days** before you can take the driving test for a permanent licence, as the Nel_AI video points out.
- So book your driving test for somewhere between day 30 and the end of month six. If the learner's licence expires first, you'll have to apply again.

While you hold it, you can drive only with a licensed driver beside you (for cars) and with an "L" sign displayed on the vehicle. Carry the licence, or keep it in DigiLocker once your state issues it there.

## Preparing for the test

- **Use the official question bank.** Delhi's transport department publishes one covering road signs and traffic rules, and Sarathi has a practice mock test.
- **Learn the signs by category:** mandatory (red circles), cautionary (red triangles) and informatory (blue rectangles). A large share of questions is about signs.
- **Know the basic rules:** overtaking from the right, priority at junctions, what flashing amber means, and when to use the horn.
- **Take the mock test until you're consistently above the pass mark.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I take the learner's licence test from home?

Yes, in most states, if you apply with Aadhaar authentication. The test runs on a computer with a camera; you verify your identity with face authentication, and a recent walkthrough shows a Windows test application is now required rather than a phone. A few states may still ask you to visit the RTO.

### What is the learner's licence fee?

The central fee is ₹150 per class of vehicle plus ₹50 for the test. States add service charges, so the total differs: Delhi charges ₹500 for one class, and in Tamil Nadu it's around ₹230–250.

### How long is a learner's licence valid?

Six months. You must hold it for at least 30 days before taking the driving test for a permanent licence, so plan your driving test within that window.

### Do I need a medical certificate for a learner's licence?

Only if you're 40 or older, or applying for a transport vehicle. Everyone else submits Form 1, a self-declaration of fitness, as part of the online application.

### What happens if I fail the online LL test?

You can take it again after paying the retest fee (₹50 in Delhi). Use the practice test on the Sarathi portal before you try again.

## Start with Aadhaar

Before anything else, check that your Aadhaar has your correct name, date of birth, address and current mobile number. The whole online route depends on it. With that in order, the application takes about 15 minutes, and the test about the same.
