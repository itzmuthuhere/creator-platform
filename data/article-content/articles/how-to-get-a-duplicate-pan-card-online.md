Lost your PAN card, or is it damaged beyond use? You don't need a new PAN for that — just a reprint of the existing one, submitted through either of two official portals: Protean eGov (formerly NSDL) or UTIITSL. The number itself stays the same, and the request takes only a few minutes to submit.

## Check which portal originally issued your PAN
It doesn't strictly matter which one you use for a reprint request, but sticking with the same agency that issued your original PAN tends to go smoother since your record already sits with them. If you're not sure, either portal will still process it — you're just applying via the 'Reprint PAN Card' or 'Request for New PAN Card / Changes' option.

## Fill the reprint request
Go to the Protean eGov PAN portal or the UTIITSL PAN portal, select the reprint or e-PAN download option, and enter your existing PAN number, Aadhaar number, and date of birth. You won't be asked to redo your photo or signature for a straightforward reprint with no detail changes — the system pulls your existing record.

## Verify with Aadhaar OTP
Since PAN and Aadhaar are linked, verification is done via an OTP sent to your Aadhaar-registered mobile number, which is faster than the older process that needed physical document uploads. If your mobile number isn't updated with Aadhaar, you'll need to fix that with UIDAI first, or fall back to the document-upload route on the portal.

## Pay the fee and choose delivery
There are two options: an e-PAN, a PDF delivered to your email, which is usually the cheaper option and near-instant once processed; or a physical card mailed to your address, which costs somewhat more and takes about two to three weeks depending on India Post delivery timelines. Fees are modest — typically in the range of ₹50 for the e-PAN and closer to ₹100 or more for a physical card with delivery, though exact amounts are set on the portal at the time of application and can change.

## Track your application
You'll get an acknowledgment number after payment — use this on the same portal's status tracking page to check progress. If it's stuck for unusually long, both portals have a grievance or query-status contact option rather than requiring you to restart the whole application.

## What If Your Registered Mobile Number Has Changed Since Your PAN Was Issued
If the mobile number linked to your Aadhaar or PAN record is outdated, the OTP-based verification for a reprint request will fail since it sends to whatever number is on file, not the one you're currently using. Update your mobile number with UIDAI for Aadhaar first if that's the mismatch, since most reprint verification flows lean on Aadhaar-linked OTP as the primary method, and this correction typically needs to be completed before a reprint request will go through smoothly.

## What a Reprint Doesn't Change
A reprint request only reissues the same card with the same PAN number and same core details — name, date of birth, father's or mother's name as originally recorded. It does not let you correct an actual error in those details. If you've noticed a spelling mistake or need to update your name after marriage, that requires a separate 'Changes or Correction in PAN' request rather than a straightforward reprint, and involves a different set of supporting documents.

## Why the E-PAN Is Usually the Better Default Choice
Beyond being cheaper and faster, an e-PAN downloaded as a PDF is generally just as widely accepted as a physical card for most practical purposes, bank KYC, tax filing, opening a demat account, since it carries the same PAN number and a digital signature confirming authenticity. Keeping a saved copy in cloud storage or DigiLocker avoids the entire reprint problem the next time a physical card gets lost or damaged.

## A Common Mistake That Delays Processing
Entering your name with a different spelling or spacing than what's on record, even a minor variation like an extra initial, is one of the more common reasons a reprint request gets stuck in verification instead of processing quickly. Cross-checking the exact spelling against your existing PAN card or a past income tax return before submitting avoids this specific delay entirely.

## Keeping a Backup Before You Ever Need a Reprint
The simplest way to avoid this whole process is downloading the e-PAN once now, while everything's in order, and saving it somewhere you'd remember — DigiLocker, a password manager's file storage, a labelled cloud folder. That way a lost physical card becomes a minor inconvenience rather than an urgent scramble the next time a bank or employer asks for it on short notice.

## What to Do If the Reprint Portal Shows an Error With Your Aadhaar Link
Occasionally the reprint request fails with a generic error citing an Aadhaar-PAN linkage issue even though both documents individually seem fine, usually because the linkage itself, not either document alone, has a data mismatch somewhere in the system. Checking your Aadhaar-PAN link status separately on the income tax portal before retrying the reprint request isolates whether the actual blocker is the linkage record itself rather than anything wrong with the reprint request you're submitting, and fixing the linkage first usually resolves the reprint error on the very next attempt.

## Why Keeping the Acknowledgment Number Matters More Than It Seems
The acknowledgment number generated after payment is the only reference either portal's support team can use to look up your specific request if something goes wrong or takes unusually long, and losing it means starting the status inquiry from scratch with only your PAN number and personal details, a noticeably slower process. Saving a screenshot of the confirmation page the moment you complete the request, rather than trusting you'll remember to note it down later, avoids that entirely preventable hassle, and it's worth keeping that screenshot alongside your other important document records rather than letting it sit buried in a general photos folder.

## What to Expect If You Need the Physical Card Urgently for Travel
A physical reprint's two-to-three-week timeline through India Post doesn't accommodate most urgent travel or visa-related deadlines, which is exactly the situation where the instant e-PAN download becomes the practical answer rather than a fallback, since most institutions accepting PAN as identification now accept the digital PDF version just as readily as the physical card for verification purposes.

## What Counts as Acceptable Proof If a Bank Asks for a Physical Copy
Some older bank branches or offline processes still ask for a physical PAN card despite digital copies being officially valid, and a printed color copy of the e-PAN PDF is generally accepted in these cases, worth trying before assuming a physical reprint is strictly required.

## Double-Checking the Delivery Address Before Submitting
For anyone requesting the physical card option, confirming the delivery address on file is current, not an old address from years ago that happens to still be saved in the system, avoids the frustrating outcome of a correctly processed card being mailed somewhere you no longer live, which then requires a whole separate correction request just to fix the address itself.

## Why It's Worth Doing This Even Before You Actually Lose the Card
Requesting an e-PAN download once, proactively, while your existing physical card is still perfectly fine, costs a few minutes and a small fee, and means the digital backup already exists in your records the day the physical card eventually does get lost, rather than starting the entire process from a standing start under time pressure.

## What to Do If You Need a Duplicate for a Deceased Family Member's PAN
Handling a deceased relative's financial affairs sometimes requires their PAN details even after they've passed, and this specific situation needs a different process, generally requiring a death certificate and legal heir documentation submitted directly to the assessing officer rather than a standard online reprint request.

## What to Do If Two People Share a Similar Name in the Same Family
A father and son with nearly identical names, or two siblings with similar initials, occasionally run into cross-matched records during Aadhaar-PAN verification. If a reprint request returns an unexpected mismatch error despite everything looking correct, checking whether a similarly named family member's records might be causing confusion in the system is worth ruling out before assuming your own application has an error.

## Why Applying Through a Cyber Cafe Isn't a Great Idea Anymore
With Aadhaar-linked OTP verification now standard, applying directly yourself through the official portal is both faster and safer than going through a local cyber cafe or agent, which used to be common when the process required physical document submission. Doing it yourself also means your personal details aren't sitting on someone else's computer afterward.

## Use the instant e-PAN as a stopgap
For urgent situations — a bank KYC deadline, say — there's a faster stopgap: the instant e-PAN download on the income tax e-filing portal itself. It's a separate, free facility for anyone with a valid Aadhaar and no existing PAN issues, and it can often get you a usable PDF well before a physical reprint would arrive in the mail.
