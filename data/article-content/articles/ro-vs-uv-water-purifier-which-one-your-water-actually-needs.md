RO and UV purifiers get marketed like one is just a fancier version of the other. They're not — each one is built to strip out a completely different kind of contamination, and which one your home needs depends entirely on what's in your water, not on which technology sounds more advanced.

## What RO (Reverse Osmosis) does
RO forces water through a very fine membrane that physically blocks dissolved solids — this includes excess salts, heavy metals, and other minerals dissolved in the water. It's built for water with high TDS (Total Dissolved Solids), which is common with borewell or groundwater sources, especially in areas where groundwater has a hard, salty, or metallic taste. RO also removes bacteria and viruses as a side effect of the filtration, but that's not its core purpose.

## What UV (Ultraviolet) does
UV purification passes water past a UV lamp that disrupts the DNA of bacteria, viruses, and other microorganisms, neutralising them without physically removing anything. UV does nothing for dissolved salts or heavy metals — it doesn't remove or reduce TDS at all. UV is suited to water that's biologically contaminated but doesn't have a high dissolved solids problem, which is more typical of treated municipal supply than raw borewell water.

As a general reference, water below roughly 300 ppm TDS is usually fine without RO, while water above 500 ppm is where RO becomes necessary rather than optional. These are general guideline ranges, not a substitute for checking your specific water.

## Why most purifiers sold today combine both
Most mid-range and above purifiers sold today are RO+UV or RO+UV+UF combinations, because they cover both problems, dissolved solids and biological contamination, regardless of which one your specific water source has. This is the safer default if you don't know your water's exact TDS level or if your source varies (like municipal supply that occasionally gets contaminated after heavy rain).

## The downside of RO you should know
RO wastes a significant amount of water in the filtration process — for every litre of purified water, a noticeable amount goes down the drain as reject water, since the membrane can't be 100% efficient. This matters more in water-stressed areas. RO also removes beneficial minerals along with harmful ones, which is why most RO purifiers include a mineraliser cartridge afterward to add some minerals back — check that yours has this before buying, since RO water without remineralisation tastes flat and isn't ideal for regular drinking.

## Maintenance nobody mentions at purchase time
RO membranes need periodic replacement, typically every 12-24 months depending on usage and input water quality, and UV lamps degrade in effectiveness over time even if they still light up, needing replacement roughly once a year. Factor this recurring cost into your decision, not just the upfront purifier price — a cheaper purifier with expensive frequent cartridge changes can cost more over three years than a pricier one with lower running costs.

## Why Some Households Need Neither, Technically
Municipal water in a small number of well-managed urban areas is treated to a standard where additional purification is optional for healthy adults. Most Indian households still choose to purify further, though, given inconsistent supply quality and aging pipe infrastructure that can reintroduce contamination between the treatment plant and the tap. Testing your specific tap water, instead of assuming based on general area reputation, gives a more reliable answer.

## What Happens If You Install RO on Water That Doesn't Need It
Running already-low-TDS water through an RO membrane strips out minerals unnecessarily and wastes water in the process for no real purification benefit — the biological contamination UV handles is the actual risk in low-TDS municipal supply, not dissolved solids. Matching the technology to your water's actual profile avoids both unnecessary cost and unnecessary water waste.

## A Simple Habit That Extends Any Purifier's Effectiveness
Regardless of which technology you choose, keeping a basic log of filter and membrane replacement dates, even a simple note in your phone, prevents the common mistake of running a purifier well past its effective cartridge life, which can mean paying for purification that's no longer happening.

## Test Before You Buy
The ₹300 TDS meter is a better starting point than any salesperson's recommendation. Borewell or high-TDS water points toward RO; low-TDS municipal supply usually only needs UV; and if you're not sure which camp you're in, a combination unit covers both at the cost of a bit more wasted water and a slightly higher running bill.
