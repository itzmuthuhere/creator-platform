Open your phone's photo gallery after a few years of use and the number alone is enough to make you close the app again — eight, ten, fifteen thousand photos, most of them screenshots and duplicates you don't remember taking. Going through that one by one really would eat an entire weekend. Working in bulk filters first, and saving individual review for a much smaller set at the end, gets the same result in a couple of hours instead.

## Start with duplicates and near-duplicates, not content
Before judging whether any photo is worth keeping, remove the mechanical duplication first. Burst-mode shots where you kept fifteen near-identical frames, screenshots you never revisited, and WhatsApp-forwarded images saved automatically to your gallery are usually where the bulk of storage goes, not the meaningful photos. Most phones have a built-in tool or setting that flags similar photos automatically; running that single pass often clears a huge chunk of the total before you've made a single judgment call about content.

## Filter by type before you filter by memory
Screenshots, WhatsApp auto-downloads, and memes make up a surprisingly large share of most people's camera rolls, and almost none of it needs individual review — it needs a bulk decision. Sort your gallery by these categories (most phones let you filter by screenshots or by folder/source) and clear each category wholesale, keeping only the handful you remember wanting. This is bulk work, not sentimental work, and treating it that way is what makes it fast.

## Handle actual photos by month, not by memory
Once you're down to real photos, don't scroll individually through years of them looking for what to delete. Go month by month instead, and for each month ask one question: keep this whole month's batch, or does it need trimming? Most months don't need trimming at all — they're a handful of good photos already. Reserve individual photo-by-photo review only for the specific months you know were photo-heavy, like a trip or an event.

## Get files off the device, not just organized on it
Old files — downloaded PDFs, forwarded documents, old resume drafts — usually shouldn't just be organized into folders on the phone; they should mostly leave the phone entirely. Move anything worth keeping to cloud storage or a computer, and set the phone's Downloads folder to be treated as temporary by default, not permanent storage. A phone that's just a viewing device for files that live elsewhere stays clean automatically instead of needing repeated cleanup.

## What to Do With Photos You Feel Guilty Deleting
Some photos get kept purely out of guilt, not because they're meaningful — a blurry shot, a duplicate of a duplicate, a screenshot from years ago whose context is long forgotten. Giving yourself explicit permission to delete without guilt for anything that doesn't pass a quick, honest 'would I ever look at this again' test removes a surprising amount of hesitation that otherwise slows the whole process down.

## Handling Videos Separately From Photos
Videos take up disproportionately more storage than photos and are reviewed far less often once saved. Applying the same month-by-month bulk-decision approach to videos, instead of letting them hide unreviewed inside a mixed photo-and-video gallery view, usually frees up more space per item reviewed than doing the same pass on photos alone.

## Setting Up Auto-Delete Rules So This Doesn't Pile Up Again
Several phones now offer an automatic rule to delete screenshots or downloaded files after a set number of days if they haven't been opened again. Turning this on after finishing a big cleanup pass prevents the same category of clutter from silently rebuilding itself over the following year, addressing the source instead of needing to repeat the same large cleanup effort annually.

## Making the Whole Project Feel Less Overwhelming to Start
The size of the backlog is usually what stops people from starting at all. Breaking the work into the specific steps above and tackling just the duplicates pass in one sitting, instead of trying to mentally commit to finishing the entire cleanup in one go, makes starting far easier and often builds enough momentum to carry through the remaining steps in the same session anyway.

## Back up before you delete anything irreversible
Bulk deletion is efficient because you're not reviewing each item, which also means occasional mistakes are inevitable. Before any bulk delete pass, confirm you have your photos backed up somewhere — Google Photos, iCloud, or a manual copy to a computer — so a bulk decision made in five seconds isn't a five-second decision you can never undo. Most gallery apps also keep a recently-deleted folder for 30 to 60 days as a safety net; know where yours is before you start.
Work through the steps in that order — duplicates, then categories, then month-by-month, then individual review only where it's warranted — and most years-old backlogs clear out in a couple of focused hours. Set aside an afternoon for it and you'll likely finish with time to spare.
