"Use less electricity" is technically correct and practically useless as advice. In most Indian homes, a handful of specific appliances and habits account for most of the bill, and fixing those brings the number down without asking you to change how you live.

## Find out what's costing you the most
Before changing anything, it helps to know where the money's going. As a rough rule, appliances that heat or cool (AC, geyser, iron, induction cooktop) use far more electricity per hour than appliances that just run motors or electronics (fans, TV, router, lights). If your bill is high and you have an AC or a geyser running regularly, that's very likely where most of the cost is, not your phone charger or Wi-Fi router.

## AC: the single biggest lever
- Set it to 24-26°C instead of 18-20°C. Each degree lower makes the compressor work harder and longer, and this alone is often the single biggest AC-related saving available, with no real comfort loss for most people once they adjust.
- Use the timer or sleep mode instead of leaving it running all night at a fixed temperature — rooms cool down and don't need the same cooling output after the first hour or two.
- Clean the AC filter monthly during heavy usage months. A clogged filter forces the unit to work harder for the same cooling, increasing consumption without you noticing.
- Check the star rating next time you replace one — a 5-star AC uses less electricity over its lifetime than a 3-star unit of the same tonnage, even though it costs more upfront.

## Geyser: the second biggest, often ignored
A geyser left switched on continuously keeps reheating water to maintain temperature even when nobody's using it, which is pure waste. Switch it on 15-20 minutes before you need hot water and off right after, rather than leaving it on standby for hours. If your geyser has a thermostat, setting it to a moderate temperature rather than maximum reduces both energy use and the standby reheating loss.

## Lighting: smaller savings, but free to capture
If you still have any old incandescent or CFL bulbs, switching to LED is one of the more straightforward wins, since LEDs use a fraction of the electricity for the same brightness and last far longer, making up their slightly higher upfront cost over time. Motion-sensor switches or simply being consistent about turning off lights in empty rooms adds up over a full month more than people expect.

## Standby power — small individually, real in total
Chargers left plugged in, set-top boxes never switched off at the wall, and appliances left on standby all draw a small continuous trickle of power. Individually tiny, but across a household with a dozen such devices running 24/7 for a month, it becomes a noticeable, avoidable slice of the bill. A power strip with a physical switch for your TV/set-top box/speaker cluster makes this a one-button habit instead of unplugging things individually.

## Understand your slab-based billing
Many Indian electricity boards bill in slabs, where the per-unit rate increases once your monthly consumption crosses certain thresholds. This means the last few units of a high-consumption month can cost noticeably more per unit than the first ones. Check your state board's slab structure — if you're consistently landing just over a slab boundary, even a small reduction in usage can drop your entire bill into a cheaper per-unit bracket, not just save the marginal units.

## What doesn't move the needle much
Phone chargers, laptop chargers, and routers left on use very little power compared to heating/cooling appliances, so obsessing over unplugging these while leaving the AC at 18°C overnight is solving the wrong problem. Focus effort where the actual load is.

## Reading Your Meter Yourself for a Faster Feedback Loop
Checking your electricity meter's reading at the start and end of a specific change, switching the AC to 25°C for a week, for instance, gives faster, more concrete feedback than waiting for the next full billing cycle, which can be a month or more away. A rough weekly self-check helps confirm whether a specific change is working before committing to it long-term.

## Solar Water Heaters as a Longer-Term Option for Geyser Costs
For households with reliable rooftop sun exposure, a solar water heater can eliminate most or all of the geyser's electricity cost entirely. The upfront installation cost is real, but it pays back over a few years in many regions and is worth researching if the geyser is consistently one of your largest single contributors to the bill.

## Refrigerator: The One Appliance That Never Turns Off
Unlike an AC or geyser you consciously switch on, a refrigerator runs continuously, which makes small inefficiencies add up over a full month more than a single big appliance used for a few hours. Keeping it reasonably full (a fuller fridge holds cold better than a mostly empty one), not overpacking to the point of blocking internal airflow, and checking that the door seal holds a strong grip on a piece of paper closed in the door, are free checks that catch a meaningful, ongoing energy loss most households never think to look for.

## Washing Machine Habits That Actually Matter
Running full loads instead of frequent half-loads, and using cold or warm wash cycles instead of hot wherever the fabric allows, cuts both water heating cost and total number of cycles run per month. The heating element in a washing machine, when used, is a much larger draw than the motor itself, so the water temperature setting matters more to the bill than most people assume.

## Using a Plug-In Energy Meter to Find Your Own Phantom Loads
A basic plug-in energy monitor, inserted between an appliance and the wall socket, shows exactly how much a specific device draws, including in standby, and costs relatively little compared to what it can reveal. Testing your own set-top box, router, and any older appliance for a day or two often turns up a specific culprit drawing far more standby power than expected, letting you fix the actual source instead of guessing at general advice.

## Ceiling Fan Regulators Quietly Waste Power Too
Older-style resistor-based fan regulators, the ones with a rotating dial, waste energy as heat to reduce fan speed rather than reducing power draw, while newer electronic regulators cut power draw proportionally with speed. Replacing old resistor regulators with electronic ones is a small, inexpensive, one-time fix that keeps paying back every month a fan runs on a lower speed setting, which for most households is most of the year.

## Checking If Net Metering Solar Makes Sense for Your Home
For homeowners with a suitable roof, rooftop solar with net metering, where excess power generated during the day feeds back into the grid and offsets your bill, has become more affordable in recent years with state subsidy schemes in many parts of India. It's a bigger upfront decision than anything else on this list, but worth researching if your bill remains high even after the smaller fixes above are already in place.

## Television and Set-Top Box Habits Worth Adjusting
Modern TVs, particularly larger screens, draw more power at maximum brightness than at a moderate setting, and most people run their TV far brighter than needed for comfortable viewing in a normally lit room. Dropping brightness a notch or two and switching the set-top box off at the plug rather than leaving it on standby overnight are both free changes that add up over a full billing cycle without affecting the actual viewing experience.

## Understanding Time-of-Day Tariffs If Your Board Offers Them
A growing number of Indian electricity boards are piloting or rolling out time-of-day tariffs, where electricity costs more during peak evening hours and less during off-peak periods like late night or early morning. If your board offers this and you can shift flexible, high-draw tasks like washing machine loads or geyser heating to off-peak windows, the savings can be meaningful without changing how much electricity you use in total, just when you use it.

## One Habit That Ties Everything Together
Glancing at your electricity board's app or website once a week rather than only at the end of the month turns these fixes into an ongoing feedback loop instead of a one-time cleanup you forget about by the next billing cycle.

## Why Small Fixes Beat One Big Change
None of the individual changes above feels dramatic on its own, but stacked together across a full month, the AC temperature, the geyser timing, the fridge seal, the standby devices, they typically add up to a noticeably lower bill without requiring the household to sacrifice comfort anywhere.

## A realistic monthly routine
None of this involves sitting without a fan or skipping hot water. Keep the AC in the 24-26°C range with a clean filter, run the geyser on demand instead of continuously, switch remaining bulbs to LED, and put standby-heavy devices on a switched power strip — that combination is where the actual savings live, not in unplugging your phone charger.
