## How to Choose a Budget Smartphone: What Actually Matters

### Display
AMOLED over LCD is worth prioritizing at this price — deeper blacks, better outdoor visibility, and usually lower battery draw for dark-themed apps. A 90Hz+ refresh rate makes scrolling noticeably smoother, but don't pay a premium purely for a refresh-rate number if it means downgrading the panel type.

### Camera
Megapixel count is the least useful spec on a camera, and it's the one I see people fall for the most. What matters: sensor size, low-light performance, and whether the main camera (not the depth/macro filler sensors) is capable. A 50MP main sensor with good software processing will usually beat a 108MP sensor bundled with cheap secondary lenses.

### Battery and Charging
Look for 5000mAh as the baseline at this price — anything less means charging more than once a day for a heavy user. Fast charging (33W or higher) matters more than raw battery capacity if you're someone who tops up rather than does full charge cycles.

### Processor and Software Updates
The chipset determines how the phone feels in two years, not just on day one. Just as important and often ignored: how many years of Android/security updates the manufacturer commits to. A phone with a slightly weaker chipset but 3 years of updates will often outlast a "faster" phone that stops getting updates after one year.

### Build and Software Experience
Check how much bloatware and ad-injected software the phone ships with — some budget Android skins are aggressive about pushing ads in the notification shade and lock screen. This is a real day-to-day annoyance that spec sheets never mention.

## Common Mistakes to Avoid When Buying a Budget Phone
- Chasing megapixel count instead of checking real camera samples or reviews for the specific phone
- Ignoring the update policy — a phone that stops getting security patches after a year is a bigger long-term cost than a slightly higher price now
- Buying based on a single spec (like "108MP camera") without checking the rest of the package
- Skipping the return window — always test the phone for a few days and use the replacement/return policy if something feels off

## Should You Wait for a Sale Instead of Buying Now
Budget phones in this segment typically see meaningful price drops during major sale events (Flipkart Big Billion Days, Amazon Great Indian Festival), often ₹1,000-2,000 off the launch price within 4-6 months of release. If your current phone is functional and the purchase isn't urgent, checking whether a sale is within a few weeks is worth the wait; if you need a phone now, the prices listed above are close to what you'll pay outside sale windows regardless.

## Financing and Exchange Offers: Read the Real Terms
No-cost EMI offers on these phones are common but not always free — some bundle a processing fee or a slightly inflated base price into the "no-cost" framing, so comparing the total EMI payments against the flat cash price is worth the extra two minutes before assuming a financing offer is a pure benefit. Exchange offers for an old phone also vary significantly between platforms for the identical old device — checking two or three exchange valuations before accepting the first one shown can change the final price.

## Is the Higher Storage Variant Worth the Extra Money
Usually, if the price gap is small. Storage isn't something you can upgrade later the way you sometimes can on a laptop, and budget phones with base storage (64GB or 128GB) fill up faster than people expect once photos, apps, and OS updates pile up over two or three years. And honestly, the difference between these three phones matters less than the spec sheets make it look. All three handle daily use fine, calls, browsing, social media, casual gaming, and the gaps pointed out above mostly matter if you have one specific priority, like camera quality or how long you'll get software updates, rather than one phone simply beating the others across the board.
