Checking your EPF balance comes down to one prerequisite: an activated UAN (Universal Account Number). Every method below — SMS, missed call, the UMANG app, or the member portal — depends on it, so start there before trying any of them.

## First, activate your UAN if you haven't
Your UAN is issued by your employer and stays the same across job changes. Go to the UAN member portal at unifiedportal-mem.epfindia.gov.in, click Activate UAN, and enter your UAN, name, date of birth, and mobile number. You'll get an OTP to set your login. Without this activation step, none of the quick-check methods below will work, because they all rely on your mobile number being linked to your UAN in EPFO's system.

## Method 1: Missed call — fastest, no data needed
Give a missed call to 011-22901406 from your registered mobile number. Within a minute you'll get an SMS back with your last contribution and PF balance, provided your UAN is linked with your Aadhaar, PAN, and bank account (KYC-seeded). This is the fastest method and works even on a basic feature phone.

## Method 2: SMS — works in multiple languages
Send an SMS to 7738299899 in the format EPFOHO UAN followed by a language code, for example EPFOHO UAN ENG for English. You can also get it in Hindi, Marathi, Punjabi, Gujarati, Telugu, and several other regional languages by changing the last three letters of the code. Again, this only works from the mobile number registered against your UAN.

## Method 3: UMANG app — most detailed view
UMANG (Unified Mobile Application for New-age Governance) is the government's multi-service app, and EPFO is one of the services inside it. After logging in with your UAN and OTP, go to Employee Centric Services and select View Passbook. This gives you a month-wise breakup of employee and employer contributions, not just a single running total — useful if you're trying to reconcile what your employer has deposited against what's shown on your payslip.

## Method 4: EPFO member e-Sewa portal — the full passbook
Log in at the Member e-Sewa portal with your UAN and password, then go to View, then Passbook. This is the most complete version — separate passbooks for each employer if you've changed jobs, interest credited annually, and pension contribution splits. It's the one to use if you're checking before a withdrawal or transfer request rather than just a quick balance check.

## What to Do If the Missed Call or SMS Doesn't Work
If the missed call or SMS method returns nothing, the most common cause is that your UAN isn't yet KYC-seeded, meaning Aadhaar, PAN, and bank details aren't linked in EPFO's system even though the UAN itself is active. Log into the member e-Sewa portal directly and check under Manage, then KYC, to see which of the three is missing, since all quick-check methods depend on this seeding being complete.

## What Happens to Your EPF When You Switch Jobs
Your UAN stays the same across employers, but each new job technically opens a new member ID linked under that same UAN, and the balance doesn't automatically merge unless you submit a transfer request, which can be done directly through the member portal under Online Services, then One Employee - One EPF Account. Skipping this step is a common reason people end up with multiple small, dormant EPF balances scattered across old employers instead of one consolidated account earning interest properly.

## Why the Passbook Sometimes Shows Two Separate Balances
If you see both an EPF and an EPS figure in your passbook, those are two different funds. EPF is your retirement savings balance, while EPS (Employee Pension Scheme) is a separate pension component that pays out as a monthly pension after retirement instead of as a lump sum. That's why its number often looks small relative to your EPF balance, and shouldn't be mistaken for an error.

## Downloading a Passbook Statement for Loan or Visa Applications
Some loan applications and visa processes ask for an EPF statement as proof of employment history or income stability, and the passbook PDF downloadable from the member e-Sewa portal is generally accepted for this. Worth generating and saving a copy periodically, instead of needing to log in under time pressure when a specific application deadline is looming.

## Setting a Simple Reminder to Check Periodically
Instead of checking only when something feels off, a quick balance check every few months, timed to something memorable like the start of a new quarter, catches a stopped employer contribution far earlier than waiting a full year would. A gap in contributions is worth flagging to your HR team immediately, not assuming it will sort itself out.

## What to Do If You Suspect Your Employer Has Stopped Contributing
A gap in the monthly contribution rows on your passbook, especially if it persists for more than one cycle, is worth raising directly with HR or payroll rather than assuming it's a temporary processing delay, since employer contribution defaults are a genuine and unfortunately not uncommon issue, and catching it early through a periodic passbook check makes the eventual resolution far simpler than discovering a multi-month gap only at the time of withdrawal or transfer, when resolving months of missing employer contributions becomes a much more involved and stressful process.

## Linking Your UAN Across Multiple Old Employers
Anyone who's changed jobs several times without ever consolidating their EPF accounts may find multiple old member IDs sitting under the same UAN, each with its own small balance still technically earning interest but effectively forgotten. The member portal's transfer request tool can consolidate these into the current active account in most cases, and it's worth doing this consolidation once rather than leaving several fragments scattered across old employers indefinitely, since a consolidated account is simpler to track, simpler to withdraw from eventually, and less likely to have a piece forgotten entirely.

## Understanding the Difference Between an EPF Withdrawal and a Transfer
When leaving a job, the temptation to withdraw the accumulated EPF balance outright rather than transferring it to the next employer's account is understandable but usually costs more in the long run, both through tax implications on early withdrawal in many cases and through the lost benefit of continuous, compounding retirement savings. A transfer, kept simple through the same online portal covered above, preserves both the tax advantage and the long-term growth that a cash-out interrupts.

## What to Do If You've Forgotten Your UAN Entirely
A forgotten UAN can be retrieved through the same member portal using your registered mobile number and basic KYC details, without needing to contact your employer or EPFO directly, a faster path than most people assume exists, and one worth knowing about before assuming a lost UAN means a trip to a regional EPFO office.

## Checking Your Nominee Details While You're Already Logged In
Since you're already inside the member portal to check your balance, it costs almost nothing extra to also verify your EPF nominee details are current under the e-Nomination section, a step many people never revisit after their very first job, even though it directly determines who receives the balance in the event something happens to the account holder.

## Why This Balance Deserves the Same Attention as a Bank Account
It's easy to treat EPF as a background number that takes care of itself, but it's one of the larger long-term savings pools most salaried Indians accumulate, and treating periodic balance checks with the same seriousness given to a savings account statement catches problems, missed contributions, unconsolidated old accounts, stale nominee details, while they're still simple to fix.

## What Happens to Your EPF If the Company Itself Shuts Down
A company going out of business doesn't erase your accumulated EPF balance, since the fund is held by EPFO independently of the employer's ongoing existence. You can still withdraw or transfer it using your UAN through the standard member portal process, though it sometimes takes a little longer if the employer's own compliance records were incomplete before closing.

## What the EPFO Mobile App Adds Beyond the Website
Beyond UMANG, EPFO has its own dedicated mobile app offering much of the same passbook and claim-tracking functionality in a lighter, faster interface. Worth trying if the UMANG app has ever felt slow or cluttered with unrelated government services you don't personally need.

## Checking Your EPF Alongside Your Other Retirement Savings
EPF is usually just one piece of a broader retirement picture that might also include the National Pension System, personal mutual fund investments, or other savings. Reviewing all of them together once or twice a year, rather than checking EPF in isolation, gives a more honest sense of whether you're on track for retirement rather than just watching one number grow.

## A quick note on why the number might look 'stuck'
One quirk worth knowing: EPFO credits interest only once a year, usually with a lag of several months after the financial year closes, so the balance can look unchanged for long stretches even while contributions go in every month. If you want to confirm deposits are happening, check the passbook's contribution rows rather than the top-line balance.
