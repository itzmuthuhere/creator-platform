Resolving a consumer dispute in India generally comes down to sequence, not legal firepower: file an informal complaint first, and only move to a formal e-Daakhil case if that doesn't resolve things. Jumping straight to a formal filing usually just adds weeks you didn't need to spend.

## Step 1: File on the National Consumer Helpline first
Go to consumerhelpline.gov.in or call 1915 (or 1800-11-4000), or use the UMANG or NCH app. Register with your mobile number, describe the issue, attach any invoice or chat screenshots, and submit. NCH forwards your complaint to the company's nodal officer and tracks a resolution window — many disputes, especially with e-commerce, telecom, and banking, get resolved at this stage because companies are required to respond within a set timeframe to keep their NCH rating from dropping.

## Step 2: Give it time, but track the status
You'll get a unique complaint number — use it to check status on the same portal. If the company responds with an offer you're not happy with, or doesn't respond within the window shown on your dashboard, that's your trigger to escalate to a formal case rather than waiting indefinitely.

## Step 3: File a formal case on e-Daakhil if needed
e-Daakhil (edaakhil.nic.in) is the official portal for filing cases before the Consumer Disputes Redressal Commissions — District, State, or National, depending on the value of your claim. Register an account, select your case type as a consumer complaint, and pick the district commission that has jurisdiction over either where you reside or where the seller operates. You'll fill in party details, facts of the case, relief sought, and upload supporting documents — invoice, correspondence, and any expert opinion if relevant.

## Fees and what happens after filing
Filing fees on e-Daakhil are nominal and scale with claim value — often a few hundred rupees for smaller claims. After filing, you'll receive hearing notices through the portal and registered email; many hearings, especially preliminary ones, are now conducted via video conferencing, so you don't necessarily need to travel to the commission's location.

## What Counts as a Valid Consumer Complaint
The Consumer Protection Act covers a broad range of disputes — defective goods, deficient services, unfair trade practices, misleading advertisements, and overcharging beyond the price displayed or agreed. So the process applies well beyond the classic example of a faulty product; banking service failures, delayed insurance claims, and even e-commerce delivery disputes all qualify under the same framework.

## Following Up Without Losing Track of Your Case
Both NCH and e-Daakhil generate reference numbers so a complaint doesn't get lost in an inbox. Saving these numbers along with the date filed in a simple note, instead of relying on email search later, makes it much easier to follow up efficiently if a company or the commission is slow to respond within the expected window.

## When a Company's Own Grievance Cell Should Come First
Many companies, particularly banks, telecom operators, and insurers, are required to maintain an internal grievance redressal officer or nodal officer, and regulators like RBI and TRAI often expect you to have attempted that internal channel and given it a reasonable window to respond before escalating to a sector ombudsman or the consumer forum. Worth checking the company's own website for this contact before jumping straight to NCH.

## Sector-Specific Ombudsmen Worth Knowing About
Certain sectors have a dedicated ombudsman separate from the general consumer forum system. The RBI Banking Ombudsman for bank-related disputes and the Insurance Ombudsman for insurance claim disputes both offer a free, relatively fast resolution path designed for their sector, and trying that route first for a banking or insurance issue is often faster than the general consumer complaint process covered above.

## A Realistic Timeline to Expect
NCH-mediated resolutions for straightforward cases — a refund, a replacement — commonly resolve within a few weeks if the company is responsive, while a formal e-Daakhil case can take several months to over a year depending on the commission's caseload and whether the opposite party contests it. Worth factoring that timeline into whether a formal filing is proportionate to the value of your specific dispute.

## Preparing Evidence Before You Even Open the Complaint Form
A complaint filed the same day an issue occurs, with a clear timeline already written out, moves faster than one filed weeks later from memory. Keep a running note from the moment a dispute starts: what was promised, what was delivered instead, every date you contacted the company and what they said. This turns a messy back-and-forth into a clean chronological account you can paste directly into a complaint form, rather than trying to reconstruct the sequence under pressure once you've decided to escalate.

## What Happens If the Company Simply Doesn't Respond at All
Silence is itself grounds to escalate. NCH tracks whether the company's nodal officer has responded within the window shown on your dashboard, and a company with no response logged against multiple complaints affects its own standing with the platform. Don't wait indefinitely for a reply that may never come; once the tracked window closes with nothing on record, that's your cue to move to e-Daakhil rather than continuing to wait out of politeness.

### Sending a Legal Notice Before Filing, If the Claim Is Substantial
For larger disputes, sending a formal legal notice through a lawyer before filing a case sometimes resolves things without needing to go to a commission at all, since a company facing a documented legal threat may settle rather than deal with a formal proceeding. This is a optional step, not required by the process, but worth considering for claims where the value justifies the modest cost of having a lawyer draft one.

## How Video-Conference Hearings Actually Work in Practice
Most preliminary hearings now happen over video call rather than requiring you to travel to the commission's physical location, which has made the process more accessible for people outside major cities. You'll typically get a link and a scheduled time through the portal or registered email. Dress and present the way you would for an in-person hearing, and have your documents open and ready to reference rather than searching for them mid-call.

## What a Typical Outcome Actually Looks Like
Consumer commissions can order a refund, a replacement, compensation for loss or harassment, and in some cases punitive costs against the company if the commission finds the dispute was handled in bad faith. The relief you get depends heavily on how clearly you stated what you wanted in the original complaint, one more reason the "relief sought" section deserves real thought rather than a vague request for action to be taken.

## Complaints Involving a Marketplace Versus the Actual Seller
For e-commerce disputes specifically, deciding whether to name the platform, the third-party seller, or both matters. Large marketplaces are increasingly held to some responsibility for goods sold through their platform, particularly where the platform handled payment and logistics directly, but the seller entity itself usually still needs to be named as a party too. Checking the seller's registered name on your invoice, not just the storefront name shown on the app, gets the right party named from the start rather than needing correction later.

## What to Do If You Win but the Company Doesn't Comply
A commission order that a company ignores isn't the end of the road. The commission has enforcement powers, including the ability to attach property or recover the amount as if it were a court decree, and you can file an execution petition for non-compliance. This step is rarely needed since most companies comply once an order is issued — ignoring it opens them to a much more serious enforcement process than the original dispute.

## When It's Faster to Just Escalate on Social Media First
For consumer-facing brands with an active customer support presence on X or Instagram, a public, factual post describing the unresolved issue sometimes gets a faster internal response than the formal complaint channel, since brands generally treat public visibility as higher priority than a private ticket. This isn't a replacement for the formal NCH or e-Daakhil process, since a social media reply carries no legal weight, but running both in parallel rather than waiting for one to fail before trying the other saves real time when the issue is urgent.

## A Quick Note on Complaint Fees
Filing through the NCH portal or e-Daakhil for consumer disputes doesn't require a lawyer, and for most individual claims the filing fee is nominal or waived entirely depending on the claim value. Don't let the assumption that this process is expensive or requires legal representation stop you from filing in the first place — that assumption is one of the more common reasons genuine, winnable complaints never get filed at all.

## What to keep ready before you even start
Keep these ready before you start: invoice or purchase proof, all written communication with the seller (emails, chat exports, call recordings if you have them), and a clear one-paragraph statement of the relief you want — refund, replacement, or compensation. Complaints without a specific ask tend to sit longer in the queue.
