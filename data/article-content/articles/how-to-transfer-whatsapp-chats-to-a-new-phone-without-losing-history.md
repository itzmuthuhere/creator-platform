Android-to-Android, Android-to-iPhone, and iPhone-to-iPhone each need a different WhatsApp transfer method, and picking the wrong one for your specific combination is the single most common way people lose years of chat history during a phone upgrade.

## Android to Android: use the direct transfer tool, not cloud restore
WhatsApp's built-in migration tool moves chats device-to-device over a local Wi-Fi connection using a QR code, without needing a Google Drive backup at all. On the new phone, during WhatsApp setup, choose "Transfer your data using an Android device" and scan the QR code shown, then keep both phones on the same Wi-Fi network and close together until the transfer finishes. This tends to be more complete and reliable than a Google Drive restore, since Drive backups don't always fully sync recent media before you switch.

## Android to iPhone or iPhone to Android: use the official migration tool
Switching platforms requires WhatsApp's own cross-platform move tool built into the app itself — do not rely on Apple's older "Move to iOS" app alone for chat history, since WhatsApp's own in-app migration flow (Settings > Chats > Chat Transfer on the sending device) is what carries chat content across platforms now. Both phones need to stay connected, charged, and on Wi-Fi for the full duration, and the process needs enough free storage on the new phone to match your chat and media size on the old one.

## iPhone to iPhone: iCloud is still the primary path
On iPhone, back up to iCloud from Settings > Chats > Chat Backup before switching, then restore during WhatsApp setup on the new device using the same Apple ID and the same phone number. iCloud backup frequency is configurable — daily is worth setting if you're expecting to switch devices soon, since a backup from last week won't include the days right before the switch.

## The number one rule that trips people up
Every method above requires using the same phone number to verify WhatsApp on the new device as was registered on the old one. If you're also switching SIM or number at the same time as the phone, do the WhatsApp transfer first while the old number is still active, before deactivating or porting it.

## Check storage and Wi-Fi before you start
Transfers fail midway more often from insufficient free space on the new phone than from any other cause — check that the new device has at least as much free storage as your old chat backup size shown in WhatsApp settings. Use Wi-Fi rather than mobile data for any of these transfers; large media libraries over mobile data are slow and more likely to time out or get throttled.

## What Happens to Starred Messages and Media During Transfer
Starred messages carry over as part of the normal chat transfer on same-platform moves — Android to Android or iPhone to iPhone. But the star status itself doesn't always survive a cross-platform move, Android to iPhone or the reverse. Re-star anything important right after switching platforms instead of assuming the marker traveled with the message.

## Handling a Failed or Interrupted Transfer
If a transfer stalls or fails partway, don't immediately retry from scratch. Check first that both devices still have a stable Wi-Fi connection and enough battery — transfers commonly fail from a dropped connection, not a genuine incompatibility. Restarting both devices and starting over from the same point resolves most stalled transfers without any further troubleshooting.

## What to Do With WhatsApp Business Accounts Specifically
WhatsApp Business accounts follow the same transfer methods as regular WhatsApp, but catalog items, saved quick replies, and labels are tied to the business profile data and transfer along with the chat backup. Confirm these business-specific features loaded correctly on the new device, using the same verification pass as chats and media — losing a catalog silently is a bigger practical problem for a business account than losing a personal chat.

## Why Waiting a Few Extra Minutes After 'Transfer Complete' Is Worth It
WhatsApp sometimes marks a transfer as finished while media is still syncing in the background, particularly for accounts with large photo and video libraries. Give it a few extra minutes on Wi-Fi after the completion message before disconnecting or switching networks — otherwise recent media can appear missing simply because it hadn't finished downloading yet.

## Keeping the Old Phone as a Backup for a Few Weeks
Instead of wiping and selling or handing over the old phone immediately after a transfer, keep it powered off but intact for a couple of weeks. It's a cheap safety net if something turns out to be missing later — far cheaper than discovering a gap in chat history after the old device is already gone for good.

## What to Do If You're Switching Numbers at the Same Time as Phones
A number change happening simultaneously with a device change adds real risk to the transfer, since WhatsApp verification depends on receiving an OTP on the number being registered. Completing the device transfer fully on the old number first, confirming everything loaded correctly, and only then going through WhatsApp's separate change-number flow to move to the new number avoids the two changes interfering with each other mid-process and reduces the chance of ending up locked out of both the old and new setup simultaneously.

## Why Group Chat History Sometimes Behaves Differently From One-on-One Chats
Very large or very old group chats occasionally take noticeably longer to fully sync during a transfer than individual conversations, particularly groups with years of shared media. Checking a couple of your largest, oldest groups during the post-transfer verification step, not just a recent one-on-one chat, catches a partial sync that a quick spot-check of smaller chats would miss entirely, since a smaller recent chat syncing correctly says nothing about whether a much larger, older one did too.

## What to Do If You've Deleted Media Locally to Save Space Before
If you previously used a storage-saving feature that removed downloaded media from the old phone while keeping the message text, that missing media generally can't be recovered during a transfer either, since the transfer moves what currently exists on the source device, not what once existed before it was cleared. Checking whether older chats already show missing media on the old phone, before assuming the transfer itself is at fault, saves a confusing troubleshooting detour on the new device.

## A Final Sanity Check Worth Doing Before You Relax
Beyond checking a handful of chats, scrolling to the very oldest conversation in your list and confirming it still opens and loads correctly is a fast, single check that catches a rare but real failure mode, a transfer that silently truncated history beyond a certain date, which a spot-check of only recent chats would never reveal.

## Why Doing This Transfer During Daytime Hours Helps
Running the full process during a period when you can monitor both devices, rather than starting it right before bed and hoping it finishes overnight, means you're available to notice and fix a stalled or failed step immediately instead of discovering a problem the next morning after both phones have been left unattended for hours.

## One More Reason to Not Rush This Process
Chat history that took years to build accumulates in a way that's easy to underestimate until it's gone, treating this transfer with the same care as backing up photos or important documents, rather than as a quick afterthought squeezed into an already busy phone-upgrade day, reflects how much is riding on it going right, and the extra care costs nothing beyond a little patience on an already busy day.

## A Short Recap of Which Method Applies to You
Same platform, Android to Android or iPhone to iPhone, use the built-in direct transfer or iCloud restore respectively. Switching platforms entirely, use WhatsApp's own in-app Chat Transfer tool rather than any general phone-migration app. Getting this one choice right at the start avoids most of the trouble described above.

## What to Do If You Manage a Shared Family WhatsApp Account
A household phone used by multiple family members under one WhatsApp number needs everyone informed before a transfer happens, since a botched or rushed migration on a shared device affects more than one person's conversations at once. Picking a time when it's least disruptive, rather than doing it mid-day without warning, avoids frustrating everyone who relies on that number.

## Checking That Voice Notes Survived the Transfer, Not Just Text
Voice messages sometimes sync more slowly than text and photos during a transfer, since audio files add real weight to the total data being moved. Playing a couple of older voice notes specifically, not just glancing at whether the message bubble is present, confirms the actual audio content transferred correctly and isn't just a placeholder.

## Verify before wiping the old phone
Once the transfer finishes, open a few older chats on the new phone and scroll back far enough to confirm photos and voice notes loaded, not just the message text. Hold off on resetting or handing over the old phone until that check is done — it's the step people skip most often when they're in a hurry to get the new device running.
