A layoff strips away more than income — it removes the daily structure that told you what to do next, which is part of why the first two weeks feel so disorienting. A handful of administrative and financial things in that window have real deadlines, even though there's no new job to chase yet, and the common reactions are to freeze or to panic-apply to fifty jobs at once. Working through a short, concrete list first tends to serve you better than either.

## Get the paperwork moving in week one
Ask HR explicitly, in writing, for your relieving letter, experience certificate, Form 16, and full and final settlement timeline. Indian companies vary widely on how fast full and final settlement happens, anywhere from a few weeks to a couple of months is common, so get a specific date, not "soon," and follow up in writing if it slips.

## Check your full and final settlement carefully
Full and final settlement typically includes your pending salary, encashment of unused earned leave, any pending reimbursements, gratuity if you've completed five years of service, and severance if your layoff terms included it. Check the leave encashment number against your own tracked leave balance — this is the line item most likely to have an error, since it depends on HR's records matching what you took. If severance was verbally promised, get it in the written termination letter or a separate signed document, not just a conversation.

## Don't let health insurance lapse silently
If your only health cover was your employer's group policy, it typically ends on your last working day or shortly after, and India has no automatic continuation like the US's COBRA — you have to act. Ask HR if the group policy offers a conversion option to an individual policy, which some insurers allow at existing terms if you apply within a short window. If not, buy an individual or family health policy immediately rather than waiting — a gap in coverage during a medical event can mean paying entirely out of pocket, and pre-existing condition waiting periods reset with new policies, so continuity matters. One thing worth asking about specifically: IRDAI-mandated health insurance portability lets you carry forward credit for waiting periods already served when you move from a group or employer policy to an individual one, or between insurers, as long as there's no gap in coverage — so check whether that route is available before assuming you're starting the waiting period from zero.

## Decide on your Provident Fund, don't ignore it
You generally have three options: transfer your EPF to your next employer once you have one, leave it as is since it keeps earning interest for a few years even if inactive, or withdraw it early. Early withdrawal before five years of continuous service is usually taxable and should be a last resort, not a first move, since it also breaks your long-term retirement corpus. If you're unsure when you'll be employed again, leaving it parked is almost always better than withdrawing out of short-term anxiety.

## Tell your network before you touch a job board
Referrals move faster than cold applications almost everywhere, and the sooner your network knows you're looking, the sooner that channel starts working for you. A short, factual LinkedIn post or a direct message to five or six people you trust, stating plainly that your role was affected by a layoff and what you're looking for next, does more in the first two weeks than fifty job board applications, which have among the lowest response rates of any channel.

## Rebuild your budget honestly
Lay out your actual monthly essential expenses against your current savings and any severance, and calculate a real runway number, not an optimistic one. Pause discretionary spending and any large financial commitments until you have visibility on timeline, but don't panic into decisions like early EPF withdrawal or breaking long-term investments before you've run the numbers on how long you can realistically hold out.

## A Note on Unemployment Support in India
Unlike some countries, India doesn't have a broad unemployment insurance system most private-sector employees can draw on, so there's no government check to rely on during the search, which makes the runway calculation above even more important than it would be elsewhere. A few states and specific industries have limited schemes, worth a quick check if you're covered by one, but the honest default assumption should be that your savings and severance are the entire runway.

## Why the First Two Weeks Set the Tone for Everything After
Handling the paperwork, insurance, and financial groundwork properly in this window, even while it feels like the least urgent part compared to job hunting, removes a stack of background stress that would otherwise resurface later at a worse possible moment.

## Updating Your Resume Before You Need It Urgently
Rather than waiting until a specific job posting prompts a rushed rewrite, spending an hour in the first week updating your resume with your most recent role's actual accomplishments, while the details are still fresh in memory, produces a stronger document than trying to reconstruct specifics weeks later once the details have started to blur and feel less concrete in your own recollection.

## Checking Whether Any Outstanding Loans Have Employment-Linked Terms
Some personal loans, credit cards, or EMI plans taken through employer tie-ups carry specific clauses tied to continued employment, worth reviewing directly with the lender rather than assuming standard terms automatically continue unchanged, since a layoff can occasionally trigger a review clause buried in the original loan agreement that's easy to miss until it becomes relevant to your current situation.

## What to Do About Any Stock Options or ESOPs
If your compensation included stock options, check the exercise window specifically, since most companies impose a limited period, commonly 90 days, after your last working day during which vested options must be exercised or they're forfeited entirely, a deadline that's easy to lose track of amid everything else happening in the same stretch of time, and one worth calendaring immediately rather than trusting yourself to remember it weeks into the job search.

## Deciding Whether to Take a Short Break Before Actively Applying
Not everyone needs to start applying within the first few days, and for some people a short, deliberate pause of a week or two to process the loss and recover some energy produces stronger applications afterward than immediately grinding through job boards while still emotionally raw from the news. This is a genuine, defensible choice within reason, not procrastination, as long as it's a bounded decision with a real end date attached rather than an open-ended drift.

## One Last Practical Check on Employer-Provided Equipment
Before returning a company laptop or phone, back up any personal files stored on it, then confirm in writing that the device and any deposit or advance tied to it has been settled, since disputes over returned equipment can otherwise delay the full and final settlement discussed earlier.

## Keeping a Simple Record of Everything You've Done
A short running note listing every step completed, HR paperwork requested, insurance applied for, network contacted, gives a sense of concrete progress during a stretch that otherwise feels shapeless, and it's useful to glance back at on a harder day to see how much has already been handled.

## Why Talking to a Financial Advisor Once Can Be Worth the Fee
A single paid consultation with a fee-only financial advisor, someone who doesn't earn commission on products they recommend, can clarify exactly how long your specific savings will realistically stretch, factoring in obligations a generic budget spreadsheet might miss. This is a optional step, not a requirement, but worth considering for a severance package large enough to need real planning.

## Checking Whether COBRA-Style Portability Exists for Any Other Benefits
Beyond health insurance, some employer benefits like meal vouchers, wellness programs, or group discounts on services end without any formal notice on your last day. Asking HR for a complete list of everything tied to your employment, not just the obvious ones like salary and insurance, surfaces smaller benefits worth accounting for in your post-layoff budget.

## What to Do About Ongoing SIP or Investment Commitments
Pausing a running SIP rather than cancelling it outright preserves the account and the accumulated units while giving you breathing room during the search — most mutual fund platforms allow a pause without penalty, unlike a full redemption, which can trigger exit loads or tax implications you don't need to deal with during an already stressful stretch.

## Protect your headspace while you do all this
A layoff is a real loss, and treating it purely as a checklist to power through tends to catch up with people a few weeks in. Keeping some structure in your days, even with nowhere to be, helps both the job search and your state of mind, and talking to others who've been through it helps too — the shame that often comes with a layoff rarely matches what it says about someone, particularly now, when restructurings are common across the industry and say more about the business than the individual.
