An unlinked PAN becomes inoperative, TDS gets deducted at a higher rate on your salary or interest income, and some banks flag your KYC as incomplete until it's fixed. Linking takes about ten minutes if your details already match across both documents, longer if they don't. Here's the process, step by step.

## Check your current link status first
Go to incometax.gov.in, scroll to Quick Links on the homepage, and open Link Aadhaar Status. Enter your PAN and Aadhaar number. You'll get one of three results: already linked, not linked, or linked but pending confirmation. Skipping this step is why people end up submitting duplicate requests and then panic when the status doesn't change for a day or two — processing isn't instant.

## Pay the late fee if the portal asks for it
Since linking became mandatory with a penalty for late compliance, most people linking now are past the free window and get routed to pay ₹1,000 before the link option activates. This is paid through e-Pay Tax on the same portal — select the relevant assessment year and 'Other Receipts' as the payment type. The payment usually reflects in the system within a few hours, sometimes up to two working days, before you can proceed.

## Submit the link request
Log in with your PAN as the user ID, go to Profile, then Link Aadhaar. Your PAN and Aadhaar numbers are pre-filled from your profile — verify them, tick the consent checkbox, and click Link Aadhaar. An OTP goes to the mobile number registered with Aadhaar, not the one registered with your bank or PAN, so make sure you have access to that number.

## Why it gets rejected — and how to fix it
The single biggest reason a link request fails is a name or date-of-birth mismatch between the two databases — a middle name present on one and missing on the other, or a spelling difference. If this happens, you don't fix it on the income tax portal. You correct whichever document has the error: use the UIDAI self-service portal for Aadhaar corrections, or the PAN correction request on the NSDL or UTIITSL portal for PAN. Once the correction is processed, the same-day link request usually goes through.

## Checking Status Through a Different Route If the Portal Is Slow
During periods of heavy traffic near compliance deadlines, the income tax portal itself can be slow to load or process. Worth trying early morning or late evening if you're hitting timeouts, since the underlying processing queue tends to clear faster outside peak daytime hours when most people are attempting the same request.

## What This Means for TDS Already Deducted at the Higher Rate
If TDS was deducted at the higher rate during a period your PAN was inoperative, that's not necessarily lost money. Once the link is completed and your PAN becomes operative again, the excess TDS deducted can usually be claimed back as a refund when you file your income tax return for that year, provided the deduction and the correction both fall within the same or adjoining assessment years. It's worth flagging to whoever handles your return so the higher TDS entries in your Form 26AS get reconciled correctly rather than assumed to be the final tax liability.

## A Quick Word on Doing This for Family Members
If you're helping a parent or family member with less digital familiarity complete this, the OTP step is the part most likely to trip them up since it goes to the mobile number linked with Aadhaar, not whichever number they currently use daily. Confirming which number is linked, through the same status-check step, before attempting the link request saves a frustrating loop of failed OTPs.

## Renewing the Habit for Future Deadline-Driven Compliance
Aadhaar-PAN linking is one of several recurring compliance deadlines — KYC updates, mobile number re-verification with banks — that tend to arrive with penalty fees attached once the free window closes. Checking your PAN, Aadhaar, and bank KYC status together once a year, instead of reactively when a transaction suddenly fails, avoids both the penalty fee and the scramble that comes with discovering an issue at an inconvenient moment, like right before a large fund transfer or a loan application.

## Why This Became Mandatory in the First Place
The linking requirement exists primarily to close a specific gap: before Aadhaar-PAN linking, it was possible for one person to hold multiple PAN cards under slightly different name spellings or details, each one usable to open separate accounts or investments that wouldn't show up connected to each other in the tax department's systems. Linking the two closes that gap by tying every PAN to a single, biometrically verified identity, which is also why a mismatch between the two records blocks the link rather than being silently ignored.

## What "Inoperative" Actually Means for Everyday Transactions
An inoperative PAN doesn't just mean a higher TDS rate. Several routine transactions require an active, linked PAN to process at all: opening a new bank account, a new demat account, or a mutual fund folio, and in some cases even completing KYC refresh on an existing account. Discovering this mid-transaction, say, while trying to open a new fixed deposit at a bank, is a common and avoidable way people first learn their PAN went inoperative.

### The Difference Between "Not Linked" and "Inoperative"
These aren't quite the same status, and the terminology on the portal can be confusing. "Not linked" is simply the state before you've completed the process. "Inoperative" is the specific penalty status a PAN enters after crossing the mandated deadline without being linked, and it's the inoperative status that triggers the higher TDS rate and transaction blocks, not the mere fact of being unlinked before the deadline existed.

## Checking If You're Actually Exempt From the Requirement
A small category of PAN holders are formally exempt from the linking requirement, including residents of Assam, Jammu & Kashmir, and Meghalaya, non-resident Indians as per the Income Tax Act, and individuals above a certain age at the time the rule was introduced. If you believe you fall into one of these categories but the portal is still treating your PAN as inoperative, the fix isn't paying the fee, it's raising a grievance through the income tax portal's grievance section citing the exemption category, since the exemption sometimes needs to be manually recorded against your PAN.

## What to Do If the Payment Doesn't Reflect After Two Days
If the ₹1,000 fee payment through e-Pay Tax hasn't reflected in your link status after the stated processing window, check your bank statement first to confirm the payment went through and wasn't reversed. If it did go through and still isn't reflecting, the income tax portal's grievance redressal section lets you raise a specific payment-reconciliation issue referencing your transaction ID, which routes to a different queue than a general query and tends to get resolved faster for this specific, well-documented type of problem.

## A Note for Anyone Who Recently Changed Their Name
A recent legal name change, after marriage, for instance, is one of the more common reasons a link request fails even when both documents seem otherwise correct. Update whichever document, Aadhaar or PAN, still reflects the old name first, and do it through the correct channel, UIDAI for Aadhaar or NSDL/UTIITSL for PAN, rather than assuming the linking process itself will reconcile a name that's different across the two records.

## Keeping Proof of Linking Beyond Just the Screenshot
Beyond the screenshot mentioned earlier, the income tax portal also lets you download a formal acknowledgment once linking is confirmed, found under the same Link Aadhaar Status section used to check status originally. This is a more durable record than a screenshot, since it carries an official reference number, and it's worth keeping alongside your other tax documents for the year in case a bank or employer's own records lag behind the actual government database.

## Doing This for Elderly or Less Tech-Comfortable Family Members
Many older PAN holders, particularly retirees who no longer transact often, only discover their PAN is inoperative when a bank suddenly rejects a transaction they've done the same way for years. If you handle finances for parents or older relatives, checking their linking status alongside your own during this same session takes a few extra minutes and avoids a much more stressful scramble later, especially since older PAN cards are more likely to have the exact kind of name-spelling mismatch that complicates the process.

## What happens if you never link it
An inoperative PAN can still be used to file returns in some limited cases, but refunds get held up, TDS certificates won't reconcile properly, and you'll keep hitting friction at banks and mutual fund platforms that run PAN validation checks. There's no real upside to leaving it unlinked once you've noticed.
Once the link goes through, the status page confirms it with 'Aadhaar-PAN linking is completed successfully'. Save a screenshot of that confirmation — it's the quickest way to settle any dispute later with a bank or employer that still shows your PAN as unlinked in their own records.
