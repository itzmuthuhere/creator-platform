Google doesn't bury this behind support tickets or hidden menus — it's sitting inside your own account, just scattered across several settings pages that most people never think to open. A few minutes across four of them shows almost everything Google has stored about you, and lets you delete most of it.

## My Activity: the master log
Go to myactivity.google.com while signed in. This shows a chronological log of searches, YouTube videos watched, Google Maps navigation, and voice assistant interactions, timestamped and often including the device used. You can delete individual items, delete everything in a date range, or set up auto-delete so activity older than 3, 18, or 36 months is removed automatically going forward. If you've never touched this setting, it's likely set to keep data indefinitely.

## Location History, now called Timeline
Google changed this significantly: Timeline data (your movement history) is now stored on your device by default rather than Google's servers, a change rolled out through 2024. You can check and manage it from Google Maps under Settings > Personal content > Location History (Timeline), where you can also delete it, set auto-delete, or turn it off entirely. If you use Google Maps for navigation regularly, this is worth checking even if you think you already turned it off years ago, since the setting structure changed.

## Ad personalization and My Ad Center
Google builds an advertising profile from your activity across its own products and, if you've allowed it, other sites and apps that use Google ads. Visit myadcenter.google.com to see the categories Google has inferred about you (interests, sometimes age range and gender), and to switch off personalized ads entirely or remove specific categories. Turning this off doesn't reduce the number of ads you see, it just makes them less targeted to your inferred profile.

## Download everything with Google Takeout
takeout.google.com lets you export a full or partial copy of your data across every Google product you use, from Gmail to Photos to Drive to search history, as a downloadable archive. This is useful both to see the raw scope of what's stored, and as a backup before you delete anything permanently. Choose specific products if you don't need everything, since a full export across years of Gmail and Photos can run into several gigabytes.

## Deleting data vs deleting your account
These are different actions with very different consequences. Deleting specific activity, location history, or ad data leaves your account and services (Gmail, Drive files, YouTube subscriptions) fully intact. Deleting your Google Account itself is permanent and takes everything with it, including a Gmail address you may have used to sign into other services, so this should never be a quick decision, do it only after transferring anything important out via Takeout.

## Checking What Third-Party Apps Still Have Access to Your Google Account
Beyond Google's own data collection, apps and websites you've signed into using 'Sign in with Google' retain ongoing access to whatever permissions you granted at the time, often long after you've stopped using that app. Checking Security > Third-party apps with account access inside your Google Account settings, and revoking anything unfamiliar or no longer used, closes off access that's easy to forget was ever granted.

## What Happens to Deleted Data on Google's Backend
Deleting activity from My Activity removes it from what you and Google's product features can see and use, but Google's own retention policies mean some data may persist briefly in backup systems before being fully purged, generally within a defined window stated in Google's privacy documentation. Worth knowing that 'deleted' means removed from active use immediately, even if backend purging isn't always instantaneous.

## Why This Is Worth Doing Even If You 'Have Nothing to Hide'
The value of reviewing this isn't only about hiding something specific. A smaller stored history also means a smaller amount of data at risk if your Google account itself were ever compromised. Reducing what's stored is a genuine security practice, not just a privacy preference, since an attacker with account access can see everything sitting in these logs too.

## What to Check if You Share a Family Google Account or Devices
Activity logged under a shared or family account can mix multiple people's browsing and search history together in the same log. Worth confirming each family member has their own separate Google account rather than sharing one, both for accurate activity tracking and because a shared login means anyone with access can see and delete everyone else's history too.

## Checking What Data Google Has From Your Android Device Specifically
Beyond browser and search activity, an Android phone itself reports diagnostics, app usage, and device backup data to Google separately from the four settings pages already covered, visible under Settings, Google, then Backup, and worth a separate glance since it's easy to assume My Activity already covers everything a phone reports back, when device-level data sits in its own distinct section entirely separate from the account-level activity log.

## Why Deleting Old Search History Doesn't Break Your Personalized Experience
A common hesitation is worrying that deleting activity history will make search, recommendations, or the assistant noticeably worse. In practice, recent activity, the last few weeks, drives most of what shapes personalization day to day, so clearing out data from years ago has minimal effect on how the products behave for you right now, while shrinking what's sitting in long-term storage that serves little ongoing purpose beyond being a historical record you're unlikely to ever revisit.

## Understanding the Difference Between Google Account Data and Android's Own Permissions
What's covered here, My Activity, Timeline, My Ad Center, is about data tied to your Google Account. Separately, individual apps on your phone request their own permissions, camera, microphone, location, managed through your phone's own Settings, Apps, Permissions menu rather than through any Google Account page, and it's worth reviewing both since they control different things and a clean Google Account doesn't automatically mean your individual apps aren't still collecting their own separate data.

## What Changes If You're Using a Work or School Google Account
A Google Workspace account managed by an employer or school operates under different rules than a personal account, since the organization's administrator retains certain visibility and control over account data regardless of what an individual user's own settings show. Checking with the organization's IT policy before assuming full personal control over deletion and privacy settings avoids a mistaken assumption about what's private on a managed account.

## A Quick Note on YouTube Watch History Specifically
YouTube's watch and search history technically lives inside My Activity but is managed with its own pause toggle directly inside YouTube's settings too, worth checking separately since pausing general Google activity tracking doesn't always automatically pause YouTube-specific tracking depending on how the two were originally configured.

## Checking Whether Voice Assistant Recordings Are Stored Separately
Voice interactions with Google Assistant can include stored audio snippets alongside the text transcript already visible in My Activity, and whether audio itself is retained depends on a separate toggle inside the Assistant's own privacy settings, worth checking individually since deleting the transcript doesn't automatically delete an associated audio recording if one exists.

## Why Two-Factor Authentication Deserves a Check During the Same Session
While you're already in your Google Account's security settings reviewing activity and permissions, checking that two-factor authentication is enabled takes another minute and closes a far bigger security gap than anything covered above, since account activity data is only worth protecting if the account itself is hard to break into in the first place.

## Why Deleting a Google Account Doesn't Delete You From the Internet
Removing your Google Account stops Google from holding your activity data, but it has no effect on other companies, social platforms, other search engines, data brokers, that may have independently collected information about you. Treat a Google account cleanup as one piece of a broader privacy effort, not the entire picture all on its own.

## What Happens to Data You've Shared With Google Family Link
Parents managing a child's account through Family Link have their own separate activity and permissions dashboard to review, distinct from a standard adult account's settings, worth checking on the same periodic schedule if you're already managing a family member's account alongside your own personal one.

## A reasonable cleanup order
- Set My Activity auto-delete to 3 or 18 months.
- Check Timeline/Location History settings and delete anything you don't need retained.
- Review My Ad Center and turn off personalization if you don't want it.
- Run a Takeout export of anything you'd want a backup of before further deletion.
- Revisit this every few months, since new Google products sometimes add their own data categories.

The reason most people think Google is a black box isn't that any of this is hidden — it's that it's split across four different settings pages nobody stumbles onto by accident. Bookmark My Activity and My Ad Center, check them every few months, and there's no mystery left in it. None of it is hidden on purpose, it just takes a few minutes to find and clear.
