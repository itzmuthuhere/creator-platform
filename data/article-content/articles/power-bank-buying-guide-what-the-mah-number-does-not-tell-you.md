A 10000mAh power bank sounds like it should charge a 5000mAh phone twice over, with room left. In practice it usually falls short of even one and a half charges, and the gap between the number printed on the box and what reaches your phone is bigger than most buyers ever get told at the point of sale.

## Why the usable output is always less than the label
The cells inside a power bank run at roughly 3.7 volts, but your phone charges over USB at 5 volts, or higher for fast charging. Getting from 3.7V to 5V requires a boost converter, and that conversion is not free, it loses energy as heat. On top of that, the battery management circuit itself draws a small amount of power. Combine both losses and a power bank typically delivers somewhere around 60 to 75 percent of its rated mAh as actual usable charge. A 10000mAh unit realistically hands over closer to 6500 to 7500mAh through the USB port.

## Wattage matters more than capacity for how fast you charge
mAh tells you how much charge is stored, not how fast it can be delivered. If your phone supports 33W or 65W fast charging but the power bank only outputs 10W or 18W, you will charge slowly no matter how large the capacity number is. Check the wattage rating on the power bank against what your phone's charger is rated for, and match the cable too, a cheap cable can bottleneck a fast-charging-capable power bank.

## Cell quality is invisible from the outside
Two power banks can share the same mAh rating and behave completely differently over a year of use, because one uses higher-grade lithium cells and a proper protection circuit, and the other uses recycled or lower-grade cells. You cannot verify cell grade without opening the unit, but there are proxy signals worth checking: a BIS certification mark, which has been mandatory for power banks sold in India for several years now, since regulations tightened, a warranty of at least six months to a year from a brand with an actual India service presence, and a weight that is not suspiciously light for its stated capacity, since real cells and a proper circuit add real weight.

## Ports split the power budget, they do not multiply it
A power bank with two or three output ports does not give each port its full rated wattage simultaneously. The total output is shared, so charging two devices at once usually means both charge slower than if you used one port at a time. If you regularly need to charge two devices simultaneously at full speed, check the spec sheet for per-port and combined output ratings specifically, not just the headline number.

## Flying with one in India
DGCA and BCAS rules that Indian airlines follow allow power banks only in cabin baggage, never in checked baggage, and cap them at 100 watt-hours, which works out to roughly 27000mAh at nominal voltage without special airline permission. If your power bank does not list a Wh rating on the label, you can estimate it by multiplying mAh by 3.7 and dividing by 1000.

## Checking Real-World Reviews for Actual Output, Not Just the Spec Sheet
Manufacturer spec sheets list ideal-case output figures. Real-world user reviews and independent teardown or testing channels often report the actual measured output under realistic conditions, which can differ from the printed specification. Cross-checking a specific model against an independent test review before buying catches a mismatch between marketing claims and delivered performance that the spec sheet alone won't reveal.

## Why Pass-Through Charging Is a Feature Worth Checking For Specifically
Pass-through charging lets a power bank charge your phone while it's simultaneously being charged itself from the wall — useful for overnight charging setups or when traveling with limited outlets. Not every power bank supports this cleanly, and some that claim to support it do so with reduced efficiency or added heat. Worth checking in reviews rather than assuming every power bank handles this well.

## Storage and Long-Term Care That Actually Extends Its Life
A power bank left fully depleted for extended periods, similar to a phone battery, can degrade faster than one stored with a partial charge. Keeping a spare power bank around 40 to 60 percent charged during long periods of non-use, instead of storing it empty or always topped up to 100 percent, extends its usable lifespan the same way it does for a phone battery.

## A simple way to size your purchase
A quick way to shop smarter: take your phone's battery capacity in mAh, decide how many extra charges you want on a bad day, then multiply by roughly 1.4 to cover conversion loss, that is the label number worth targeting. Anything well beyond it is just extra weight and cost for capacity you will rarely draw on.
